## What symptom → cause mappings are consistently taught (≥2 sources)?

### Takeaway
The mappings that show up in two or more independent sources:
- **Fluid condition.** Milky pink fluid means coolant contamination. Dark fluid with a burnt smell means overheating or burnt clutches. Fine gray paste on the magnet is normal wear, but large flakes or chunks are not.
- **Overfill.** Too much fluid foams, then slips and pushes fluid out the vent.
- **Converter-area leak.** Red fluid points to the pump or converter-hub seal. Dark oil points to the rear main seal.
- **Whine.** A whine that follows engine rpm and is present in Park and Neutral points to the pump. Noise that follows road speed points to the geartrain or final drive.
- **Vibration.** Vibration in P/N at a fixed rpm points to the flexplate or converter.
- **TCC stuck on.** The engine stalls when the vehicle comes to a stop.
- **TCC never applies.** Cruise rpm stays high, the fluid runs hot and mpg drops.
- **TCC shudder.** A light-throttle shudder, roughly 35–55 mph, that goes away when the TCC releases.
- **Stop/start pump or accumulator failure.** Delayed engagement or a hard engagement right after an auto-restart.
- **Apply chart.** Isolate the element that applies only in the failed range or ranges.

### Cited Findings

**Fluid condition (A.4, A.10): Universal**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Healthy ATF is translucent red or pink with no particles. Dark brown or black fluid means oxidized or overheated fluid. A burnt smell means overheating or clutch damage. A milky look means coolant contamination. Sources: [Mister Transmission, Transmission Fluid Diagnosis](https://www.mistertransmission.com/transmission-fluid-diagnosis/); [Monster Transmission, fluid color](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/what-color-is-transmission-fluid); [Mortons on the Move](https://www.mortonsonthemove.com/transmission-fluid/). Scope: Universal. Color norms vary by fluid type, and some newer ATFs are not red (needs SI).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Pink, milky ("strawberry milkshake") ATF means coolant or water has entered the fluid. The usual path is a leak in the transmission cooler inside the radiator tank. The mixed fluid damages seals and friction material. The repair is to replace the radiator and flush both the cooling system and the transmission. Sources: [freeasestudyguides, pink milky ATF](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/atf-pink-milky.html); [freeasestudyguides, transmission fluid cooler](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-fluid-cooler.html); [GMB, oil in the coolant](https://gmb.net/blog/oil-in-the-coolant/). Scope: Universal wherever the cooler sits in the radiator.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The same cooler breach can also show as pink, foamy **coolant** (ATF in the coolant). Source: [GMB](https://gmb.net/blog/oil-in-the-coolant/). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Dark or black fluid with a strong burnt smell means badly overheated, oxidized fluid. Possible causes include bad clutches, overheating or blocked fluid passages. One source warns that "a flush alone may not be the right call." Sources: [Rohnert Park Transmission](https://rohnertparktransmission.com/blog/transmission-fluid-burnt-smell); [AutoBarn](https://www.autobarn.net/symptoms/burning-smell-transmission); [Monster Transmission](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/what-color-is-transmission-fluid). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Varnish forms when heat oxidizes ATF. The oxidized molecules are sticky and build up as sludge and varnish, which can make valves stick or leak and cause slipping or harsh shifts. Source: [Transmission Digest, transmission fluid hydraulics](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/transmission-fluid-hydraulics/). A fluid-additive vendor says the same thing, but it has a product to sell ([Sea Foam](https://seafoamworks.com/help-center/how-to-use-trans-tune-in-automatic-transmission-fluid/)). Universal.

**Pan and magnet debris (A.10): Universal (normal vs. abnormal)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): A thin coat of fine gray sludge or paste on the pan magnet is normal wear, especially at high mileage. It is fine particles from the clutches and friction material. Some friction material in the pan bottom is also normal. Large silver flakes, metal slivers or brass chunks are not normal and point to active failure. Burnt, dark fluid often comes with excessive metal. Sources: [Rick's Free Auto Repair Advice](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/is-metal-on-the-transmission-pan-magnet-a-sign-of-trouble/); [BITOG, gray material in trans pan](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/gray-material-in-trans-pan.346618/); [BITOG, metal shavings](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/metal-shavings-in-atf-normal.44571/); [ih8mud, clutch material in pan](https://forum.ih8mud.com/threads/clutch-material-in-the-transmission-pan.400667/). Source quality: forum and blog level, but the sources agree. Universal.
- OPEN: No textbook or OEM statement surfaced on reading debris type: steel/ferrous (gears, bearings), aluminum (case, pump, valve body), brass/bronze (bushings, thrust washers) or friction material (clutches, bands). Brass chunks were mentioned (above). The full material-to-source key needs Halderman or Today's Technician.

**Fluid loss and leaks (A.10): Universal**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): At the converter housing, **red** fluid points to the front pump seal or converter-hub seal. **Black** engine oil points to the rear main seal. A rear valve-cover or cam-plug leak can also run down into the bellhousing. Confirm by pulling the inspection cover and watching for drips at idle. Replacing either seal means separating the engine and transmission. Sources: [Toyota Nation](https://www.toyotanation.com/threads/tran-fluid-leak-is-this-torque-converter-seal-problem.394727/); [Jeep Forum, 42RE front pump seal](https://www.jeepforum.com/threads/transmission-leak-42re-front-pump-seal.4326829/); [Wrangler TJ Forum](https://wranglertjforum.com/threads/torque-converter-housing-area-leaks.13311/). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): A converter can also leak through its welds (pinholes). One snippet says a smoke machine can find them. The same source group lists the pump bushing, pump body and pump-to-case O-ring as other bellhousing leak paths. Source: [Gearstar](https://gearstar.com/blog/bad-torque-converter-symptoms-diagnosis-and-fixes/). A smoke test is a shop technique, not a documented OEM procedure. Scope: Universal, method needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): For UV dye, clean the area first. Add dye to the ATF, drive so it circulates, then find the fluorescent trace with a UV lamp. Sources: [Wrangler Forum](https://www.wranglerforum.com/threads/automatic-transmission-fluid-leak-advice-needed-on-what-to-look-for.2472627/); [MotorBiscuit](https://www.motorbiscuit.com/uv-dye-oil-leak-detection-finding-leaks-easily/); [Honda/Snap-on ATF dye kit](https://honda.snapon.com/HondaAcura/Catalog/Product?id=UVUATFDYEKT). Universal. Use a dye rated for ATF.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Overfill** lets the rotating parts churn the fluid and whip air into it. Aerated fluid does not carry pressure or heat well, so clutches apply weakly, line pressure becomes erratic and the fluid runs hot. The foam expands and pushes fluid out the **vent**. Sources: [Gearbox Insider](https://gearboxinsider.com/transmission-fluid-overfilled-symptoms-and-fix/); [Overbeck Auto](https://overbeckauto.com/what-happens-when-you-overfill-transmission-fluid/); [Grassroots Motorsports](https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/grm/transmission-blowing-fluid-out-the-ventwhy/67390/page1/); [BITOG](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/damage-from-overfilling-auto-transmission.197258/). Universal.
- INFERRED: Low fluid also lets the pump pull air, so low and high levels can produce the same symptoms (slip, noise, heat). Fluid out the vent, or foam on the dipstick, points to **overfill or aeration** before an internal seal failure. The GM statement that fluid checked cold reads overfilled once hot (see the next question) supports this.

**Road-test symptom definitions (A.8, A.9): Universal**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Flare** is engine overspeed between gears during a shift. Normally rpm drops immediately into the next gear. Causes include worn friction material, low line pressure, or wrong off-going/on-coming timing in a clutch-to-clutch shift. Sources: [BITOG, what is shift flare](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/what-is-shift-flare.180375/); [BITOG, slipping vs flare](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/automatic-transmission-question-slipping-vs-flare.338792/); [Diesel Stop](https://www.thedieselstop.com/threads/dumb-question-what-is-shift-flare.114001/). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Flare is short, at the shift. **Slip** lasts longer and can happen before or after a shift, including while cruising in gear. Source: [BITOG, slipping vs flare](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/automatic-transmission-question-slipping-vs-flare.338792/); a quizlet deck repeats it (lead only). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): A **tie-up** (bind-up) is when the transmission is in two gear states at once. Either the on-coming element applies too fast compared with the off-going element's release, or the off-going element fails to release. Brief overlap during a shift is normal. Lasting tie-up absorbs shift energy and damages hardware. Sources: GM/Ford-family patents, [US 7559873, resolving tie-up in clutch-to-clutch transmission](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7559873) and [US 9222531, tie-up monitoring logic](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9222531). Universal.
- INFERRED: Road-test feel: tie-up feels like the vehicle is braked or bogs during or after a shift, with rpm pulled **down**. Flare and slip pull rpm **up**. This follows from the definitions, but no source phrased it this way.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Delayed engagement** (a lag moving from P/N into D or R) is attributed to low fluid, a clogged filter, low pump pressure, worn or hardened internal seals, valve body wear, solenoid faults or worn clutches. The delay can be worse cold. Sources: [Sun Devil Auto](https://www.sundevilauto.com/blog/what-causes-delayed-engagement-in-transmission); [Noah's Garage](https://www.noahsgarage.com/delayed-engagement-transmission-causes-fixes/); [Crown Auto](https://crownautoservice417.com/automotive-knowledge-center/repair-library/transmission/delayed-transmission-engagement/). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Delayed engagement only after sitting** (overnight, first start) means the converter drained back to the sump. One snippet describes a check: note the dipstick level engine-off at night and again in the morning. A much higher morning level confirms drainback. The snippets name the converter drain-back check valve (Ford diesel / Stellantis examples) and name GM 8L45/8L90 as having a "delayed engagement after sitting" condition. Sources: [4L60E delayed engagement, Core Transmission Parts](https://coretransmissionparts.com/blogs/news/4l60e-delayed-engagement-causes-diagnosis-and-the-real-fix); [Ram Forumz, converter drain-back](https://www.ramforumz.com/threads/converter-drain-back-delayed-shifting.238018/); [Transmission Digest Aug 2019](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/august-2019-issue/); [Sonnax, Chrysler RWD cold](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/188-the-cure-for-the-common-chrysler-rwd-cold). Scope: the principle is Universal. The 8L45/8L90 mention is GM-specific and **needs SI/TSB** (bulletin number not recovered).

**Clutch/band apply chart isolation (A.8, A.14): Universal method**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The apply chart pinpoints the faulty element by elimination. Find what is applied in the failed gear or range and rule out anything that also applies in a range that works. The textbook example: slip in first gear in **D and 2** but **not** in manual **1** means the **overrunning (one-way) clutch** has failed. Manual 1 applies a band or clutch that holds the same member, so it takes over for the one-way clutch. Source: [freeasestudyguides, clutch and band charts](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-clutch-band-chart.html) (fetch blocked; snippet only). The method is Universal. **Which elements are involved depends on the unit's apply chart (needs SI).** The same method in a Chrysler unit chart: [Mopar1973Man 46RE/47RE/48RE diagnostic chart](https://mopar1973man.com/cummins/articles.html/general-cummins/85_transmission-transfer-case_85/automatic-transmission-diagnostic-chart-46re-47re-48re-r352/) (Unit-specific).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Slips in all forward gears, reverse OK**: suspect the forward (input) clutch, including its piston seals, a forward-circuit hydraulic leak, or the low roller clutch. Confirm with a pressure test (see `hydraulic_pressure_testing.md`). Sources: [Diesel Place](https://www.dieselplace.com/threads/reverse-works-forward-gears-slip-badly.338023/); [Expedition Forum 4R70W](https://www.expeditionforum.com/threads/2003-expedition-4r70w-line-pressures-no-forward-only-reverse.34148/). Scope: the method is Universal. Clutch names are Unit-specific (GM Hydra-matic style "forward clutch").
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **No reverse, forwards OK**: the fault is limited to the reverse path. Candidates are the reverse clutch or low/reverse element, a reverse solenoid, the valve body, and the shift linkage or range sensor. Low fluid can also cause it. Check fluid, then linkage, before internal parts. Sources: [Mister Transmission, 5 reasons car won't reverse](https://www.mistertransmission.com/5-reasons-your-car-wont-reverse/); [Firestone](https://www.firestonecompleteautocare.com/blog/maintenance/car-wont-go-in-reverse/); [Monster Transmission 4L60E no reverse](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/4l60e-no-reverse-causes-symptoms-and-solutions). Universal method.
- INFERRED (exam reasoning): Use the apply chart this way (from the method above):
  - **Failed in several ranges:** look for the one element **common to all** the failed ranges.
  - **Failed in one range only:** look for an element **unique to** that range.
  - **Rule out** anything that also applies in a range that works. For example, if 2nd works and the element slipping in 3rd is also applied in 2nd, that element is exonerated.
  - **No forward and no reverse**: think of something common to everything. That means fluid level, pump or pressure, the manual valve or linkage, the converter/input, or output parts such as a broken shaft or park parts. It does not mean a single clutch.

**NVH (A.9): Universal**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The first question is whether the noise follows **engine rpm** or **road speed**. A whine in Park and Neutral that rises with engine rpm in every range is almost always the **pump**, which turns with the converter hub whenever the engine runs. A clogged filter or an air leak on the pickup side also makes a pump whine that rises with rpm. Noise that rises with **vehicle speed** points to the geartrain or planetaries, the output, or the final drive. Sources: [Transmission Digest, "Noise? What noise?"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/noise-what-noise-oh-that-one/); [Gears, "The Sound of Silence"](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/the-sound-of-silence-diagnosing-transmission-noises/); [Rohnert Park Transmission](https://rohnertparktransmission.com/blog/transmission-whining-noise). Universal.
- INFERRED: A noise in **one gear only** points to that gear's planetary set or apply element, which you find with the same apply-chart logic. This extends the method above. No snippet stated it directly.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Vibration in **P/N at a fixed rpm band** points to a flexplate or converter that is out of balance or cracked. A cracked flexplate can also sound like a heat-shield or exhaust rattle. Check converter-to-flexplate bolts and inspect through the bellhousing inspection cover while turning the engine. A metallic clunk on engagement or a rattle at idle in gear can be flexplate or converter parts. Sources: [TCCoA, rpm-based vibration](https://www.tccoa.com/threads/rpm-based-vibration-engine-or-torque-converter-flexplate.167729/); [Car Treatments, bad flexplate](https://cartreatments.com/bad-flexplate-symptoms/); [Werkwell](https://www.werkwell.com/news/automatic-transmission-flexplate-your-guide-to-diagnosis/); [Gearstar](https://gearstar.com/blog/bad-torque-converter-symptoms-diagnosis-and-fixes/). Universal.

**TCC mechanical/hydraulic (A.13): Universal unless noted** (see also `stator_owc_diagnosis.md`)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **TCC stuck on**: the engine stalls when the vehicle comes to a stop, because the converter locks the engine to the wheels. It may restart and stall again when put in gear. Usual causes are a stuck TCC solenoid or apply valve, often jammed by dirty fluid. One OEM-adjacent note (RepairPal, citing a TSB-type pattern) says it can happen after extended freeway driving and clears once the unit cools. Sources: [RepairPal](https://repairpal.com/torque-converter-clutch-can-stick-after-extended-freeway-driving-265); [OBD-Codes P0742](https://www.obd-codes.com/p0742); [iCarsoft P0742](https://www.icarsoft-us.com/blogs/news/p0742-code-torque-converter-clutch-stuck-on-fix); [LiveAbout, GM lock-up diagnosis](https://www.liveabout.com/diagnosing-gm-converter-lock-up-problems-262653). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **TCC never applies / slips**: engine rpm does not drop at cruise, fuel economy falls, and the transmission overheats (the converter slipping turns power into heat). There can be shudder. Sources: [AutoZone, bad torque converter](https://www.autozone.com/diy/transmission/symptoms-of-a-bad-torque-converter); [Monster Transmission](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/torque-converter-failure-symptoms-warning-signs-costs-and-real-fixes); [Edmunds P0741](https://www.edmunds.com/obd-dtc/p0741.html). Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): To verify apply on a road test, cruise at light steady throttle where the TCC should apply. Watch **TCC commanded state** and **TCC slip rpm** (engine rpm minus turbine speed). If the TCC is commanded on but slip stays high, the apply side (hydraulic or friction) is failing, not the command. Sources: [Sun Transmissions P0741](https://suntransmissions.com/blogs/news/p0741-code-how-to-diagnose-the-torque-converter-clutch-and-find-the-right-fix); [Apex Tech Nation P0741](https://apextechnation.com/articles/p0741-torque-converter-clutch-code); [Gears, NAG1 P0741 TCC slip](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/nag-1-722-6-p0741-tcc-slip/). Numbers in snippets ("200 rpm" set threshold; 0–50 / 50–200 / 200+ rpm bands; 55–65 mph test speed) come from aftermarket blogs, not OEM. **Do not use them. Needs SI.**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **TCC shudder** appears at light throttle, often roughly 35–55 mph. It feels like driving over rumble strips when the converter locks, and the rpm "dances". It goes away with more throttle or when the TCC releases. Scan-tool approach: record TCC commanded vs. actual slip, TCC solenoid current, VSS, TPS and load at the shudder speed. Sources: [Moveras, shudder diagnosis](https://moveras.com/Support/Articles/Shudder_Diagnosis); [Gears, shudder diagnosis](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/shudder-diagnosis/); [Monster Transmission, 6L80E shudder](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/6l80e-torque-converter-shudder-causes-fixes-monster). The speed band is a snippet generality (needs SI). The 6L80E source is GM-unit-specific.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Overheating** causes that are consistently listed are a restricted cooler or cooler lines (debris or clutch material), low fluid (less fluid to carry heat), towing or heavy load (the converter works harder), and a TCC that does not lock or slips at cruise. A cooler bypass valve can keep lube flowing when the cooler is blocked, so the unit runs hot instead of failing quickly. Sources: [VehicleServicePros, transmission overheat](https://www.vehicleservicepros.com/service-repair/underhood/article/21186321/transmission-overheat); [Gearbox Insider](https://gearboxinsider.com/transmission-overheating-causes-and-fixes/); [Core Transmission Parts, 4L60E overheating](https://coretransmissionparts.com/blogs/news/4l60e-overheating-causes-and-real-fixes); [BITOG, 4R70W bypass valve](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/4r70w-transmission-overheating-failed-cooler-bypass-valve.302831/). Universal.

**Stop/start auxiliary pump / accumulator (A.15)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Automatics with engine stop/start add either an **electric auxiliary pump** that holds apply pressure during auto-stop, or a **solenoid-latched high-pressure accumulator** that re-strokes the launch clutch on restart. Either way, first gear is ready the moment the engine refires. Sources: patents [US 8116932, auxiliary pump diagnostic systems](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8116932) and [US 8380405, trouble-deciding device for electric oil pump](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8380405); [Wikipedia, GM 9T50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GM_9T50_transmission) (spring-loaded accumulator engages the first-gear clutches on restart); [Sonnax, stop-start accumulators](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1157-stop-start-accumulators-what-you-don-t-know-can-hurt-you) and [Transmission Digest reprint](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/stop-start-accumulators-what-you-dont-know-can-hurt-you/). Scope: the pump design is Universal/multi-OEM. The accumulator design is documented for **GM 9T50 (and 8L family), GM-specific**.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Failure symptoms are a **delayed engagement, sluggish launch or hard "kick"/clunk right after an auto-restart**. Without stored pressure, the mechanical pump has to fill the clutch while the engine is already turning the input. Sources: patent language on engagement delay after restart ([US 8116932](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8116932)); owner reports [MBWorld, auxiliary pump failure](https://mbworld.org/forums/c-class-w205/811731-auxiliary-transmission-pump-failure.html); [SubaruOutback.org P2797](https://www.subaruoutback.org/threads/p2797-auxilary-transmission-oil-pump-stuck-off-issue.557960/) (CVT "hard kick"). Universal principle.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Related DTCs: **P2797** means auxiliary transmission fluid pump performance/stuck off ([SubaruOutback.org](https://www.subaruoutback.org/threads/p2797-auxilary-transmission-oil-pump-stuck-off-issue.557960/)). **P0C2D** is auxiliary pump control module feedback ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1169966-error-code-p0c2d-auxiliary-transmission-fluid-pump-control-module-feedback-signal-low-2.html)). **P171D**, "accumulator system performance", is a GM engagement-bump case ([Gears, "Light Turns Green, Car Turns Mean – P171D"](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/light-turns-green-car-turns-mean-p171d-accumulator-sys-performance/)). P171D is GM-specific. **Needs SI** for exact DTC definitions.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The Sonnax / Transmission Digest article title is "what you don't know can hurt you". Its snippet says the accumulator stores high pressure, which is a **service safety hazard** during disassembly. On restart the clutch-control solenoid is already at full apply, so there is no cushion, which can cause an engagement clunk. Sources: [Sonnax](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1157-stop-start-accumulators-what-you-don-t-know-can-hurt-you); [Transmission Digest](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/stop-start-accumulators-what-you-dont-know-can-hurt-you/). GM-specific (9T/8L).
- INFERRED: A clunk or delay that happens **only after auto-restart**, with normal engagement after a key start, isolates the fault to the stop/start hold system (aux pump or accumulator and its solenoid). It is not the main clutch or the converter. This is the likely exam logic, but it rests on inference.

### Inferences
- The most consistent two-source rule across the whole set is to **classify the symptom by what it follows**:
  - engine rpm (pump, converter, flexplate)
  - vehicle speed (output, final drive, tires, driveline)
  - one gear or range (the apply element unique to it)
  - all ranges (fluid, pump, pressure, input, manual valve)
  - only after restart or after sitting (stop/start hold, converter drainback)
- Each symptom definition implies an rpm behavior: flare and slip raise rpm, tie-up drags rpm down, and a stuck-on TCC kills the engine at a stop.

### Gaps
- No Halderman or Today's Technician full text for exact symptom definitions (flare/slip/tie-up/bind-up), or for the debris-type-to-component key. Tried via search; all hosting pages were blocked or were quizlet leads only.
- No GM TechLink article on stop/start aux pump or accumulator diagnosis surfaced. The GM 8L45/8L90 drainback TSB number was not recovered.
- No public OEM TCC slip specs. Every number seen was aftermarket-blog level.

## What diagnostic order / first-check rules do ASE-oriented sources emphasize?

### Takeaway
The order these sources give:
1. Verify the concern.
2. Check fluid level and condition, correctly: level vehicle, the specified temperature, the right procedure for dipstick or check-plug units.
3. Research TSBs, software updates and service history.
4. Visual inspection for leaks, linkage, mounts and cooler lines.
5. Pull **engine and transmission** DTCs.
6. Road test in every range, with scan data where possible.
7. Only then run pressure, stall or teardown tests (other notes).

For no-dipstick units, the level check is done hot within an SI temperature window read on the scan tool, engine running, vehicle level, at a check plug. A cold check overfills.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): "The first check should always be on the transmission fluid level and its condition." Incorrect fluid level is "the most common cause of trouble with an automatic gearbox". Source: [How a Car Works, diagnosing faults in automatic transmission](https://www.howacarworks.com/transmission/diagnosing-faults-in-automatic-transmission). The first steps in a no-reverse case are also fluid, then linkage, then internal ([Mister Transmission](https://www.mistertransmission.com/5-reasons-your-car-wont-reverse/)). The "check fluid and filter, then pressure test" order: [Diesel Place](https://www.dieselplace.com/threads/reverse-works-forward-gears-slip-badly.338023/). Tasks A.2 and A.4. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Follow the manufacturer's level-check procedure (engine running or off, gear position), because methods differ. Sources: [Quaker State](https://www.quakerstate.com/en_us/car-care/checking-your-transmission-fluid.html); [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/guides-and-gear/how-check-transmission-fluid-level). A.4. Universal (needs SI).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **No-dipstick units** are checked at a level (inspection) plug from below. The vehicle must be level, the engine idling, and the fluid within a specified temperature read on a scan tool. A small trickle from the plug means correct level. If nothing comes out, add fluid until it trickles. Sources: [O'Reilly](https://www.oreillyauto.com/how-to-hub/how-to-check-transmission-fluid-without-a-dipstick); [AutoZone](https://www.autozone.com/diy/transmission/how-to-check-transmission-fluid-without-a-dipstick); [Rick's Free Auto Repair Advice](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/how-to-check-transmission-fluid-without-a-dipstick/). A.4. The principle is Universal. Temperature windows differ by unit: snippets quote 95–113°F for some units and 115–133°F for others, which is unattributed (**needs SI**).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), **GM-specific, AF50 8-speed**:
  1. Engine running, A/C off.
  2. Wait for transmission fluid temperature to reach **35–45°C (95–113°F)**.
  3. Move the shift lever P→D through every position, pausing at least 2 s in each, and repeat.
  4. Return to Park.
  5. Raise the vehicle **level** with the engine running.
  6. Remove the level plug and check for a dribble.

  Why the window matters: ATF expands a lot as it heats, so a level set cold will be overfilled at operating temperature. Source: [Gears, GM's AF50 8-speed fluid level and condition check](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/gms-af50-8-speed-transmission-fluid-level-and-condition-check/). A.4. The 35–45°C figure is attributed to this one unit only, so **needs SI for any other GM unit**. The same 95–113°F range also appears unattributed in the O'Reilly/AutoZone snippet group, so the attribution is uncertain.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): GM factory scan-tool access (MDI2 + GDS2) is required for many GM checks, and TFT is read in transmission live data. Sources: [gmparts.com diagnostic support](https://www.gmparts.com/technical-resources/diagnostic-support-resources); [Leamastech](https://www.leamastech.com/blog/how-check-transmission-fluid-temperature). GM-specific.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **TSBs and software come early.** TSBs often begin by telling you to confirm the TCM has the latest calibration. GM guidance is to compare the ECM/TCM calibrations with TIS2Web and apply a relevant update **before** doing learns. An update clears the clutch adapts, so the transmission may shift harshly or flare until Service Fast Learn and adaptive learning are done. Sources: [NHTSA-hosted GM bulletin, "Information on Transmission Adaptive Functions"](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10112288-9999.pdf) (fetch blocked; snippet); [NHTSA-hosted TCM software update bulletin 2023](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10234022-0001.pdf); [Brake & Front End, reprogramming TCMs](https://www.brakeandfrontend.com/reprogramming-todays-transmission-control-modules/). A.3 and A.14. Mostly GM-specific (TIS2Web). The principle of checking TSBs and calibration first is Universal. Details are in `tcm_programming_relearn.md`.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Pull **all engine codes along with transmission codes** before transmission-specific testing. Source: [Apex Tech Nation, harsh shift diagnosis](https://apextechnation.com/articles/harsh-shift-diagnosis); [Advanced Transmission, misdiagnosed transmission problems](https://advancedtransmission.com/you-may-not-need-a-rebuilt-transmission-other-problems-that-can-cause-a-misdiagnosis/). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): A road test verifies slipping, and it must include **every gear range**. A quiz-deck item frames "avoid testing in different ranges" as the wrong answer. Source: [quizlet, Chapter 13 troubleshooting mechanical/hydraulic/electrical](https://quizlet.com/879756488/chapter-13-trouble-shooting-mechanical-hydraulic-and-electrical-problems-flash-cards/). This is a lead only; the source is a study site. A.8.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): For shudder or TCC concerns, record a scan-tool "movie" at the speed the concern happens, capturing commanded vs. actual TCC slip plus VSS, TPS and load, so a changing input cannot fool you. Source: [Moveras](https://moveras.com/Support/Articles/Shudder_Diagnosis); [Gears, shudder diagnosis](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/shudder-diagnosis/). A.8 and A.13. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): For a flexplate/converter NVH check, pull the bellhousing inspection cover, check the converter bolts and look for cracks while turning the engine. Source: [Car Treatments](https://cartreatments.com/bad-flexplate-symptoms/); [TCCoA](https://www.tccoa.com/threads/rpm-based-vibration-engine-or-torque-converter-flexplate.167729/). A.2 and A.9. Universal.

### Inferences
- **The level check comes before the road test** for a reason. If the level is wrong, every road-test symptom is unreliable: a low level gives slip, delay or whine, and an overfill gives foam, slip and venting. The fix may be as simple as correcting the level.
- On a no-dipstick unit, the likely exam trap is checking **cold** or with the **vehicle not level**. Either gives a false reading. Cold leads to overfill once hot, and a tilted vehicle gives a false trickle or no trickle.
- Checking TSBs and software early saves diagnosis. A shift-quality complaint may be a known calibration fix, and flashing clears the adapts, which changes how the unit shifts.

## What are the common reasoning traps (e.g., assuming the transmission when it's an engine or input-sensor issue)?

### Takeaway
The traps sources warn about most:
- **Engine misfire mistaken for TCC shudder**, and the reverse.
- **Engine-side inputs** (TPS, MAF/MAP, misfire) that cause harsh, late or early shifts.
- **Wheel-speed, ABS or traction-control faults** that block upshifts.
- **Motor mounts** that make harsh-engagement clunks.
- **CV/U-joint** clunks.
- **Transfer-case wind-up** from mismatched tires, which feels like a bind.
- **Overfill** mistaken for an internal leak.
- **Drainback** mistaken for a worn pump.

Rule: pull engine codes and verify inputs **before** condemning the transmission (A.22).

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Misfire vs. TCC shudder.** A load-dependent misfire can feel exactly like converter shudder. It may not set a code or light, because a misfire has to repeat enough times within a drive cycle before a DTC sets. To tell them apart, check misfire counters, fuel-system data, cylinder contribution and DTCs. Swap the coil or plug to see if the misfire follows it. Sources: [Transmission Digest, "TCC Vibration, Judder or Shudder Misdiagnosed"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/tcc-vibration-judder-or-shudder-misdiagnosed/); [Auto Service World, "Engine Misfire or TCC Chuggle?"](https://www.autoserviceworld.com/carsmagazine/engine-misfire-or-tcc-chuggle/); [Gears, shudder diagnosis](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/shudder-diagnosis/). A.9, A.13, A.22. Universal. (The search summary merged these, so attribution per sentence is uncertain.)
- INFERRED: The distinguishing test is to command or allow the TCC **off** at the complaint speed. Shudder that disappears with the TCC released points to the converter. Vibration that stays points to the engine, driveline or tires. This agrees with the snippets ("goes away when TCC unlocks"), but no single source stated it as a test step.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **TPS input.** Shift timing depends on throttle position, engine speed and road speed. If the module reads the throttle as closed, it shifts early and soft. If it reads the throttle as open, it shifts late and harsh. Sources: [freeasestudyguides, TPS transmission](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/throttle-position-sensor-transmission.html); [Car Treatments, TPS symptoms](https://cartreatments.com/throttle-position-sensor-symptoms/); [General Transmission Reno](https://generaltransmissionreno.com/what-controls-your-transmission/). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The **opposite trap**: harsh or delayed shifts **with no TPS codes** can be the TCM or valve body. Check line pressure, adapts and solenoid operation before blaming the TPS. Source: TPS snippet group above (the individual URL is unclear; likely [Fabheavyparts](https://www.fabheavyparts.com/blogs/buyers-guide/throttle-position-sensor-tps-what-it-does-symptoms-and-how-to-test-it) or [Blikai](https://www.blikai.com/blog/sensors/throttle-position-sensor-replacement-symptoms-fix-guide)). Low-authority source, so treat it as a lead.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Engine-side inputs such as misfire, MAF and TPS "are one of the most commonly missed root causes on harsh shift diagnosis". Techs go straight to the transmission. Sources: [Apex Tech Nation](https://apextechnation.com/articles/harsh-shift-diagnosis); [J&S Auto, issues mistaken for transmission problems](https://jsautori.com/diagnosing-issues-mistaken-for-transmission-problems/). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Vacuum** matters only on older vacuum-modulator units, where a leak changes shift feel. Electronically controlled units have no vacuum line to the transmission, so a vacuum leak acts only through engine load or MAP inputs. Sources: [GMT400 forum](https://www.gmt400.com/threads/will-a-vacuum-leak-affect-my-transmission.71180/); [Quora](https://www.quora.com/Can-a-vacuum-leak-cause-shifting-problems) (lead). A.22. Universal/historical.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Wheel speed, ABS or traction control.** A faulty ABS wheel-speed sensor can stop upshifts, and traction-control intervention can command engine and transmission changes. A faulty VSS or output speed sensor causes erratic shifts, no shift, harsh shifts or limp mode. Wheel-speed faults can come before transmission speed-sensor DTCs. Sources: [Chevy HHR forum](https://www.chevyhhr.net/forums/problems-service-repairs-42/wheel-speed-sensors-not-letting-transmission-shift-52487/); [ClubTouareg](https://www.clubtouareg.com/threads/abs-wheel-speed-sensor-and-transmission.29099/); patent on coordinated engine/transmission control during traction control ([US 6009967](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6009967)). A.22. Universal principle. Which module uses which speed source is platform-specific (needs SI).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Motor mounts.** A broken mount causes a harsh thud or clunk when shifting into D or R, because the engine lurches, and it can be mistaken for a transmission or driveline fault. Sources: [aa1car, motor mounts](https://www.aa1car.com/library/motor_mounts.htm); [Rick's Free Auto Repair Advice](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/bad-motor-mount-symptoms-you-should-never-ignore/); [Car Treatments](https://cartreatments.com/bad-engine-mount-symptoms-and-replacement-cost/). A.9, A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **CV/U-joints.** A clunk on acceleration, deceleration or engagement can come from play in an inner CV joint (FWD) or from driveshaft CV or U-joints (RWD/AWD). A worn outer CV usually also clicks in tight turns under power. Worn mounts can change axle angles and imitate CV vibration. Sources: [AutoZone, CV axles](https://www.autozone.com/diy/cv-axle/how-to-tell-if-your-cv-axles-are-going-bad); [NAPA CV axles](https://napacvaxles.com/top-5-noise-symptoms-associated-with-a-worn-cv-shaft/); [Friendly Auto Centers](https://www.friendlyautocenters.com/blog/how-do-worn-engine-mounts-cause-unusual-car-behavior-and-annoying-vibrations). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Transfer case, mismatched tires.** Tires of different rolling diameter make the 4WD driveline wind up and bind, especially in turns, and can destroy active transfer cases and transmissions. A snippet quotes a tolerance of "within 1/8- to 1/4-inch of each other in circumference" (source uncertain; **needs SI** or the OEM tire spec). Sources: [Transmission Digest, "Shift Pointers: failures caused by incorrect tire sizes"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/shift-pointers-failures-caused-by-incorrect-tire-sizes/); [Tomorrow's Technician](https://www.tomorrowstechnician.com/transmission-failures-can-be-caused-by-incorrect-tire-sizes/); [Import Car](https://www.import-car.com/transmission-failures-caused-by-incorrect-tire-sizes/). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Overfill trap.** Fluid out the vent and slipping from **overfill**, or foam, can look like an internal failure. Sources: see the overfill findings in the first section ([Gearbox Insider](https://gearboxinsider.com/transmission-fluid-overfilled-symptoms-and-fix/); [Grassroots Motorsports](https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/grm/transmission-blowing-fluid-out-the-ventwhy/67390/page1/)). A.10.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Drainback trap.** A delay only on the first engagement after sitting is converter drainback, not a worn pump or clutch. Sources: see the drainback findings in the first section ([Core Transmission Parts](https://coretransmissionparts.com/blogs/news/4l60e-delayed-engagement-causes-diagnosis-and-the-real-fix); [Ram Forumz](https://www.ramforumz.com/threads/converter-drain-back-delayed-shifting.238018/)). A.8.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Post-flash trap.** Right after reprogramming, the adapts are zero, so rough shifts or flares are expected until the relearn is finished. They do not mean a new mechanical fault. Source: [Brake & Front End](https://www.brakeandfrontend.com/reprogramming-todays-transmission-control-modules/); GM adaptive-functions bulletin (snippet) ([NHTSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10112288-9999.pdf)). A.3, A.14. See `tcm_programming_relearn.md`.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Converter-housing leak trap.** Front pump seal leaks drip from the bellhousing and are confused with rear main seal leaks. Fluid color separates them (red ATF vs. dark engine oil). Sources: [Toyota Nation](https://www.toyotanation.com/threads/tran-fluid-leak-is-this-torque-converter-seal-problem.394727/); [Jeep Forum](https://www.jeepforum.com/threads/transmission-leak-42re-front-pump-seal.4326829/). A.10.

### Inferences
- **Mounts and CV joints:** both come before internal transmission parts when the complaint is a clunk on engagement or takeoff. Neither shows up in transmission scan data, so check them visually.
- **Stop/start clunk trap (INFERRED):** a clunk only after auto-restart, with normal engagement after a key start, is the stop/start hold system (A.15). It is not a mount or the main clutch. A mount would clunk on every engagement.
- **Cooler contamination trap (INFERRED):** milky ATF found during a "slipping" complaint means the radiator cooler must be replaced along with the transmission repair. Otherwise coolant keeps coming back. This follows from freeasestudyguides/GMB, which say to replace the radiator and flush both.
- **Mixed-evidence rule (INFERRED, Universal):** a symptom that follows the TCC being on or off, one gear range, or the transmission's own speed data belongs to the transmission. A symptom that follows engine data (misfire counts, TPS/MAF/MAP), wheel-speed data, tire size or chassis movement belongs outside it.
