## Q1. What data does the TCM take from other modules over CAN, and why does that make other-system faults look like transmission faults?

### Takeaway
The TCM does not decide shifts from its own sensors alone. It uses the engine's torque signal, and on many vehicles it also uses vehicle speed, brake-switch state and torque/gear requests from other modules (ECM, EBCM, BCM, ADAS modules), all over the serial data bus. A missing, wrong or implausible message from any of those modules can cause harsh shifts, wrong shift points, a TCC that won't apply or a default gear, even when the transmission itself is healthy.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): A GM shift-quality bulletin excerpt tells the tech to confirm scan data can be obtained and looks normal, comparing against "Control Module References." It lists the modules involved as **TCM, ECM, BCM, EBCM, IPC and TRCM**. — [GM service bulletin via NHTSA, 2020 (MC-10178286)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2020/MC-10178286-9999.pdf). A.22 / A.23. GM (OEM-specific), Needs SI for the exact model.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The shift control module uses more than calculated load, throttle and input/output speed. It also uses **MAF and engine temperature** to set shift points and line pressure. The TCM's data list can show engine values such as MAF grams per second. — [Gears Magazine, Transmission Scan Data Analysis](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/transmission-scan-data-analysis/). A.22. Universal principle.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Example of an engine input causing a shift complaint: a faulty **BARO sensor** makes the TCM assume sea level and more available engine torque, so it **raises line pressure** and the shifts turn harsh. Negative fuel trims appear alongside. — [Gears Magazine, Transmission Scan Data Analysis](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/transmission-scan-data-analysis/) (single snippet; the source name was attributed in the search summary). A.22. Universal principle; the specific strategy Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Vehicle speed can come from different places depending on design. Some manufacturers use **ABS wheel-speed sensors** for the speedometer and the VSS message to the ECM/TCM. Others use a **transmission OSS**, and the TCM then broadcasts VSS over CAN to the BCM. "You cannot assume where the VSS information for a given module or function may come from without consulting the service information." — [Underhood Service, Wheel Speed Sensors: ABS and TCS Signal Use](https://www.underhoodservice.com/wheel-speed-sensors-abs-and-tcs-signal-use/); also published at [Brake & Front End](https://www.brakeandfrontend.com/wheel-speed-sensors-abs-and-tcs-signal-use/) (same article, two outlets). A.22 / A.9. Universal principle; the source for any given car Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): ESC, traction control and electronic throttle programs live in different controllers and share information on the CAN bus. — [Underhood Service](https://www.underhoodservice.com/wheel-speed-sensors-abs-and-tcs-signal-use/). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **P0700** is informational. The ECM turns on the MIL when the TCM asks for it over serial data. The real fault codes are in the TCM, and not every scan tool can read the TCM. — [OBD-Codes P0700](https://www.obd-codes.com/p0700); [JEGS P0700 article](https://www.jegs.com/tech-articles/p0700-code-causes-fixes-transmission-control-system-mil-request/). A.22 / A.23. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): In one GM patent, the TCM sends CAN messages to the EBCM for a speed-control function, and during that control "the transmission module may be configured as a **slave to the EBCM**" to hold the right gearing. — GM patent result returned for query (USPTO, [Accelerator pedal torque request systems](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8209102) and related GM speed-control patents in the same result set; the snippet did not clearly show which patent number carried the sentence). A.22. Design principle only (patent); does not show a specific production vehicle.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): For A.22, the first step is a **whole-vehicle scan (all modules)**, not just a TCM/ECM scan. U-codes or chassis codes in the EBCM, BCM or ADAS modules can explain a "transmission" symptom. This follows from P0700 being only a pointer and from the TCM relying on other modules' data.
- INFERRED (Universal): When a harsh shift or wrong shift point comes with **no transmission-internal DTC**, compare the TCM's view of *received* data (engine torque, vehicle speed, brake status, throttle) with the source module's own PIDs. A mismatch points to a network or source-module problem, not the transmission.

### Gaps
- OPEN: A GM-published list of the exact CAN messages a particular GM TCM (6L80, 8L90, 10L80, 9T65) consumes was not found in public snippets. That is SI or GM training material.

## Q2. How do ADAS functions (adaptive cruise, forward collision alert/braking) interact with the transmission, and how can ADAS faults show up as transmission complaints?

### Takeaway
Adaptive cruise and collision-mitigation systems ask for **engine torque reduction**, **braking** and in some designs **downshifts** over the network. So a sudden slowdown, an unexpected downshift, or "the transmission kicked down on its own" can be a normal ADAS action or an ADAS fault, not a transmission fault. This was an OPEN item after the earlier pass. Public GM-specific evidence tying an ADAS fault to a transmission complaint is still thin. The design principle is well documented.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): GM ACC "automatically accelerates and brakes the vehicle up to moderate levels" to keep a driver-selected gap, works in stop-and-go traffic on many vehicles, and uses the existing **Forward Collision Alert sensors**. FCA uses the **Active Safety Control Module, the windshield front-view camera and the long-range radar sensor module**. — [GM Parts, Driver Assistance Systems (technical aid PDF)](https://www.gmparts.com/content/dam/gmparts/na/us/en/index/technical-resources/technical-documents/gm-driver-assistance-systems-aid.pdf); corroborated for the ACC/FCA description by [GM Authority, GM Adaptive Cruise Control](https://gmauthority.com/blog/gm/general-motors-technology/gm-safety-technology/gm-active-safety-technology/gm-adaptive-cruise-control-technology/) and [GM Authority, Forward Collision Alert](https://gmauthority.com/blog/gm/general-motors-technology/gm-safety-technology/gm-active-safety-technology/gm-forward-collision-alert/). A.22. GM (OEM-specific); module names Need SI for each model.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): In ACC designs, the ACC module produces a target speed and an **axle torque request**. A controller converts that into an engine torque command and **shift commands** sent to the engine and transmission controllers. "Engine torque reduction is used in vehicles equipped with adaptive cruise control." — USPTO patents returned for "adaptive cruise control commands transmission downshift": [Adaptive cruise control system for vehicle (6769504)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6769504); [OBD compliant rationality check for ACC (8521377)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8521377); [Transmission Digest, Torque Reduction](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/torque-reduction/) (ACC torque-reduction sentence). A.22. Design principle (patents); Universal as a concept, implementation Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): When ACC applies the brakes, the ACC system may tell the ECM to hold minimum/idle torque so the engine does not fight the braking. — GM patent result, same query set ([USPTO 8209102 result set](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8209102)). A.22. Design principle.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Some ACC patents describe a **downshift request** module (for example, a downshift signal when speed drops below a threshold). — [USPTO 8255134, ACC downshift request systems](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8255134) (written for manual-mode transmissions); [USPTO 10214210, Vehicle gear transition during braking event](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10214210) (title only). A.22. Design principle only.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Bosch describes ACC as a comfort system that intervenes in engine management and brakes "only within defined limits." Another source gives the heavy-vehicle order as de-throttle, then engine retarder, then foundation brakes. — [Bosch Mobility, Adaptive cruise control for passenger cars](https://www.bosch-mobility.com/en/solutions/assistance-systems/adaptive-cruise-control/); [Bosch Mobility, Adaptive distance control](https://www.bosch-mobility.com/en/solutions/assistance-systems/adaptive-distance-control/). A.22. Universal (supplier explainer).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): AEB uses a sequence of warnings, then **automatic torque reduction**, then retarder, then automatic braking. False activations are a known concern, reduced by camera/radar fusion. — [EU Commission AEBS report (CIRCABC)](https://circabc.europa.eu/sd/a/3ab87fdc-5715-4733-af50-c3608034ca56/report_aebs_%20en.pdf); [Bosch Mobility, Automatic emergency braking](https://www.bosch-mobility.com/en/solutions/assistance-systems/automatic-emergency-braking/). A.22. Universal (heavy-vehicle-weighted source for the sequence).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Owner-level complaints for some non-GM makes include **unexpected braking/sudden deceleration** and "front radar unavailable due to obstruction" messages. — [LemonLawAssist, forward emergency braking warning](https://lemonlawassist.com/car-issues/forward-emergency-braking-system-warning-light/). A.22. Consumer-level lead only (low quality); shows the complaint pattern.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Mercedes published an "Adaptive Cruise with Braking, Design and Function" document (M-366-001) through NHTSA. — [NHTSA TSB file MC-10219062](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10219062-0001.pdf) (title only; fetch blocked). A.22. OEM (non-GM). Lead only.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): **Symptom:** unexpected slowdown or downshift, "lost power" or "transmission kicked down" while cruising. **How to tell:** ask whether cruise/ACC was on. Check for an ACC/FCA/AEB event, radar or camera DTCs, or a "front radar blocked" message, and look for a braking request in EBCM data. **Next step:** do a whole-vehicle scan. Check active-safety module DTCs and the radar/camera for blockage or misalignment (Needs SI for calibration). Try to reproduce with ACC off. If the event disappears with ACC off and transmission data (commanded gear, slip) is normal, the transmission is not the cause.
- INFERRED (Universal): A downshift during ACC deceleration can be **normal operation** (engine braking and gear selection for the coming speed). It is not a defect by itself. Compare to a known-good vehicle or the SI "description and operation."
- INFERRED (Needs SI): On GM vehicles, an ACC/FCA fault more likely shows up as disabled ACC/FCA with a driver message than as a shift defect. This is not documented.

### Gaps
- OPEN: No public GM TechLink or TSB snippet was found that describes a transmission complaint (harsh downshift, TCC cycling) traced to an ADAS module or radar fault. Not found in snippets; may exist in GM SI or TechLink issues that were not indexed.
- OPEN: Whether a GM TCM gets direct "downshift requests" from the active safety module or only torque/axle-torque requests through the ECM/EBCM. Only patents speak to this. Needs SI.
- OPEN: Steering angle's role in shift scheduling (for example, holding a gear in a corner). No public source found.

## Q3. How do ABS / wheel-speed sensor / EBCM and traction/stability control faults present as transmission concerns?

### Takeaway
On vehicles that get vehicle speed from the ABS/EBCM, a failed wheel-speed sensor, lost EBCM communication (U0121) or a bus fault can take away the speed signal. The result can be no upshift or "stuck in a gear," a dead speedometer, a disabled TCC or cruise, or limp mode. Separately, traction/stability control **cuts engine torque** (throttle, spark, fuel) and brakes a wheel, which drivers feel as "slipping" or "hesitation."

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The EBCM runs ABS, traction control, StabiliTrak and hill-start assist, and "on newer cars feeds **wheel-speed data to the speedometer cluster, transmission, and cruise control**." When U0121 sets, all of those features drop out at once. — [FreeOnPlate, U0121](https://freeonplate.com/u0121-lost-communication-with-ebcm/) (aggregator). Corroborated: when the PCM loses the ABS data it loses the vehicle-speed signal used for shifting and cruise; "speedometer may not work and cars don't shift out of certain speeds." — [iCarsoft knowledge base, U0121](https://icarsoft.com/knowledge/article/u0121-code-lost-communication-with-abs-module-fix-it); [JustAnswer Dodge Ram U0121/U1110 "no speedometer"](https://www.justanswer.com/dodge/lvpsl-u0121-u1110-u1120-no-speedometer-installed-new-abs.html). A.22 / A.21. Applies to designs that use EBCM speed. Needs SI for whether a given GM uses OSS or WSS.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): U0121 causes include open or shorted CAN+/CAN- circuits, shorts to power or ground, and rarely a faulty module. Check module **power, grounds and bus voltages** first. One aggregator states that 35% of fixes were battery and 30% ground problems (aggregator statistic, method unknown; do not quote). — [OBD-Codes U0121](https://www.obd-codes.com/u0121); [RepairPal U0121](https://repairpal.com/obd-ii-code-u0121-lost-communication-with-anti-lock-brake-system-abs-control). A.21 / A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Owner and forum reports describe wheel-speed/ABS sensor faults causing no shift out of first, limp mode or "funky shifts" (Lexus LX, Chevy HHR, VW threads). — [LX Forums, limp mode no shift out of first, ABS sensors](https://www.lxforums.com/threads/car-in-limp-mode-no-shift-out-of-first-abs-sensors-bad.358783/); [ChevyHHR.net, wheel speed sensors not letting the transmission shift](https://www.chevyhhr.net/forums/problems-service-repairs-42/wheel-speed-sensors-not-letting-transmission-shift-52487/); [VWVortex, bad wheel speed sensor funky shifts](https://www.vwvortex.com/threads/can-a-bad-wheel-speed-sensor-cause-funky-shifts.9556049/). A.22. Forum-level (anecdotal); the pattern is consistent across makes.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Speed-signal loss usually puts the transmission in **fail-safe/limp**, often with **maximum line pressure** and a single middle gear (some units hold 3rd). — [Quora, bad speed sensor no shift](https://www.quora.com/Can-a-bad-speed-sensor-cause-the-transmission-not-to-shift-at-all) (lead-quality); [BenzWorld, speed sensor failure and limp home](https://www.benzworld.org/threads/does-speed-sensor-failure-lead-to-limp-home-mode.1511520/). A.22. The general principle is Universal; which gear it defaults to is design-specific (Needs SI).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Traction control reduces engine torque by "limiting throttle application and/or fuel delivery, retarding ignition spark, or completely shutting down engine cylinders," and/or brakes the spinning wheel. — [Wikipedia, Traction control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traction_control_system); [Cars.com, Common problems with traction control](https://www.cars.com/articles/common-problems-with-traction-control-1420680310438/). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): GM owner threads connect "Service StabiliTrak / Traction Control / Engine Power Reduced" messages with loss of power and "not shifting." Some were traced to throttle/TPS faults. — [SilveradoSierra, traction control, StabiliTrak, engine power reduced](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/traction-control-stabilitrak-engine-power-reduced.711607/); [AcadiaForum, loss of power, not shifting, traction control turns off](https://www.acadiaforum.net/threads/loss-of-power-not-shifting-traction-control-turns-off.33304/). A.22. GM, forum-level.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Brake & Front End has an article on the GM "Service ABS / TCS / Power Steering and Suspension" message (shared-network warnings across chassis systems). — [Brake & Front End](https://www.brakeandfrontend.com/gm-service-abs-tcs-power-steering-and-suspension-message/) (title only). A.22. GM, lead.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): **Symptom:** no upshift, stuck in one gear, dead or erratic speedometer, cruise and TCC not working, ABS/TCS lights on. **How to tell:** if the ABS/TCS/StabiliTrak lamps and a dead speedometer come with the shift complaint, the cause is probably upstream of the transmission. Compare each wheel-speed PID in the EBCM, and compare TCM OSS with the vehicle speed reported by the EBCM/IPC. **Next step:** whole-vehicle scan (U0121 or EBCM WSS DTCs), then WSS signal/circuit test and CAN power/ground/bus checks. Recheck shifting after the fault is repaired and codes are cleared.
- INFERRED (Universal): **Symptom:** "slips" or sags on wet or loose surfaces or in turns, with the RPM steady or dropping. That is different from true slip, where **RPM flares while vehicle speed stays flat** and TCM calculated slip rises. **How to tell:** watch for a TCS-active flag or indicator, a torque-reduction request in data, and wheel-speed differences. Real clutch slip shows as ISS/OSS ratio error in TCM data. TCS intervention shows as reduced engine torque with the gear ratio still correct.
- INFERRED (Universal): A wheel-speed sensor that drops out intermittently can briefly trigger ABS/TCS or send an implausible speed. That can cause shift busyness or momentary TCC release that feels like a transmission defect. Plausible, but not directly documented in snippets.

- OPEN: Which GM transmissions/platforms use EBCM wheel speed rather than transmission OSS for the TCM's shift schedule. Needs SI per vehicle; not public.
- OPEN: No GM TechLink snippet describing a WSS-caused TCC or shift concern was found.

## Q4. How does engine torque management (spark retard or torque reduction during shifts) and electronic throttle/APP work together with shift quality, and how do engine-side faults cause harsh shifts?

### Takeaway
During many upshifts the TCM asks the ECM to cut torque briefly, mostly by retarding spark. If the torque-reduction message is missing or wrong, or the ECM's torque estimate is off (fuel trim, airflow, BARO or throttle errors), the clutches have to absorb full engine torque and the shift is harsh. Electronic throttle/APP faults can put the engine into reduced power, which drivers often report as "the transmission won't shift" or "stuck in gear."

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Torque reduction is commanded during upshifts. The TCM may tell the ECM to reduce engine torque to bring engine speed down to match the higher gear. — [Transmission Digest, Torque Reduction](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/torque-reduction/). Corroborated by GM-assigned patents on crankshaft torque modification during shifts: [USPTO 8721498](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8721498); [USPTO 8696517](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8696517). A.22. Universal principle.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Spark retard is the preferred torque-reduction method because it removes and restores torque quickly. — [USPTO 8721498 result](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8721498); [Quora, torque management during shifts](https://www.quora.com/What-is-torque-management-in-automatic-transmissions-and-why-is-it-used-during-gear-shifts) (lead). A.22. Universal principle.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Transmission Digest case:** 2007 Pontiac Solstice, 2.4L, **5L40-E**, harsh-shift complaint, only **P2637** (invalid engine torque signal from ECM to TCM). **Long-term fuel trim was about -20**, showing a combustion/fueling problem that threw off the engine torque calculation and caused the hard shifts. — [Transmission Digest, Torque Reduction](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/torque-reduction/) (snippet; fetch blocked, so the final repair was not seen). A.22 / A.23. GM (OEM case); lesson is Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): "Harsh shifts are typically a byproduct of an engine torque-reduction error code being set." If the torque-management request is not received (broken or corrupted communication), the engine keeps full power through the shift. — [iCarsoft, P2544 Torque Management Request Signal A](https://www.icarsoft-us.com/blogs/news/p2544-torque-management-request-signal-a-harsh-shift-fix); the concept matches the Transmission Digest snippet above. A.22. Universal (P2544 is a generic code; its exact setting criteria Need SI).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): HP Tuners has a GM "Transmission > Torque Management" parameter group, which shows GM calibrations include shift torque-management tables. — [HP Tuners help, GM Trans Torque Management](https://www.hptuners.eu/help/vcm_editor_parameters_gm_trans_tm.htm). A.22. GM (aftermarket tuning doc; used only to show the tables exist).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): "Reduced Engine Power" is a protective mode set when the ECM sees conflicting throttle or pedal sensor data. Examples: **P2135** (TP sensor A/B correlation) and **P2138** (APP sensor 1/2 correlation). In limp mode the vehicle "may be stuck in a higher gear." — [Carista, Engine Power Reduced warning](https://carista.com/en-us/blogs/news/engine-power-reduced-warning-common-causes-and-how-to-fix-them); [go-parts, GM throttle body failure, reduced engine power](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/fuel-injection-throttle-body-cadillac-cts-chevrolet-camaro-chevrolet-silverado-1500-2014-2025); [CarFromJapan, TPS symptoms](https://carfromjapan.com/article/car-maintenance/throttle-position-sensor-symptoms/). A.22. Universal concept; GM-specific limp behavior Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Engine-side inputs (misfire, MAF, TPS) are commonly missed causes of harsh shifts. — Already logged in [Apex Tech Nation, Harsh shift diagnosis](https://apextechnation.com/articles/harsh-shift-diagnosis) (see earlier pass). A.22. Universal.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): **Symptom:** harsh upshifts (often the same shifts every time), sometimes with no transmission DTC or only a "torque signal invalid / torque management" code. **How to tell:** read ECM DTCs and fuel trims. Look at engine torque PIDs (actual vs. requested), torque-reduction request/delivered PIDs and spark advance during the shift if available. A spark-retard dip that doesn't happen during the shift, or engine torque data that doesn't make sense, points to the engine or the network. **Next step:** fix the engine fault (fueling, airflow, BARO, misfire) or the communication fault first. Then re-evaluate shift quality, and run the shift-adapt relearn only if SI calls for it. Do not reset adapts first to "see if it helps"; the GM bulletin snippet in Q9 warns against a fast learn when a specific shift complaint exists.
- INFERRED (Universal): **Symptom:** "won't shift / stuck in gear / no power," with a Reduced Engine Power or StabiliTrak message. **How to tell:** ETC/APP DTCs in the ECM (for example P2135/P2138 class), throttle desired vs. actual PIDs. The TCM's gear command and ratio look normal for the very low torque. **Next step:** diagnose the ETC/APP circuit (reference, ground, signals) before touching the transmission.

### Gaps
- OPEN: The final repair in the Solstice case (what caused the -20 trim) was not visible in the snippet.
- OPEN: GM PID names for torque-reduction request/actual on current GM TCMs. Needs SI or a scan tool.

## Q5. How do low battery voltage, charging-system faults and power/ground problems (A.21) cause erratic shifting, U-codes and limp mode?

### Takeaway
Modules need steady supply voltage and good grounds. Weak batteries, charging faults, high alternator AC ripple and poor grounds can cause TCM resets, lost communication (U0101/U0100/U0121), corrupted data and TCM-stored codes. The driver feels harsh shifts, lost gears or default-gear operation. Several U-codes across several modules point to a power, ground or bus problem, not to the transmission.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): One of the most common causes of **U0101** (lost communication with TCM) is a weak battery or unstable voltage (failing battery, irregular alternator output, poor/loose grounds). "Even a brief voltage drop can cause the TCM to shut down or stop transmitting data." — [Sun Transmissions, U0101](https://suntransmissions.com/blogs/news/code-u0101-lost-communication-with-the-tcm-transmission-control-module); [OBD-Codes U0101](https://www.obd-codes.com/u0101). A.21 / A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **U0402** (invalid data from TCM) can come from weak battery, failing alternator or corroded terminals causing voltage drops that corrupt data packets. **U0103** (lost communication with gear shift module) can be low resting voltage or a blown shifter fuse "without any actual module failure." — [go-parts, U0402](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-u0402); [go-parts, U0103](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-u0103). A.21 / A.22. Universal concept. The voltage figures in these aggregator snippets are **not specs**; do not quote. Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Multiple U-codes across multiple modules indicate a bus problem** rather than individual module failures. With key on engine off, CAN-H and CAN-L each normally read about mid-supply (roughly 2.5 V). If one is pulled high or low, suspect a short or a module holding the bus down. Low or unstable voltage can interrupt TCM startup and leave U0101 in history. — [Apex Tech Nation, CAN bus diagnostics](https://apextechnation.com/articles/can-bus-diagnostics); [iCarsoft, U0101](https://www.icarsoft-us.com/blogs/news/u0101-code). A.21 / A.22. Universal principle; bus voltage values for a given network Need SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Too much alternator AC ripple (usually from a failed rectifier diode or stator winding) "can cause electronic modules to misbehave." On Ford F-150/Super Duty, TCM-stored **P1674** (control module software/power) comes with harsh shifting, clunks or lost gears, tied to low voltage or high ripple. — [go-parts, P1674](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p1674) (Ford-specific); [MOTOR, Alternator Ripple Effect](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/alternator-ripple-effect/); [Pico Technology guided test, alternator AC ripple](https://www.picoauto.com/library/automotive-guided-tests/charging-starting/charging/AGT-002-alternator-ripple-without-ecm-control/); [Fluke, testing ripple](https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/digital-multimeters/how-to-test-alternator-ripple-voltage-with-a-multimeter). A.21. The ripple principle is Universal; P1674 is Ford (OEM-specific). The ripple thresholds shown in snippets are aggregator figures, **not specs**; use the tester or SI value.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): When communication faults happen, the transmission may use a failsafe strategy and stay in one gear, or command a default state. — [Apex Tech Nation CAN article](https://apextechnation.com/articles/can-bus-diagnostics) and patent result [USPTO 9829096](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9829096). A.22. Universal principle; default behavior Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The Motor Age/MOTOR article "A New Twist on Network Diagnosis" exists (content not visible in snippet). — [MOTOR magazine](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/new-twist-network-diagnosis/). Lead only.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal; plays to Logan's electrical strength): **Symptom:** intermittent harsh or erratic shifts, lost gears, limp mode, "Service Transmission" message, often after a no-start, jump-start, battery replacement or during high electrical load. U-codes appear in several modules. **How to tell:** freeze-frame/history shows U-codes in many modules at once, and system voltage PIDs in the TCM/ECM drop or swing. The transmission symptom clears after a key cycle. **Next step:** test and charge the battery. Test charging output and ripple with a lab scope or tester. Voltage-drop test the TCM power and ground circuits under load. Check CAN-H/CAN-L voltages and termination resistance per SI. Then clear codes and verify before any transmission work.
- INFERRED (Universal): A **single** U-code in only one module points more to that module's power/ground or its bus branch. Many modules setting U-codes at the same time points to supply voltage or a bus short.
- INFERRED (Universal): Low supply voltage lowers the current available to PWM/pressure-control solenoids and can distort line-pressure control. Plausible mechanism for harsh or soft shifts; not directly documented in snippets.

## Q6. How do transfer case/AWD coupling, driveline (U-joint, CV, differential), engine mount and exhaust problems get mistaken for transmission faults?

### Takeaway
Binding, shudder in tight turns, clunks on engagement and speed-related vibrations often come from AWD couplings, transfer cases, driveshafts, differentials, mounts or exhaust contact, not from the transmission. The quick tests are: does it follow **vehicle speed** or **engine speed/gear**; does it go away when AWD is disabled (fuse); does it change on **tight turns**; and does it match chassis/engine movement.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): AWD coupling concerns can cause noise, vibration, shudder, jerking, binding or chatter **while turning at low speed**, attributed to decreased friction of the coupling oil in harsh use. — [Hyundai/Kia service bulletin via NHTSA, RDU binding/shudder and/or bump/clunk (MC-10189785)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2021/MC-10189785-0001.pdf); [go-parts, Hyundai/Kia AWD coupling failure](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/heads-up-display-assembly-kia-k5-hyundai-santa-fe-hyundai-santa-cruz-2021-2026). A.22. OEM-specific (Hyundai/Kia); the symptom pattern is Universal for electronically controlled couplings.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Transfer-case problems "can often be mistaken for transmission issues, misfires, or even bad tires." **Tire circumference mismatch** is "the most common misdiagnosis." A quick trick is to **pull the AWD fuse**: if the binding stops, suspect the coupling. The drive test is slow, tight circles near full lock in both directions. — [Apex Tech Nation, Transfer case diagnosis](https://apextechnation.com/articles/transfer-case-diagnosis); [Apex Tech Nation, Viscous coupling explained](https://apextechnation.com/articles/viscous-coupling-center-diff). A.22. Universal. (Single publisher; the fuse trick applies only to electronically actuated couplings and depends on the vehicle, so Needs SI.)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Separating driveline from transmission noise: change gears when the noise occurs while holding road speed. If the noise or vibration frequency **changes**, the source is engine or transmission. If it **stays the same**, it is driveshaft or driveline. A steady vibration that rises with road speed points to U-joints or driveshaft balance. — [FreeASEStudyGuides, Driveline vibrations](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/driveline-vibrations.html); [4xShaft, Diagnosing drive shaft vibrations](https://4xshaft.com/blogs/general-tech-info-articles/diagnosing-drive-shaft-vibrations). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Worn U-joints and worn transmission slip yokes cause similar symptoms: clunk into reverse, driveline slack, highway vibration, play on takeoff. Early U-joint vibration can be mistaken for tire imbalance. — [Coastline Restorations, slip yoke vs U-joints](https://coastlinerestorations.com/symptoms-of-bad-transmission-slip-yoke-vs-failed-u-joints/); [ZekeParts, bad U-joint symptoms](https://www.zekeparts.com/blog/bad-u-joint-symptoms/). A.22. Universal (RWD/4WD).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Differential noise is often misattributed, and owners replace tires, driveshafts and suspension parts without finding the source. — [West Coast Differentials, diagnosing differential problems](https://differentials.com/diagnosing-differential-problems/); [TDot Performance, diagnosing differential and driveline noises](https://blog.tdotperformance.ca/guides/diagnosing-differential-driveline-noises/). A.22. Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Worn or broken engine/transmission mounts make a clunk or thud on engagement or shifts, and a loose mount lets **the exhaust knock against the body**. "A loose exhaust hanger, damaged motor bracket, or worn transmission mount can create a similar thump." — [Counterman, Engine and transmission mount noise](https://www.counterman.com/engine-and-transmission-mounts-keep-noise-and-vibration-at-bay/); [Lodi Shell, engine mount failure signs](https://lodishell.net/2026/05/25/engine-mount-failure-signs/); [1A Auto, bad engine mounts, jerking when shifting](https://blog.1aauto.com/bad-engine-mounts/). A.22. Universal.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): **Binding or shudder only in tight, low-speed turns** → transfer case/AWD coupling or tire mismatch (check tire sizes, wear and pressure first), not the transmission. Transmission shudder follows TCC apply or a gear range, not steering angle.
- INFERRED (Universal): **Clunk on Drive/Reverse engagement:** have a helper hold the brakes, shift N→D and N→R, and watch engine rock, mounts, exhaust hangers and heat shields. Excess engine movement or visible contact = mount/exhaust. No movement plus a delayed or harsh engagement in TCM data (engagement time, line pressure command) = transmission.
- INFERRED (Universal): **Vibration:** match it to engine RPM (engine/converter/TCC side) or to road speed (driveline/tires). Use the gear-change test at steady speed, or a vibration analyzer if available.
- INFERRED (GM-relevant): On GM AWD/4WD, confirm transfer-case/AWD DTCs (TCCM/AWD module) in the whole-vehicle scan. Their module names and actuator tests Need SI.

- OPEN: No GM-specific AWD coupling bulletin found in snippets (the bulletin found was Hyundai/Kia).
- OPEN: The source of the exhaust-contact finding is consumer-grade. No trade-press article centered on exhaust contact mistaken for a transmission problem was found.

## Q7. How do tire size and axle ratio changes, and the related programming, affect shift points, speedometer and transmission behavior?

### Takeaway
GM calibrations store factory tire size and axle ratio. Changing either without reprogramming throws off the speedometer/odometer and the vehicle-speed-based functions, and can shift the effective shift points and features like grade braking. Sources disagree on whether the TCM's shift *decision* itself changes, because it depends on whether that design schedules on output-shaft speed or calculated road speed.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): "The TCM calibration is based on factory tire size and rear axle ratio, and changing either of these parameters will have some adverse impact on transmission operation, including features like **grade braking and shift stabilization**." — [DieselPlace, changing TCM calibration for larger tires](https://www.dieselplace.com/threads/changing-tcm-calibration-for-larger-tires.60462/) (forum; it paraphrases what appears to be GM wording). A.22 / B-area programming. GM, forum-level; Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), **conflicting**: another forum post argues the TCM math uses the output shaft counter, so the shift *decision* does not change with tire size. — [DieselPlace, re-gearing and larger tire size settings in ECM and TCM](https://www.dieselplace.com/threads/re-gearing-and-larger-tire-size-settings-in-ecm-and-tcm.295670/). Contradicted by HP Tuners, whose "automatic scaling" rescales transmission shift tables to match new gear ratio and tire size (implying shift points expressed in road speed are affected). — [HP Tuners, Gear & Tire Adjustment Guide](https://www.hptuners.com/articles/gear-tire-adjustment-guide-for-vcm-suite/); [HP Tuners support, GM speedometer calibration](https://support.hptuners.com/support/solutions/articles/153000064342-gm-speedometer-calibration). A.22. GM; design-dependent.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Tire size changes throw off the speedometer, odometer, fuel economy and miles-to-empty calculations, plus ECM tables that use vehicle speed. — [Colorado Fans, reprogram ECM after tire/wheel size change](https://www.coloradofans.com/threads/reprogram-ecm-after-tire-wheel-size-change.421633/); [Chevrolet Forum, reprogram speedometer for different tire size](https://chevroletforum.com/forum/1999-2006-gmt800-108/need-reprogram-speedometer-different-tire-size-104786/). A.22. GM, forum-level.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): **Symptom:** "shifts late/early," "speedometer is off," "busy shifting on grades," or grade braking/cruise acts oddly after a tire, lift or gear change. **How to tell:** check the actual tire size and axle ratio against the RPO/calibration (GM: SPID label RPO codes for the axle ratio) and compare the speedometer with GPS. **Next step:** reprogram tire size/axle ratio using the OEM programming tool (GM: SPS/tire-size calibration where supported; Needs SI). Do not diagnose the transmission until the calibration matches the hardware.
- INFERRED (Universal): When the TCM computes vehicle speed from OSS and a stored tire/axle constant, the reported road speed is wrong. The TCM may send that wrong speed to other modules (speedometer, cruise, ABS plausibility), which spreads the error across systems.
- INFERRED: Mismatched tire *circumference between axles* is a separate issue (AWD/4WD wind-up, already in the earlier pass). Uniformly oversized tires cause a calibration error, not a bind.

### Gaps
- OPEN: No public GM source (TechLink/SI) was found that states how GM TCMs use tire size in the shift schedule. Forum sources conflict. Needs SI.

## Q8. What stop/start-related complaints look like transmission problems?

### Takeaway
On stop/start vehicles, the transmission has to hold clutch apply while the engine is off, using an **auxiliary electric pump** or a **hydraulic accumulator**. A failure there, or the wrong valve body, gives a bump, lurch or delayed engagement **on auto-restart**. It can look like a clutch fault, but the root cause is in the stop/start support hardware. Some restart bumps are normal characteristics.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): GM transmissions with automatic stop/start use an electric transmission fluid pump that keeps fluid pressure during an auto-stop event. — [GM TechLink, Mid-January 2025 issue (10-speed transmission item)](https://gm-techlink.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/GM_TechLink_02_Mid-January_2025.pdf) (snippet; the snippet did not attribute the "G5" pump designation clearly to a specific unit). A.22. GM (OEM-specific); the application Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): On **GM 8L90** stop/start applications, the valve body has an extra case-side hole that feeds the **12345R clutch** from the auxiliary fluid accumulator. **Putting a non-stop/start valve body in a stop/start unit causes delayed, abrupt engagement after engine start.** — [Transmission Digest, The GM 8L90 Stop/Start or Go/No-Go](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/gm-8l90-stop-start/); [Transmission Digest, stop/start accumulators](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/stop-start-accumulators-what-you-dont-know-can-hurt-you/). A.22 / B/C (component ID). GM 8L90 (OEM-specific).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): When the auxiliary accumulator has no pressure, the engine restarts with a very noticeable bump or lurch. One or more clutches have released, and the main pump reapplies them hard. — [Gears Magazine, "Light Turns Green, Car Turns Mean" (P171D accumulator system performance)](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/light-turns-green-car-turns-mean-p171d-accumulator-sys-performance/). A.22. OEM (article-specific; P171D is a manufacturer-specific code, so Needs SI).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Some 2015–2016 GM vehicles with 8L45/8L90 may have delayed engagement into Drive or Reverse after sitting engine-off for hours or overnight. — [Transmission Digest 8L90 archive/snippet](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/gm-8l90-stop-start/). Also reported: an infrequent audible thud 400–700 ms after a key start or garage shift (source attribution unclear in the snippet; GM TechLink 2025 or TD). A.22. GM. Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Auxiliary pump failure (for example **P2797** on 2021–2025 Chevrolet Trailblazer) gives delay, hesitation or a harsh clunk when pulling away from a stop. — [go-parts, P2797 Trailblazer](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p2797-chevrolet-trailblazer-2021-2025). A.22. GM (aggregator; Needs SI).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Non-GM example: Toyota TSBs 0110-22 (pop/clunk on initial start) and 0111-22 (hesitation from stop, surge) for 2022–2023 Tundra, both fixed with a TCM software update. — [autoevolution, Toyota Tundra TSBs](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/toyota-issues-tsbs-for-tundra-pop-clunk-noise-and-hesitation-from-stop-and-surge-concerns-207408.html). A.22. OEM (Toyota). Shows that a software fix, not hardware, is sometimes the repair.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): **Symptom:** bump, lurch, clunk or hesitation **only on auto-restart** (not on key start or a normal stop). **How to tell:** reproduce with stop/start on, then with it disabled (button off). If the symptom only happens with auto-stop events, focus on the aux pump/accumulator, its DTCs, and the battery/stop/start enabling conditions (the BCM/ECM inhibit auto-stop when the battery state of charge is low; Needs SI). **Next step:** check for aux pump or accumulator DTCs, run aux pump output tests per SI, confirm the correct stop/start valve body after any valve body replacement, and check for TSBs/software.
- INFERRED (Universal, A.21 overlap): A weak battery can **disable** auto-stop entirely ("my stop/start quit working"). That is an electrical/BCM condition, not a transmission fault.

## Q9. What brake-switch and other "small input" faults show up as transmission complaints, and what general diagnostic order separates the transmission from other systems?

### Takeaway
The brake pedal switch/position sensor tells the TCM/ECM to release the TCC and, on some units, to use neutral-idle at a stop. A faulty switch can cause TCC no-apply, stalling at a stop, or the transmission dropping in and out of gear at a stop. The Gears Magazine 6T40 case shows the cost: two transmissions replaced for a brake-switch fault. The overall order is: whole-vehicle scan, verify inputs and network data, rule out chassis/driveline causes, then condemn the transmission.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): **Gears Magazine case:** 2012 Chevy Cruze, **6T40**, 42,000 miles, clean fluid, **dropping in and out of gear at a stop**, no codes. The transmission was torn down (nothing found), rebuilt with a new TEHCM, then replaced with a used unit, and the problem stayed. Cause: **the brake light switch was cycling on and off at a stop**, in time with the drop-outs. The transmission is designed to go to neutral at a stop with the brake applied, and the bad switch kept triggering that feature. — [Gears Magazine, Faulty brake light switch has transmission shifting in and out of gear while stopped](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/faulty-brake-light-switch-has-transmission-shifting-in-and-out-of-gear-while-stopped/). Corroborated by a similar non-GM pattern: [Acurazine, faulty brake light switch causing shift into neutral while stopped](https://acurazine.com/forums/1-2g-mdx-2001-2013-166/faulty-brake-light-switch-causing-shift-into-neutral-while-stopped-982150/); [Gears Magazine, 2013 Santa Fe loses power, won't shift due to faulty brake light switch](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/2013-santa-fe-loses-power-wont-shift-due-faulty-brake-light-switch/). A.22. GM case (6T40); the pattern is Universal.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): The stop-lamp switch releases the TCC and cancels cruise. Faults cause TCC no-apply, or **stalling at a stop** when the TCC doesn't release. Working brake lamps and cruise cancel **do not prove the switch is good**: bent or missing internal wipers made the ECM see the brake as always applied. — [YourMechanic, P0703 torque converter/brake switch B circuit](https://www.yourmechanic.com/article/p0703-torque-converter-brake-switch-b-circuit-malfunction-by-jerry-renshaw); [LiveAbout, diagnosing GM converter lock-up problems](https://www.liveabout.com/diagnosing-gm-converter-lock-up-problems-262653); [DieselPlace, brake pedal switch failure and TCC operation](https://www.dieselplace.com/threads/brake-pedal-switch-failure-tcc-operation.398647/). A.22 / A.9. Universal (P0703 is generic).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): GM bulletin guidance on shift-quality work: if the transmission, valve body or TCM is replaced, reset shift adapts with Characterization Data refresh and Service Fast Learn. But **if there is a specific shift concern, do not perform Service Fast Learn**; complete the appropriate driving learn procedure instead. — [GM service bulletin via NHTSA 2020 (MC-10178286)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2020/MC-10178286-9999.pdf); related [GM bulletin 16-NA-019, Transmission adaptive functions / low-mileage harsh shifts (NHTSA MC-10112288)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10112288-9999.pdf). A.22 / A.23 / B. GM (OEM-specific), Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): GM 16-NA-019: low-mileage vehicles may feel firm/harsh, slip or flare while the adaptive function learns. A harsh feel coming to a stop is "most likely a 3-1 shift." — [NHTSA MC-10112288](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10112288-9999.pdf). A.22. GM. (Adaptive learning as normal operation, an alternative to "defect.")
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Some GM vehicles may rock slightly in Park at startup after a cold soak, with a clunk, from residual fluid in the clutch packs causing a partial apply. — [Search summary citing GM bulletin content, via Audizine/Colorado Fans result set](https://www.coloradofans.com/threads/transmission-clunk-on-startup.427024/) (attribution weak; treat as a lead). A.22. GM; Needs SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): Transmission Digest has a "Three rules for transmission diagnosis" article (content not visible). — [Transmission Digest](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/three-rules-for-transmission-diagnosis/). Lead only.

### Inferences
- INFERRED (Universal): **Brake switch.** **Symptom:** TCC won't apply (possible P0741-type code or poor highway fuel economy), stall at a stop, or neutral-idle cycling at a stop. **How to tell:** watch the brake switch/pedal position PID in the TCM and ECM while pressing and releasing the pedal and while holding it steady at a stop. Look for flicker in time with the symptom. **Next step:** repair the switch or its adjustment/circuit, then verify.
- INFERRED (Universal), **suggested A.22 triage order** built from all findings above:
  1. Verify the complaint and conditions (cruise/ACC on? stop/start event? turning? 4WD/AWD mode? recent tires, battery or programming?).
  2. **Whole-vehicle scan**: all modules, history and current, U-codes, ADAS/EBCM/transfer-case codes, TSB check.
  3. **A.21 base checks**: battery state, charging output and ripple, TCM power/ground voltage drop, bus health if U-codes are present.
  4. **Input/data plausibility**: engine torque, fuel trims, throttle/APP, BARO/MAF, vehicle-speed source, wheel speeds, brake switch, as seen by the TCM compared with the source module.
  5. **Chassis/driveline isolation**: tire sizes, AWD fuse/2WD test, tight-turn test, gear-change-at-speed vibration test, mount/exhaust check under brake-torque engagement.
  6. Only then do transmission-internal tests (pressure, slip/ratio data, clutch-apply data) and repair, followed by the SI-specified relearn.

- OPEN: GM neutral-idle strategy applications and enable conditions (the 6T40 case implies a stop-neutral feature). Needs SI.
- OPEN: No public source ranked how often each non-transmission cause turns up in real transmission misdiagnosis. Only case studies and forum patterns exist.
