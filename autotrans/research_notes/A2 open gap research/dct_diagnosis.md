## Q1. How do odd/even shafts and pre-selection work, and why does that matter for diagnosis?

### Takeaway
A DCT is two manual gearboxes sharing one output. Odd gears sit on one input shaft and even gears (usually with reverse) on a concentric hollow shaft, and each shaft has its own clutch. The TCM pre-selects the next gear on the idle shaft with a synchronizer/shift fork, then swaps clutches. That lets you split faults into "one clutch/shaft" patterns (only odd or only even gears affected) versus faults common to both.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / UNIVERSAL / A.1: Odd gears (1-3-5-7) are on one input shaft. Even gears (2-4-6, and R) are on a hollow input shaft running concentrically around it. Each shaft has its own clutch. — [Firgelli Auto, Dual-clutch transmission mechanism](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/dual-clutch-transmission); [PatSnap Eureka, How a DCT works](https://eureka.patsnap.com/article/how-a-dual-clutch-transmission-works-inner-mechanism-explained)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL / A.1: Pre-selection means engaging a non-driven gear on the idle shaft while the vehicle is driving in a gear on the other shaft. The transmission can be "in two gears at once", but only one clutch transmits power at any moment. An upshift = engage the target gear on the non-driven shaft, then release the driving clutch while applying the other. — US patent language via [USPTO 8600633, Gear preselect systems for a DCT](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8600633) and [USPTO 8375816](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8375816) (patent text; authoritative on mechanism, not on any specific vehicle)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A shift is essentially a clutch hand-off, with no torque interruption. One excerpt gives a shift-time range; it is a marketing-style figure, so don't memorize it. — [Firgelli Auto](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/dual-clutch-transmission)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Ford DPS6): The DPS6's two clutches are called Clutch A and Clutch B, integrated into one assembly. — [Gears Magazine, Diagnosing the DPS6](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/diagnosing-the-dps6-knowing-the-good-helps-you-identify-the-bad/); [ATRA DPS6 Introduction (Bill Brayton), PDF mirror](https://akpphelp.ru/assets/template/redesign/files/guidelines/6dct250-manual-scheme-photo.pdf)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: **Odd-vs-even split is the DCT version of an apply chart.** A fault that affects only 1-3-5 (or only 2-4-6-R) points at that shaft's clutch, its actuator/position sensor, or that half's shift forks/synchros. A fault in every gear points at something common: the TCM/mechatronic supply or pressure, the engine-torque signal, the dual-mass flywheel, or contamination of both clutch faces. This mirrors the elimination logic in `apply_chart_reasoning.md`.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Because pre-selection is predictive, a TCM that guesses wrong (for example the driver lifts off after an upshift was pre-selected, or kicks down) has to swap the pre-selected gear before the shift. That shows up as a **delay or hesitation on kickdown or on a "change of mind"**, not as slipping. It is a normal DCT characteristic that customers report as a concern. It is also a reason TSBs often recalibrate software rather than replace parts (see Q6).
- INFERRED: The trap is to call a DCT "an automatic with two clutch packs". It has no planetary gearsets and no apply chart of bands and clutches, and there is no hydraulic "shift valve" logic in the TC-auto sense. The shifts are manual-transmission synchronizer engagements.

### Gaps
- OPEN: No public source reached confirms which shaft carries reverse on each named DCT (DPS6, DQ200, D7GF1). Reverse is commonly on the even shaft, but verify per unit (SI).

## Q2. Dry vs. wet DCTs: which units are which, and how does that change symptoms and service?

### Takeaway
**Dry** DCTs (Ford DPS6, VW DQ200, Hyundai/Kia 7DCT D7GF1/D7UF1) have clutches running in air, outside the gear-oil sump. They shed heat poorly, so they have to limit slip. Typical complaints are low-speed judder, overheat warnings in creep and on hills, and contamination if oil gets onto the clutch. **Wet** DCTs (VW DQ250/DQ381, Hyundai/Kia 8DCT, GM/Tremec TR-9080 in the C8 Corvette) run multi-plate clutches in fluid that also cools them. They handle more torque and heat, but the fluid condition becomes a clutch and mechatronic issue, so they need periodic fluid (and often filter) service.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (Ford DPS6): A fully automatic, **dry** dual-clutch, electronically controlled, automatically shifted 6-speed manual transmission, designed to operate like a conventional automatic. — [Gears Magazine, Diagnosing the DPS6](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/diagnosing-the-dps6-knowing-the-good-helps-you-identify-the-bad/); [ATRA DPS6 Introduction PDF](https://akpphelp.ru/assets/template/redesign/files/guidelines/6dct250-manual-scheme-photo.pdf); [Scribd, DPS6 Intro](https://www.scribd.com/document/325670316/DPS6-Intro)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (VW): The DQ200 is a 7-speed **dry**-clutch unit used with smaller engines. The DQ250 is a 6-speed **wet** multi-plate unit rated for more torque. The excerpt quotes torque ratings (DQ250 ≈ 400 N·m, DQ200 ≈ 250 N·m) from secondary sources; treat them as OEM-context only. — [Wikipedia, Direct-shift gearbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-shift_gearbox); [AxleAddict, DQ250 vs DQ200](https://axleaddict.com/repair-maintenance/vw-dsg-transmission-problems-guide); [DSG Repair Brisbane, Wet vs dry DSG clutches](https://dsgrepairbrisbane.com.au/wet-versus-dry-dsg-clutch/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL principle, OEM example (VW): A dry clutch isn't bathed in oil, so it can't reject heat as quickly as a wet clutch. It can overheat in hard driving, on the track, or in slow traffic up hills. Long periods of low-speed slip speed up dry-clutch wear. — [DSG Repair Brisbane](https://dsgrepairbrisbane.com.au/wet-versus-dry-dsg-clutch/); [AxleAddict](https://axleaddict.com/repair-maintenance/vw-dsg-transmission-problems-guide) (specialist-shop and consumer sites; principle is consistent with Hyundai OEM owner's-manual text below)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Hyundai, OEM source): Because the **dry-type** DCT transfers power by friction between clutch cover and disc, it can overheat. The system gives visual and audible warnings (LCD cluster message and chime) when clutch temperature is above normal. Named conditions: repeated stop-and-go up hills at slow speeds, and prolonged heavy stop-and-go traffic. — [Kia/Hyundai TSB PDF (KMA), NHTSA mirror](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/SB-10135096-0699.pdf); [Hyundai Owner's Manual, DCT warning messages](https://ownersmanual.hyundai.com/docview/webhelp/Hyundai/63f60ca1-48cd-4943-adfc-f084815f8405/idcc514d707a6.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Hyundai/Kia): The D7GF1 is a 7-speed dual-clutch "automatically shifted manual transmission" built by Hyundai Powertech. It uses electric servos and motors as its actuators. — [Gears Magazine, The D7GF1 and D7UF1](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/the-d7gf1-and-d7uf1-a-closer-look-at-hyundai-kia-dcts/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Hyundai/Kia 8-speed **wet** DCT): The 8WDCT is found in turbo-2.5 and N models (for example Santa Fe 2.5T, Sonata N-Line, Santa Cruz 2.5T, Elantra N, Kona N, K5 GT, Sorento 2.5T). Model list from a secondary source. — [go-parts, 2020–2026 Hyundai/Kia transmission guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/transmission-assembly-kia-carnival-hyundai-elantra-hyundai-kona-2020-2026); [Hyundai TSB PDF, 8WDCT judder](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10206957-0001.pdf)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (GM): The C8 Corvette's 8-speed DCT is GM RPO **M1L**, supplied by Tremec as the **TR-9080**. It is a wet DCT with specified FFL-4 fluid (Pentosin/Fuchs FFL-4, ACDelco DCTF). — [Wikipedia, Chevrolet Corvette (C8)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Corvette_(C8)); [Tremec TR-9080 literature PDF (Corvette Action Center)](https://www.corvetteactioncenter.com/specs/c8-corvette/2020-corvette/images/TREMEC_TR-9080DCT_literature.pdf); [LSX Mag, Inside the TR-9080](https://www.lsxmag.com/tech-stories/tremec-tr-9080-inside-the-c8s-dual-clutch-8-speed-part-1/) (fluid list from forum/press excerpts; confirm fluid in GM SI)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL — how dry vs. wet changes the symptom picture:
  - **Dry:** heat is the main enemy. Expect judder at launch and in creep, a thermal-protection warning after repeated creeping, uphill parking or trailer launches, and burnt-friction smell. Any oil on the clutch (a seal leak) is fatal to the friction surface, so the fix is replacement, not cleaning (see Q4). There's no clutch fluid to service. The gear oil is separate.
  - **Wet:** heat is handled by the fluid, so judder is more often tied to **fluid condition and calibration** (friction modifiers aging). A fluid change with the correct fluid plus a relearn is a legitimate first step, which is never true for a dry clutch. Clutch debris circulates through the mechatronic valves, so wet-unit mechatronic faults are partly fluid-maintenance faults.
- INFERRED / GM relevance: For a GM dealership tech, the GM-branded DCT is the C8's TR-9080 (wet). GM's mass-market lineup is otherwise torque-converter automatics. DPS6, DQ200 and 7DCT are the dry DCTs you'd see on trade-ins and used cars, and in textbook examples.

- OPEN: I found nothing reliable on how TR-9080 fluid is shared between clutch cooling and gear lubrication (single vs. split circuit). SI or Tremec literature needs a full read.

## Q3. How are clutches and gears actuated (electromechanical vs. hydraulic mechatronic)?

### Takeaway
There are two families. **Electromechanical** units (DPS6, Hyundai/Kia 7DCT) use electric motors with integral position sensors to stroke each clutch, and motors or solenoids to move shift drums or rails and forks. **Hydraulic mechatronic** units (VW DQ250/DQ381 wet, and the DQ200, which is dry but still hydraulic) use a combined TCM and valve body: a pump (the DQ200 has an electric pump and accumulator) plus solenoid/pressure regulators apply the clutches and move fork pistons. Diagnosis differs. Electromechanical faults show up as motor current and position-sensor PIDs and DTCs. Hydraulic mechatronic faults show up as pressure build-up DTCs, leaks and pump run time.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (Ford DPS6): The TCM actuates the clutches through **two individual electrically driven clutch motors and actuator systems**, one per clutch. Each motor contains a **clutch position sensor** that is part of the motor and isn't serviceable separately. **Two brushless DC motors integral to the TCM** turn **two gear-selector drums**, which move the shift forks. The dry clutches are "electronically controlled and mechanically actuated". — [Gears Magazine, Diagnosing the DPS6](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/diagnosing-the-dps6-knowing-the-good-helps-you-identify-the-bad/); [ATRA DPS6 Introduction PDF](https://akpphelp.ru/assets/template/redesign/files/guidelines/6dct250-manual-scheme-photo.pdf); [Scribd DPS6 Intro](https://www.scribd.com/document/325670316/DPS6-Intro)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Ford DPS6): **P0901 (Clutch A actuator control circuit performance)** is described as indicating a bad clutch actuator. The two clutch motors are identical and interchangeable, so they can be swapped to see whether the fault follows the motor. Wrong PIDs for clutch position, TR sensor or speed sensors may not set codes immediately but can cause no-start or no-move. — [Gears Magazine, Diagnosing the DPS6](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/diagnosing-the-dps6-knowing-the-good-helps-you-identify-the-bad/) (single source; confirm swap-test legitimacy and any relearn after a swap in Ford SI)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Hyundai/Kia 7DCT): The **Clutch Actuator** has **two electric servos with built-in position sensors**. The **Gear Actuator** has **two shift motors, two select solenoids, and four sensors**, and moves separate shift rails independently. Both actuators can be serviced without removing the transmission, but the dealer (at the time of writing) did not sell individual motors, sensors or solenoids. — [Gears Magazine, The D7GF1 and D7UF1](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/the-d7gf1-and-d7uf1-a-closer-look-at-hyundai-kia-dcts/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (VW DQ250 wet): The mechatronic unit is the TCM integrated with the valve body. It controls all hydraulic shift functions and clutch engagement. The excerpt says it controls **6 solenoid valves and 2 clutch pressure regulators**. It is described as the DQ250's most commonly replaced major component. — [Eco-Torque, DQ250 mechatronic failures](https://eco-torque.co.uk/blogs/news/vw-audi-02e-dq250-dsg-mechatronic-failures-symptoms-fault-codes-fixes); [Scribd, DSG DQ250 mechatronic overview](https://www.scribd.com/document/377809112/DSG-02E-Romana) (specialist-shop sources; solenoid count needs VW SSP confirmation)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (VW DQ200 dry): The mechatronic hydraulic pump circulates oil through a filter to a **hydraulic accumulator**, which holds pressure while the pump is idle. **P189C** = function restriction due to insufficient pressure build-up. **P17BF** is linked to the same accumulator/housing crack failure. A crack at the accumulator mount causes low pressure and leakage and can trigger pump protection that immobilizes the car. — [Langwieser, P189C & P17BF in DQ200 mechatronics](https://www.langwieser-performance.com/pages/p189c-p17bf-faults-in-dsg-dq200-mechatronics); [Eco-Torque, DQ200 accumulator](https://eco-torque.co.uk/products/0am-0cw-dq200-dsg-7-speed-hydraulic-accumulator-p17bf-p189c-audi-seat-skoda-vw); [OEM VW Shop, 0AM accumulator kit](https://www.oemvwshop.com/0am398025-genuine-0am-dq200-mechatronic-accumulator-body-repair-kit-p17bf-p189c-fault-repair-p2277972/) (parts/specialist sources; DTC definitions consistent across three)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Hyundai 8DCT): A recall on 8-speed DCT vehicles involved an **electric oil pump** circuit-board component detaching because of insufficient soldering at the supplier. The remedy was a TCU software update plus venting the system. — [n-cars.net forum thread on Hyundai 8DCT recall](https://n-cars.net/forums/threads/hyundai-recalls-8-speed-dct-vehicles-including-all-ns-sold-in-the-u-s-market-check-your-vin.8943/page-3); [K5 Owners forum](https://www.k5owners.com/threads/kia-hyundai-recalling-on-8-speed-dct.2391/) (forum-quoted recall text only; recall number not confirmed from NHTSA; OPEN)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (fits your electrical strength): Electromechanical DCT actuators are **position-servo loops**: motor, position sensor, TCM. Diagnose them like any closed-loop actuator. Compare commanded vs. actual position PIDs, check motor current, and ask whether the fault follows the part in a swap. A clutch that won't reach commanded position, or reaches it too slowly, gives a DTC before it gives a symptom. That matches the DPS6 P0901 example.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: In hydraulic mechatronic units, **low pressure affects everything the unit does**: both clutches and all shift pistons. So "every gear plus a pressure DTC" points at the pump, accumulator, housing leak or fluid, not at one clutch. That's the same reasoning as "low pressure in all ranges" in `hydraulic_pressure_testing.md`.
- INFERRED: The DQ200 is the trap case. It's a **dry-clutch** unit with a **hydraulic** mechatronic. Dry/wet describes the clutch environment. Electromechanical/hydraulic describes the actuation. The two are independent.

## Q4. Kiss/touch-point adaptation and relearn: when is it required, and what does it involve?

### Takeaway
The TCM has to know the exact actuator position (or pressure) at which each clutch starts to carry torque: the "kiss point" or "touch point". It learns this continuously and stores it. **After clutch replacement, mechatronic/TCM/actuator replacement or reprogramming, the stored values are wrong or missing, and a scan-tool-guided relearn is mandatory.** Skipping it, or running it with a mechanical problem present (misadjusted actuator rods, forks not engaged), gives harsh or juddery engagement that looks like a hardware fault. Adaptive values sitting at their limits are a diagnostic clue that the TCM has been compensating for a wearing or contaminated clutch.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (patent definition): The touch point is the clutch-actuator stroke position at which power begins to transfer through the clutch. It's conventionally learned in neutral: the TCM lets the input shaft freewheel down and detects the point where its deceleration changes as the clutch starts to transmit torque. — [USPTO 10677298, Method of learning touch point of DCT](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10677298); see also [USPTO 8725372, Method for kiss point adaptation](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8725372)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (Ford DPS6) / A2 task for relearn per `tcm_programming_relearn.md`: For DPS6 clutch replacement on 2012–2016 Focus, the PCM/TCM must be reprogrammed, including **TCM Adaptive Learning** and a **post-repair road test**, with IDS at or above a stated software release. The adaptive learn has **three phases**: (1) adaptive-memory clear and preliminary learn, including the **Clutch Travel Test** that verifies actuator travel range; (2) initial clutch adjustment, which sets pressure-plate/disc clearance and needs the engine running; (3) **Clutch Touch Point Learn**, where the TCM watches engine-torque changes to find the engagement points. The drive cycle teaches start and finish touch points for the shift forks and the clutch actuators. — [Ford TSB PDF (NHTSA mirror), DPS6](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/SB-10092372-5448.pdf); [Scribd, DPS6 Clutch Adaptive Learn Guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/528370112/DPS6-Clutch-Adaptive-Learning); [FORScan forum, DPS6 adaptive learning](https://forscan.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=1672) (IDS release number deliberately omitted; SI)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Ford DPS6): A JustAnswer thread describes a Clutch Travel Test failing after TCM repair. It shows that the learn can fail when the hardware is wrong. — [JustAnswer, Focus clutch travel test fails after TCM repair](https://www.justanswer.com/ford/i1udy-2015-ford-focus-replaced-tcm.html) (anecdotal; illustrative only)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (Hyundai/Kia 7DCT, OEM TSB): The clutch touch-point relearn **must be performed** after replacing or adjusting the Clutch Actuator, replacing the clutch assembly, or replacing the Gear Actuator, so the TCM can set touch points for launch and shift timing. It runs through the factory scan tool (GDS). Preconditions: ignition on/engine off, Park, no DTCs, foot off the brake. There's a **static** phase with the engine off, then a **dynamic** phase with the engine running in Park while the TCM modulates the clutch actuators. After completion, turn the ignition off and wait for the modules to write learned data. The learn can be attempted **once per ignition cycle**. It **may fail if the clutch-actuator control rods are out of adjustment**. The excerpt gives phase and wait durations; they are omitted here (SI). — [Hyundai/Kia TSB PDF (NHTSA mirror), DCT clutch learn](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2020/MC-10185033-0001.pdf); [Transmission Digest, Servicing the Hyundai/Kia DCT](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/servicing-the-hyundai-kia-dual-clutch-transmission-dtc/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (VW DQ200): After every mechatronic installation or replacement, a **basic setting** with a diagnostic tester is required. It relearns clutch kiss points, selector-fork positions, gear-actuator calibration and hydraulic calibration. During installation all actuator push rods must be engaged with their selector forks, and the tester checks actuator positions, fork movement and clutch clearance. — [Langwieser, DQ200 mechatronics installation](https://www.langwieser-performance.com/blogs/news/how-to-properly-install-the-dq200-mechatronics); [Running Roo, DQ200 can't complete basic settings](https://www.runningroo.com.au/why-cant-a-dq200-mechatronic-unit-complete-basic-settings-after-installation/); [Eco-Torque, DQ200 clutch adaptation](https://eco-torque.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-perform-clutch-adaptation-on-the-0am-dq200-dsg-gearbox)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (BMW MDCT via Transmission Digest): A clutch adaptation must follow reprogramming. It teaches each clutch's engagement point and saves it in the control module, and it's needed whenever the clutch or internal components are replaced. — [Transmission Digest, BMW dual-clutch transmission diagnostics](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/bmw-dual-clutch-transmission-diagnostics/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / OEM (GM C8 M1L): After transmission service (for example a fluid flush/filter change), GM's GDS2 **Transmission Service Fast Learn (SFL)** is run to restore shift quality. — [CorvetteForum, DCT hydraulic flush procedure (MDI2/GDS2)](https://www.corvetteforum.com/forums/c8-tech-performance/4811042-dct-hydraulic-flush-procedure-ac-delco-mdi2-and-gm-gds2-4.html) (owner forum; confirm the procedure name and when it applies in GM SI)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (secondary): Adaptive values at or near their limits mean the TCM has been compensating for a degrading clutch. Elevated clutch temperature during testing and abnormal slip during engagement point to clutch hardware or calibration. — [ApexTechNation, Diagnosing DCT shudder and hesitation](https://apextechnation.com/articles/dct-shudder-diagnosis) (low-authority aggregator; principle consistent with the OEM relearn logic above and with TC-auto adapt reasoning in `shift_phases_adapts.md`)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: **The relearn is part of the repair, not an optional add-on.** On a DCT the TCM *is* the clutch adjustment. A new clutch with old or cleared adapts engages at the wrong point, which gives harsh engagement, flare, judder or a no-move. So "just replaced the clutch/mechatronic and it's worse" should make you think "relearn not done, failed, or done with a mechanical preload problem" before "bad part".
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: A relearn that **won't complete** is itself a diagnostic result. OEM texts name mechanical causes: actuator rods out of adjustment (Hyundai), push rods not engaged with forks (VW), a failed travel test (Ford). Fix the mechanical condition. Don't keep re-running the learn.
- INFERRED: This parallels TC-auto adaptive learn (GM "fast learn" / clutch fill adapts) in `shift_phases_adapts.md` and `tcm_programming_relearn.md`. The difference is that on a DCT the learned value is the launch device itself, so a bad adapt affects every drive-away, not just one shift.

## Q5. What causes the classic DCT symptoms (judder, harsh engagement, hesitation, rollback, overheat warnings, mechatronic faults, DPS6 seal leaks)?

### Takeaway
Most DCT drivability complaints come from the launch clutch doing the job a torque converter used to do. Judder and harsh or hesitant take-off come from clutch friction condition (wear, glazing, **oil contamination on dry units**), wrong touch-point adapts, or calibration. Overheat warnings come from prolonged slip in creep, uphill or heavy-load use, especially on dry units. Flashing gear indicator, limp mode or no-drive is more often mechatronic/TCM or an electrical fault than friction. The Ford DPS6's signature failure is **input-shaft seal leakage contaminating the dry clutch**, which gives shudder on light acceleration. Ford's fix moved from cleaning to **replacing the clutch** (plus seals, software and relearn). A separate DPS6 TCM failure causes **loss of drive/neutral default, no-start, or loss of power**.

### Cited Findings

**Ford DPS6 (dry, electromechanical)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (Ford) / A.1 context: Affected **2011–2014 Fiesta and Focus** with DPS6 may show excessive clutch **shudder during light acceleration** because of fluid contamination of the clutch from **leaking transmission input-shaft seals**. — [Ford TSB PDF (NHTSA mirror), 2011–2015 Fiesta / 2012–2016 Focus](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/SB-10092372-5448.pdf); [MOTOR, Navigating the challenges of DPS6](https://www.motor.com/2024/12/navigating-the-challenges-of-dps6-dual-clutch-transmissions/); [FocusFanatics, TSB 13-4-5 intermittent clutch shudder](https://www.focusfanatics.com/threads/tsb-13-4-5-intermittent-transmission-clutch-shudder-dps6-automatic-transmission.314031/) (TSB number from forum thread title; confirm)

**Hill rollback and creep**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (patent): With no torque converter, DCT creep is **synthesized** by TCM-controlled slipping of the launch clutch. - DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (owner/forum-level): Simulated creep may not be enough to stop a roll on a grade. Holding the car on a hill with the throttle causes excessive clutch slip and damage. Hill-start assist holds the brakes until drive-away. Some DCTs don't creep at all. — [Whirlpool forums, DCT driving tips](https://forums.whirlpool.net.au/archive/3z8zmqz3); [CorvetteForum, Will the DCT have torque creep?](https://www.corvetteforum.com/forums/c8-general-discussion/4300497-will-the-dct-have-torque-creep.html); [Santa Cruz Forums, rolls backwards in D](https://www.santacruzforums.com/threads/does-your-vehicle-roll-backwards-while-the-gear-is-in-d.9541/) (forum-level only; principle backed by the patent above)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL — symptom → first suspicion (DCT):
  - **Judder/shudder from a stop or in creep, gone at cruise:** launch-clutch friction (contamination on dry units, glazing or wear), touch-point adapts, calibration. First moves: check TSBs/software, then adapts, then an inspection for leaks and contamination (dry) or fluid condition (wet).
  - **Harsh or bumpy engagement right after a repair:** relearn not done or failed (Q4).
  - **Hesitation from a stop / delayed kickdown:** TCM strategy (clutch-slip protection, wrong pre-selection) or thermal protection. Often software. Only condemn hardware if adapts are at their limits or slip is abnormal.
  - **Rollback on a hill:** usually normal DCT behavior (no hydraulic-coupling creep) or a hill-hold function that isn't active or has an unmet condition. Only a concern if the clutch is slipping under throttle.
  - **Overheat warning + blinking gear indicator:** thermal self-protection from sustained slip. Mostly usage-driven on dry units. Recurrent cases → software TSB or a dragging clutch.
  - **Loss of drive/neutral, flashing gear display, no-start, U-codes:** TCM/mechatronic or power/ground/network. Think electrical first (your strength): the DPS6 TCM program is the textbook example.
  - **All gears affected + pressure DTC (hydraulic units):** pump/accumulator/leak/fluid. **Only odd or only even gears affected:** that clutch or actuator half.
- INFERRED / OEM (Ford DPS6): **A contaminated dry clutch is a two-fault repair.** Replacing the clutch without fixing the input-shaft seal (and the leak source) brings the shudder back. Ford's repair bundle (seals + clutch + software + adaptive learn) reflects that. Contamination found → find the source → replace (don't clean) → reprogram → relearn.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Moisture intrusion into a dry clutch housing is listed by one low-authority source as a judder cause. It's plausible because the housing is vented to air, but I don't have a reliable source.

## Q6. Service differences: gear oil vs. clutch/mechatronic fluid

### Takeaway
There is no "ATF" in the TC-auto sense to check and change across a DCT family. **Dry** DCTs lubricate gears, synchros and bearings with a gear-oil-type fluid, and the clutch itself runs dry. Some (DPS6) use that fluid for the shift mechanism, while the **DQ200** keeps a **separate mechatronic hydraulic oil**. **Wet** DCTs use one fluid for clutches, hydraulics and gears, with specified change intervals and often a filter. The unit-specific fluid spec matters, since the wrong fluid causes judder. Every DCT fluid service is OEM-specific: fill method, fluid spec, and whether a relearn/fast-learn follows. SI governs.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet), corroborated / OEM (Ford DPS6): **Motorcraft Dual Clutch Transmission Fluid (XT-11-QDC, WSS-M2C200-D2)** is formulated for the DPS6 dry dual-clutch transmission as a "dual functioning fluid" that protects gears, synchronizers and bearings **and operates the shift mechanisms**. ## Q7. How does DCT behavior differ from a torque-converter automatic, and how should you reason about DCT vs. TC symptoms on the test?

### Takeaway
A DCT has **no torque converter**. That means no fluid coupling, no torque multiplication, no stator, no TCC, and no stall test in the TC sense. Creep and hill hold are software functions (controlled clutch slip, brake hold), not hydraulic byproducts. So TC-diagnosis tools (stall speed, TCC-apply shudder at cruise, stator freewheel/seize) don't apply. Launch-clutch slip, touch-point adaptation, clutch temperature and actuator position are the DCT equivalents. On the test, first identify the transmission type from the stem (A.1), then use the logic that fits that type.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A.1 on the A2 test covers identifying the transmission type: torque-converter automatic, dual-clutch automatic, CVT, and hybrid/electrified drive. — [ASE Official Automobile Study Guide (PDF)](https://www.ase.com/uploads/ASE_Automobile_Study_Guide_2025.pdf); see also the verified 2026 edition logged in `autotrans/research.md` (task map row A.1)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: DCT creep is simulated through the launch clutch, "simulating the torque converter function". — [USPTO 8260513](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8260513)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (secondary, low authority): TCC shudder typically appears in steady cruise under light load with the tach oscillating. DCT judder appears at take-off and in creep. The Hyundai TSB defines judder on its creep test at very low speed. — [Hyundai 8WDCT judder TSB (NHTSA mirror)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10206957-0001.pdf); [moteursforge, Transmission shudder/judder causes](https://moteursforge.com/en/transmission-shudder-judder-causes/); [Transmission Digest, TCC vibration, judder or shudder misdiagnosed](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/tcc-vibration-judder-or-shudder-misdiagnosed/) (moteursforge gives specific km/h bands; not repeated here as they're not authoritative)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A large share of DCT shudder/drivability complaints are resolved by TCM software updates. Hyundai's own TSB says to apply the TCU update before replacing the transmission. — [Hyundai TSB (NHTSA mirror)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10206957-0001.pdf); [ApexTechNation](https://apextechnation.com/articles/dct-shudder-diagnosis)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL — **DCT vs. TC side-by-side (for reasoning, not memorizing):**

  | Behavior | TC automatic | DCT |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Launch device | Fluid coupling plus stator multiplication | Friction clutch slipped under TCM control |
  | Creep at idle in D | Natural (converter drag) | Synthesized by clutch slip; may be weak or absent; can overheat a dry clutch if held on a grade with throttle |
  | Hill hold | Converter creep plus brakes; rollback is possible but damped | Needs hill-start assist (brake hold); rollback when it drops out is often normal |
  | Low-speed shudder | TCC apply shudder (cruise, light load) or engine misfire | Launch-clutch judder (0–creep speeds): friction, contamination, adapts, software |
  | Stall test | Valid diagnostic (stator, holding members) | Not applicable (no converter). Holding brake + throttle just slips and heats a clutch |
  | Shift mechanism | Clutch-to-clutch or band apply via hydraulics/planetaries | Synchronizer/fork pre-select plus clutch hand-off |
  | Heat source in stop-and-go | Converter slip (fluid heat, handled by cooler) | Clutch slip (dry: poor heat rejection → warnings) |
  | Post-repair learn | Shift adapts / fast learn (quality) | Touch-point learn (can't launch properly without it) |

- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL — test-taking reasoning:
  1. **Read the stem for type clues** (A.1): "dual clutch", "dry clutch", "clutch actuator", "shift fork", "synchronizer", "mechatronic", "touch point", "odd/even" mean DCT. "Torque converter", "stall speed", "TCC", "stator", "planetary", "band" mean TC auto. "Belt/chain", "pulley", "ratio" mean CVT.
  2. **Eliminate answers that use another type's parts.** A DCT stem answered with "stator one-way clutch", "TCC solenoid" or "stall test" is wrong. So is an "ATF change" answer for a dry-clutch judder.
  3. **Launch judder on a DCT → clutch/adapt/software.** Cruise-speed shudder on a TC auto → TCC/fluid/engine. Don't swap them.
  4. **"Just replaced X and now it engages harshly" → relearn.** This holds for both types, but it's almost always the answer on a DCT.
  5. **Electrical symptoms (no-drive, flashing indicator, U-codes) → power/ground/network/TCM first.** Your electrical strength applies directly: DCT actuators are motor-plus-position-sensor loops.
  6. **Contamination on a dry clutch → find and fix the leak source + replace the clutch.** Tech-A/Tech-B pairs that stop at "clean the clutch" or "replace the clutch only" are incomplete.
- INFERRED: Both types share generic A2 electronic logic (DTC types, TCM power/ground, CAN checks, per `area_a_electronic_diagnosis.md`). The type-specific difference is mostly in the launch device and in what "learn" means.
