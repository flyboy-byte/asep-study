## 1. For each B task, what facts/procedures are consistently taught (≥2 sources)?

### Takeaway
Area B reasoning comes down to a few cause-and-effect chains that sources repeat consistently. (1) The TR switch or linkage is out of index, so the module or manual valve sees the wrong position. That gives start-in-gear or no-start in P/N, a wrong PRNDL display, backup-lamp faults, and cross-leak or slip. (2) Wear lets the yoke wobble in its bushing, which makes the extension seal leak. Replacing the seal alone doesn't fix it. (3) A failed cooler or bypass mixes coolant into the ATF or restricts flow. Glycol destroys friction bonding, so the unit must be rebuilt, and the cooler must be flushed and flow-tested after any failure. (4) Valve-body assembly errors cause cross-leaks: uneven torque, a misplaced check ball, or the wrong spacer plate or gasket. (5) Fluid service hinges on the right fluid type, knowing how much fluid each method actually replaces, and checking level at the specified temperature. Overfill and underfill both cause aeration.

### Cited Findings

#### B.1 Manual linkage / cables / TR switch (neutral safety)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1): The neutral safety switch / TR sensor is part of the starter control circuit. It lets the starter run only in Park and Neutral and tells the TCM the selected range. The neutral safety switch "usually has the backup lamp circuit built in." A misadjusted TR sensor can cause harsh shifting or confuse the PCM. — [FreeASEStudyGuides, TR sensor troubleshooting](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-range-sensor.html); [FreeASEStudyGuides, electrical TR sensor test](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/electrical-transmission-range-sensor.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1): A TR sensor that is out of index or has worn splines "commonly causes no crank in Park but cranks in Neutral" (or the reverse). The switch "is loose and needs to be adjusted" is listed as a cause. — [YourMechanic, neutral safety switch](https://www.yourmechanic.com/article/symptoms-of-a-bad-or-failing-neutral-safety-switch); [Samarins, won't start in Park but starts in Neutral](https://www.samarins.com/diagnose/why-car-will-not-start-in-park.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1): Misalignment with the manual shift shaft "happens frequently after a transmission repair or replacement." A shifter cable problem can leave the in-car lever in Park while the lever at the TR switch hasn't moved far enough to confirm Park. — [Gearstar, bad TR sensor symptoms](https://gearstar.com/blog/what-happens-when-a-transmission-range-sensor-goes-bad/); [YourMechanic, TR position sensor](https://www.yourmechanic.com/article/symptoms-of-a-bad-or-failing-transmission-position-sensor-switch)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1/B.10): TR faults show up as a wrong gear indication, shifts at the wrong time or harsh shifts, no crank in Park, limp mode, and DTCs P0705–P0708. — [Gearstar](https://gearstar.com/blog/what-happens-when-a-transmission-range-sensor-goes-bad/); [iCarsoft P0705](https://www.icarsoft-us.com/blogs/news/p0705-code)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1): There are two basic adjustment methods. One is sliding the switch (loosen the mounting bolts and rotate or slide it). The other is aligning index marks or a gauge pin. TR testing uses a high-impedance (10 MΩ) DMM. — [FreeASEStudyGuides TR sensor](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-range-sensor.html); [CarFromJapan NSS adjust](https://carfromjapan.com/article/the-testing-and-adjustment-of-a-neutral-safety-switch/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1): Linkage/cable adjustment method: put the selector in the reference position (usually Neutral or Park), disconnect the cable at the transmission, and put the transmission lever in its matching detent by hand. Then reconnect or adjust until the lever position and detent match. One cited check: select Reverse, move gently to Park, and see whether the transmission arm can still move. If it can, adjust. A maladjusted cable "can allow the manual valve to be held between detents." — search summary blending [DieselPro Allison cable adjustment](https://dieselpro.com/blog/shift-lever-and-cable-adjustment-procedures-for-allison-m-mh-marine-transmissions/), [GMT400 shifter cable adjustment](https://www.gmt400.com/threads/shifter-cable-adjustment.45912/), [FullSizeBronco](https://www.fullsizebronco.com/threads/transmission-shift-cable-adjustment.520281/) (snippet; forum + marine sources, low authority)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1): "If the manual valve is incorrectly adjusted, it will leak line pressure into the wrong circuit." A misadjusted shift cable moves the manual valve so that fluid flows into the wrong circuit. — [FreeASEStudyGuides, manual shift valve leak](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-manual-shift-valve.html) (snippet; only one source states the manual-valve cross-leak mechanism directly)

#### B.2 External seals and gaskets
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.2): Pinpoint the source with UV dye and a black light before replacing anything. Location tells you the source: pan-gasket leaks drip from the pan edge, converter/front-seal leaks show at the bellhousing, and output/extension leaks show at the rear. — [HowStuffWorks, transmission leak](https://auto.howstuffworks.com/under-the-hood/diagnosing-car-problems/mechanical/transmission-fluid-leak.htm); [CarSymp pan vs axle seal vs cooler line](https://carsymp.com/oil-leak-repair/transmission-fluid-leak/leak-at-pan-vs-axle-seal-vs-cooler-line-diagnosis/) (snippet; consumer sites. The "40% of leaks are pan gasket" figure in one result is unsourced, so don't use it)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.2/B.12): Fluid pushed out of the vent or dipstick tube points to overfill, aerated or overheated fluid, or a clogged vent. With a clogged vent, air can't escape as fluid expands, so fluid exits the fill tube. — [FreeASEStudyGuides, leak/vent check](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-leak-check.html); [YourMechanic Q&A, fluid out vent](https://www.yourmechanic.com/question/transmission-fluid-comes-up-out-of-the-transmission-vent-tube-attached-to-the-transmission-dipstick-tube-by-phillip-h)

#### B.3 Driveshaft yoke, axle joints, bushings, seals
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.3/B.2): A worn extension-housing (tailshaft) bushing lets the slip yoke wobble, which makes the seal leak. It can also cause driveshaft vibration. Accelerated bushing wear comes from a binding U-joint, a bent driveshaft, or case/driveline misalignment. — [FreeASEStudyGuides, extension housing bushing](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/automatic-transmission-extension-housing-bushing.html); [7173Mustangs bushing thread](https://7173mustangs.com/threads/transmission-extension-housing-bushing.16363/); [Ford-Trucks extension housing seal/bushing](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/798005-i-need-very-quick-help-re-extension-housing-seal-bushing-tranny-leak.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.3): Inspection: with the yoke in the splines, move it up and down. There should be little play. Also check the yoke's seal surface for pitting or grooving, because a pitted yoke causes premature seal wear and a repeat leak. "Just replacing the seal may not fix the problem." The yoke can wear as well as the bushing. — same sources as above (snippet)

#### B.4 Pans and covers (debris interpretation)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.4): A light layer of fine gray "paste" on the magnet and a little dark friction material are normal wear. Fine ferrous particles should feel like graphite or fine silt between the fingers. — [Ricks Free Auto Repair Advice, metal on magnet](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/metallic-shavings-on-transmission-magnetic/); [FreeASEStudyGuides, pan inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-oil-pan-inspection.html); [St George Transmission, pan debris](https://www.stgeorgetransmission.com/blog/what-is-the-stuff-in-my-transmission-pan)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.4): Red flags include large metal flakes, chunks of clutch material, a burnt smell, heavy sludge, and "glitter." "Large silver flakes or brass chunks signify immediate danger." — [Ricks](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/metallic-shavings-on-transmission-magnetic/); [RxMechanic](https://rxmechanic.com/metal-shavings-in-transmission/); a rebuilder short video [Firepunk TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@firepunk/video/7615238462832119071) that measured a pack losing only .002 in. while the pan had normal friction dust (low-authority format, but a concrete example)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.4): What the metal type points to:
  - **Brass/bronze** = bushings or thrust washers ([St George Transmission](https://www.stgeorgetransmission.com/blog/what-is-the-stuff-in-my-transmission-pan); [RxMechanic](https://rxmechanic.com/metal-shavings-in-transmission/)).
  - **Steel/iron (magnetic)** = gear teeth, bearings or races, shafts ([Ricks](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/metallic-shavings-on-transmission-magnetic/); [FreeASEStudyGuides](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-oil-pan-inspection.html)).
  - **Aluminum (non-magnetic, silvery)** = case, valve body, pump body or pistons ([EngineNeeds, metal shavings in pan](https://engineneeds.com/metal-shavings-in-transmission-oil-pan/); [BenzWorld high-aluminum thread](https://www.benzworld.org/threads/non-ferrous-shavings-in-transmission-filter-high-aluminum-content-in-atf.1782081/)).
  - **Black friction material** = clutches or bands ([FreeASEStudyGuides](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-oil-pan-inspection.html)).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.4): Aluminum and brass are non-magnetic, so the magnet won't catch them. Use a fine strainer to see them. — [EngineNeeds](https://engineneeds.com/metal-shavings-in-transmission-oil-pan/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.4): Casting slag or flash may appear on the first inspection of a new or reman transmission. — [FreeASEStudyGuides pan inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-oil-pan-inspection.html)

#### B.5 Engine cooling vs. transmission cooling; cooler, thermostat/bypass, lines
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): In many vehicles the ATF cooler is a heat exchanger inside a radiator tank. When its wall corrodes or cracks, ATF and coolant mix into a pink, milky "strawberry milkshake." It can show up in the coolant reservoir, on the ATF dipstick, or both. Don't drive the vehicle. — [go-parts Colorado cooler guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/ps-2004-2022-chevrolet-colorado-automatic-transmission-oil-cooler); [Wrangler Forum SMOD](https://www.wranglerforum.com/threads/strawberry-milkshake-transmission-fluid.1409370/); [MDXers cooler failure w/ pics](https://www.mdxers.org/threads/failure-of-transmission-cooler-inside-of-radiator-w-pics.44374/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): Glycol/water in ATF dissolves or delaminates the bonding adhesive of friction material (water flashes to steam at the plate interface). It also attacks the friction-modifier additives, which causes shudder, especially at TCC apply. — [Gears, "ATF and Water: The Never-Ending Problem"](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/atf-and-water-the-never-ending-problem/); [Acustrip/Gears June 2014 coolant contamination PDF](https://www.acustrip.com/res/pdf/CoolantContamination.pdf); [Transmission Digest, flooded 62TE](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/flooded-62te-water-glycol/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): Once coolant is found in ATF, the repair calls for all rubber seals, all composition-faced clutch plates and bands, and the torque converter to be replaced, with the unit thoroughly cleaned and rebuilt. A fluid flush alone is not a repair. — search summary of [Acustrip/Gears PDF](https://www.acustrip.com/res/pdf/CoolantContamination.pdf) and a [2023 GM service bulletin on NHTSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10234619-0001.pdf) (snippet; which source says which cannot be separated. The bulletin's content was not read)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): A thermal bypass valve / transmission thermostat keeps fluid from going to the cooler until the fluid is warm. It recirculates fluid (or sends converter-out oil to lube) during warm-up, then opens to the cooler. — [Sonnax, Understanding Cooler Control Valves (thermal vs. pressure)](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1268-understanding-cooler-control-valves-what-thermal-vs-pressure-control-means-for-your-build); [Mishimoto thermal bypass valve R&D](https://www.mishimoto.com/engineering/2020/01/dodge-ram-67l-cummins-thermal-bypass-valve-rd-pt1); oil-cooler bypass valve patents (e.g., [US 7299994](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7299994))
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): Failure modes:
  - **Stuck in recirculation/cold position** (debris, or the element never "pins out"): no cooler flow, so the transmission and converter overheat, transmission temperature codes can set, and TCC apply can be affected ([Sonnax cooler control valves](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1268-understanding-cooler-control-valves-what-thermal-vs-pressure-control-means-for-your-build); [BITOG 4R70W overheating/bypass](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/4r70w-transmission-overheating-failed-cooler-bypass-valve.302831/)).
  - **Stuck open**: fluid is overcooled and slow to warm, with rough or cold shifts. The 6L80 adapter vendor says the thermostat "reduces rough shifts caused by overcooled transmission fluid" ([Improved Racing 6L/8L/10L cooler adapter](https://www.improvedracing.com/6l-8l-10l-automatic-transmission-cooler-adapter-with-thermostat.html); vendor page).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): A thermal control valve is not the same thing as a cooler pressure-relief/bypass valve. Sonnax gives the example of a Chrysler/Ram radiator-mounted pressure bypass that opens on head pressure (their figure: 55 psi) [Other OEM example / Needs SI]. — [Sonnax cooler control valves](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1268-understanding-cooler-control-valves-what-thermal-vs-pressure-control-means-for-your-build)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): Some vehicles use a coolant-to-ATF **warmer** (heat exchanger) so that warm engine coolant heats the ATF after a cold start. This cuts parasitic loss and cold-start wear and improves fuel economy. — [Google Patents US20160273647A1, ATF warmer coolant circulation](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20160273647A1/en); [CruzeTalk ATF warmer](https://www.cruzetalk.com/threads/atf-warmer.130498/) (snippet; the "1.7%" economy figure in the summary has no traceable source, so don't use it)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): After every overhaul or exchange, and after any failure, the cooler, auxiliary cooler, lines and bypass valve must be cleaned, flushed and back-flushed. Otherwise debris left in the cooler goes back into the new unit. One rebuilder recommends flushing in the flow direction, then reverse, then flow again. — [Transmission Digest, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/avoiding-after-overhaul-problems/); [Saab 9-4X (GM AF40-6) SI mirror, cooler flushing and flow test](https://saabwisonline.com/9-4x/2011/4-transmission/automatic-transmission-af40-6/fault-diagnosis-fault-symptoms/transmission-fluid-cooler-flushing-and-flow-test); [Saturn VUE SI mirror, cooler flushing and flow test](https://gm.saturn.team/Saturn/VUE___FWD/2008/91/10/6875/151537) (titles and summary only)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal / Needs SI] (B.5): The flow test is done with the vehicle at operating temperature: disconnect the return line and time the fluid delivered. One rebuilder rule of thumb for 5/16 in. lines is a minimum of 1 quart in 20 seconds, with most healthy units giving about 1 quart in 10 seconds. Don't judge flow with the flusher, because it pushes harder than the transmission pump, and a cooler can clog again after flushing. — [Transmission Digest, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/avoiding-after-overhaul-problems/) (snippet); an [NHTSA-hosted "Automatic Transmission Oil Cooler Flow Test Essential" bulletin](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2015/MC-10113664-9999.pdf) exists (title only; OEM not confirmed)

#### B.6 Valve body components
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.6): Check balls that are missing or in the wrong place can cause no-shift or no-move. Valve body gaskets that are misaligned or swapped cause slipping or late, hard shifts. An accumulator piston sticking up above the gasket surface is also listed as a cause. — search summary of [Holley/B&M TH-700 (4L60) Transpak instructions](https://documents.holley.com/70235.pdf), [B&M 4L80E Transpak instructions](https://www.catalograck.com/ImgVD/BMA/9500865-00.pdf), [ck5 TH350 missing check balls](https://ck5.com/forums/threads/th350-missing-check-balls-in-valvebody.344394/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.6): Separator (spacer) plates and gaskets are application-specific. A gasket with the wrong or faded print pattern, or a mis-stamped plate, sends pressure into the wrong circuit and causes cross-leaks, delayed engagement, erratic shifts or clutch distress. Compare the new plate or gasket hole-for-hole to the old one. — [FreeASEStudyGuides, valve body separator plates](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/valve-body-separator-plates.html); [Sonnax Valve Body Inspection and Rebuilding](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/172-valve-body-inspection-and-rebuilding); [Cobra Transmission TH400 spacer gasket listing](https://cobratransmission.com/400-3l80-valve-body-to-spacer-plate-gasket-9175-1)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.6/B.9): Inspect solenoid inlet screens and O-rings for clogging or leakage. Low or dirty fluid, a clogged filter or restricted passages are common non-electrical causes of solenoid performance DTCs. Resistance specs vary by unit (the summary gave values from about 5 to 31 Ω across makes) [Needs SI]. Measure with the circuit unpowered. — [OBD-Codes P0752](https://www.obd-codes.com/p0752); [KBB P0758](https://www.kbb.com/obd-ii/p0758/); [Transmission Digest, slow-to-respond solenoid](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/diagnosing-unusual-problem-slow-respond-solenoid/) (title only)

#### B.7 Valve body torque and sequence
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.7): Run all bolts in by hand, then torque in steps from the center outward in a spiral or criss-cross pattern with a calibrated (inch-pound) torque wrench. This gives even gasket compression and prevents warping, and over- or under-tightening causes leaks or malfunction. — search summary blending [JustAnswer Ford valve body torque sequence](https://www.justanswer.com/ford/pmzcu-trying-figure-torque-sequence.html), [Scribd 6R80 VB bolt torque sequence](https://www.scribd.com/document/579374488/6R80-Transmission-Valve-Body-Bolt-Torque-Sequence), [Explorer Forum A4LD VB sequence](https://www.explorerforum.com/forums/threads/a4ld-valve-body-bolt-torque-sequence.501353/) (snippet; Q&A and forum sources. The center-out principle matches general bolted-joint practice, but no textbook quote was found)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Other OEM example / Needs SI] (B.7): Example values appear only in inch-pounds or low N·m (e.g., 6R80: 8 N·m / 71 lb-in per a Scribd copy; Allison MD3060 about 89–106 lb-in per a JustAnswer reply). These show the magnitude only. **Do not quote them as specs.** — [Scribd 6R80](https://www.scribd.com/document/579374488/6R80-Transmission-Valve-Body-Bolt-Torque-Sequence); [JustAnswer Allison](https://www.justanswer.com/medium-and-heavy-truck/ccxw9-torque-spec-sequence-when-reassembling.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.7): "Valve body bolts being loose" is listed alongside swapped gaskets as a cause of slipping. — [Holley/B&M TH-700 instructions](https://documents.holley.com/70235.pdf) (snippet)

#### B.8 Accumulators and servos
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.8): An accumulator piston that bottoms too fast because of a worn seal can produce a **slide-bump** shift: slip, then a pressure spike at the end of stroke. Leaks can be at the inner bore or outer seal or a worn centering pin, and they change shift feel. — [TruckTransDiag, "Accumulators and how to 'fix' shifts" PDF](https://www.trucktransdiag.com/downloads/Accumulators.pdf); [SilveradoSierra 4L60E after rebuild](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/2005-4l60e-after-rebuild-issue.665753/) (snippet)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.8): A leaking servo seal or cover means band apply pressure can't build, which gives band slip or flare. A broken servo retaining clip or cushion-spring retainer can cause harsh/delayed shifts or a 2-3 flare. — [JustAnswer AX4S servo cover leak](https://www.justanswer.com/transmissions/74us3-transmissions-windstar-1999-hi-okay-ax4s-issue.html); [SilveradoSierra 4L60E](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/2005-4l60e-after-rebuild-issue.665753/) (snippet; forum-level)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.8): On many units, band apply clearance is set by a selective servo apply-pin length rather than an adjusting screw. A pin that is too short leaves too much band travel, so the band slips and can eventually break. Rebuilders use longer pins as a fix. — [Sonnax, 4T60-E/4T65-E longer servo pin](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/612-stop-4t60-e-4t65-e-case-wear-band-slippage-breakage-with-a-longer-servo-pin); [Sonnax, TH400/4L80-E low/reverse servo setup](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1371-easy-th400-4l80-e-low-reverse-servo-setup-craft-your-own-tool); [GMT400 4L60E band adjust](https://www.gmt400.com/threads/adjusting-the-band-on-a-4l60e.28174/)

#### B.9 Electrical/electronic components and circuits
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.9): **Fluid wicking.** When a case pass-through connector seal fails, ATF wicks up inside the wiring harness by capillary action and can fill the TCM connector. Check by unplugging the TCM connector and looking for ATF. The repair is usually a case connector, a harness section, and often a TCM. — [BenzWorld, ATF wicking into TCM](https://www.benzworld.org/threads/atf-wicking-into-transmission-control-module.3085611/); [Duramax Forum, fluid from harness plug](https://www.duramaxforum.com/threads/leaking-tranny-fluid-from-wiring-harness-plug-what-is-it.17334/page-2); [JustAnswer GMC 2500HD leak at harness](https://www.justanswer.com/gm/23n3w-leak-harness-that-part-electronic-shifting-control-module.html). The summary names Chrysler 45RFE/545RFE/68RFE as an "extremely common" example [Other OEM example].
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.9): **Fretting corrosion.** Vibration and thermal cycling cause micro-motion that oxidizes terminal contact surfaces and raises resistance. The result is intermittent faults. — [Transmission Digest, "What is Fretting Corrosion?"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/what-is-fretting-corrosion/); [diag.net Terminal Fretting](https://diag.net/msg/m5u8x3pbl0fervpkneyeu36ghi); [Techedom terminal plating fretting](https://techedom.com/terminal-plating-fretting-corrosion-causes-and-prevention/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.9): **Terminal drag (pin-fit) test.** Insert the correct-size mating test probe into the female terminal to check spring tension. A loose drag means poor contact. Don't spread terminals with meter probes. — [GM TechLink Mid-Sept 2020, "Proper Terminal Testing"](https://gm-techlink.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/GM_TechLink_18_Mid-September_2020.pdf) (title + summary); [diag.net Electrical Connector and Terminal Service](https://diag.net/msg/m4gczf77pb9iq1fl5w9bza0pxd). #### B.10 Control module inputs, outputs, data communications
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.10): U0101 means the network master or another module stopped receiving TCM messages. Typical symptoms are several warning lamps, no shifting or a fixed gear (often 2nd or 3rd in the snippets), and limp mode. Common causes are low battery or voltage, wiring (chafed, broken or corroded), the CAN bus, or the TCM. — [OBD-Codes U0101](https://www.obd-codes.com/u0101); [RepairPal U0101](https://repairpal.com/obd-ii-code-u0101-lost-communication-with-tcm); [TroubleCodes.net U0101](https://www.troublecodes.net/ucodes/u0101/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.10/B.1): The TR/range input is safety-critical. Without a valid range input, a TCM may command maximum line pressure (harsh engagements) and default to a limp gear. — [go-parts P1819 / IMS](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p1819) (snippet; one consumer source. The general "limp = fixed gear + max pressure" behavior matches `hydraulic_pressure_testing.md` and `scan_tool_bidirectional.md` in this folder)

#### B.11 Powertrain mounts
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.11): Worn or broken engine/transmission mounts cause a clunk on P→R or D→R engagement, harsh-feeling engagement, and vibration through the floor and shifter. The engine can move under load. — [Mechanic Base, defective transmission mount](https://mechanicbase.com/transmission/transmission-mount-symptoms/); [AutoBuffy transmission mount](https://autobuffy.com/newsroom/transmission-mount-symptoms-cost); [Complete Auto, mounts fail](https://www.completeautore.com/6-problems-that-start-when-the-transmission-and-engine-mounts-fail)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.11/B.1): A mis-positioned transmission loads the shift linkage or cable, the driveshaft and the wiring, so a bad mount "can affect the shifting performance." — [Mechanic Base](https://mechanicbase.com/transmission/transmission-mount-symptoms/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.11): To install and align, loosen all the major mounts or the crossmember, let the powertrain settle to its neutral (centered) position, and then tighten. A mount forced or "wedged" into place transmits vibration. Mis-positioning changes driveline or U-joint angles on RWD and CV-axle angles on FWD. — [ThirdGen driveline vibration after mount replacement](https://www.thirdgen.org/forums/transmissions-drivetrain/681396-driveline-vibration-after-replacing.html); [Audizine motor mount alignment](https://www.audizine.com/threads/motor-mount-alignment-droning-in-cab-after-mount-install.533834/); [Toyota Nation vibration after mounts](https://www.toyotanation.com/threads/vibration-after-new-motor-mounts.328024/) (snippet; forum-level, but the forums agree with each other)

#### B.12 Fluid and filter; level/type with or without dipstick
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): A pan drain/drop replaces only part of the fluid, because fluid stays in the converter, cooler, lines and valve body. Estimates vary: "about 30–50%" in one source and "one-quarter to one-third" in another. A cooler-line exchange machine replaces most or all of the fluid (about 90–100%). — [K&K Richardson, flush vs drain-and-fill](https://www.kkrichardson.com/kwik-blog/transmission-flush-vs-drain-and-fill); [BITOG, fluid remaining after drain and fill](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/post-how-much-fluid-remains-in-trany-torque-converter-during-a-drain-and-fill.326593/). **Conflict:** the percentages differ by source and by design, so a guide should say "a fraction, often roughly a third to a half" [Needs SI for capacity by unit].
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): ATF, CVT fluid and DCT fluid are not interchangeable. CVT fluid is engineered for steel belt/chain-to-pulley friction under high clamp force. ATF in a CVT gives belt slip, shudder, whine or RPM flare, and pulley damage. DCT fluid is for clutch-engagement friction in dual-clutch units. — [Valvoline, CVT Fluid 101](https://shop.valvolineglobal.com/blog/cvt-fluid-101-why-a-cvt-transmission-uses-different-fluid); [Atlantic Lubes ATF vs CVT vs DCT](https://atlanticlubes.com/blog/atf-vs-cvt-vs-dct-fluids-understanding-modern-transmission-fluids/); [CVTExpert, ATF in a CVT](https://cvtexpert.com/what-happens-if-you-put-atf-in-a-cvt-transmission/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): ATF specs (Dexron, Mercon LV/ULV, ATF+4) differ in friction-modifier packages and viscosity. The fluid is the hydraulic medium, the lubricant, the clutch friction modifier and the coolant all at once, so the wrong fluid changes clutch engagement. ATF+4 in its applications is linked with preventing TCC shudder. Using Dexron/Mercon in an ATF+4 unit is reported to cause shudder. — [Valvoline DEXRON VI/MERCON LV product page](https://shop.valvolineglobal.com/products/dexron-vi-mercon-lv-atf-full-synthetic-automatic-transmission-fluid); [Mobil ATF+4 product page](https://www.mobil.com/en/lubricants/for-personal-vehicles/our-products/products/mobil-atf4) ("not recommended for vehicles that require Ford Mercon or GM Dexron"); [BITOG ATF+4 vs Dexron VI](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/atf-4-vs-dexron-vi.235335/); [Lubegard OEM ATF conversion chart](https://www.lubegard.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/12th-Edition-OEM-ATF-Conversion-Chart.pdf)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12/B.2): **Overfill.** Rotating parts churn the fluid into foam (aeration). Aerated fluid can't hold steady pressure, which gives erratic or soft or harsh shifts, clutch slip, overheating (foam is a poor coolant), and fluid pushed out the vent or dipstick tube. — [FreeASEStudyGuides leak/vent check](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-leak-check.html); [CarFromJapan overfill](https://carfromjapan.com/article/problems-face-much-transmission-fluid/); [Sun Transmissions overfill](https://suntransmissions.com/blogs/news/more-is-not-better-why-overfilling-your-transmission-can-be-fatal)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): **Underfill.** The pump draws air at the pickup, which also aerates the fluid. Result: slipping, delayed engagement, pump noise, and overheating. "Aeration … results from low or high fluid levels." — [YourMechanic Q&A, fluid out vent](https://www.yourmechanic.com/question/transmission-fluid-comes-up-out-of-the-transmission-vent-tube-attached-to-the-transmission-dipstick-tube-by-phillip-h); [CarFromJapan bubbles](https://carfromjapan.com/article/bubbles-in-transmission-fluid/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): **No-dipstick check, general.** Vehicle level, engine idling, and fluid temperature read from the scan tool (or a probe thermometer if no scan tool) inside the SI window. Shift through the ranges and return to Park. Remove the check or level plug and add fluid until it just drips or runs from the level opening. Checking outside the temperature window gives a wrong level because ATF expands a lot with heat. One source warns that checking hot makes expanded fluid run out, which leaves the unit low once cooled. — [O'Reilly, check fluid without a dipstick](https://www.oreillyauto.com/how-to-hub/how-to-check-transmission-fluid-without-a-dipstick); [AutoZone, check fluid without dipstick](https://www.autozone.com/diy/transmission/how-to-check-transmission-fluid-without-a-dipstick); [Ricks, no dipstick](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/check-transmission-fluid-level-without-a-dipstick)

### Inferences
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.1): "Crank in gear" (switch indexed toward Park/Neutral) and "no crank in one of P/N" (indexed away) are the same fault seen from opposite directions. Backup lamps often share the switch, so wrong-range backup-lamp operation points to the switch or its adjustment, not the bulbs. Before replacing a switch, verify linkage adjustment, because a mis-set cable produces the same symptoms.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.1/B.6): If the manual valve sits between detents, it partly opens one circuit and partly exhausts or cross-feeds another. That lowers apply pressure on the holding clutch, which slips and burns. It explains why sources list linkage adjustment under both drivability and "burned clutch" causes. The only direct statement of this found is the FreeASEStudyGuides snippet, so treat it as INFERRED beyond that source.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.5): Because glycol damages friction material and seals, a milky pink fluid finding means rebuild or replace plus cooler replacement. The combination "replace radiator, flush ATF, done" is the wrong answer pattern. Exam distractors are likely to offer "flush and refill" as the fix.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.5): The thermal bypass valve works the opposite way from an engine thermostat in one respect. An engine thermostat stuck open gives slow warm-up. A transmission bypass stuck in the cold/recirc position gives overheating, because the cooler never gets flow. The questions can therefore hinge on which position it stuck in.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.7): Torquing out of sequence or unevenly can distort a thin aluminum casting and spacer plate. The resulting cross-leaks produce slips and bad shifts that look like hard-part failures. So after a valve body job, the first thing to suspect for a new slip is the job itself: sequence, torque, check-ball placement, gasket ID.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.12): Overfill and underfill both cause aeration, so both can present as "slip + erratic shift + overheat." The tie-breakers are fluid out the vent or foamy fluid after a service (overfill) vs. delayed engagement or whine, worse on turns or when cold (underfill).

## 3. Common traps?

### Takeaway
Area B traps usually offer a plausible parts-replacement answer that skips the root cause. Examples: replacing a TR switch without checking linkage, or replacing a seal without the bushing or yoke. Others: flushing coolant-contaminated fluid instead of rebuilding, reusing a cooler without flushing and flow-testing, checking a no-dipstick unit at the wrong temperature, or topping up with "universal" fluid. Treat magnet paste and light friction dust as abnormal and you've fallen for the pan trap.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.1): TR misalignment "happens frequently after a transmission repair or replacement." A cable problem alone can prevent Park confirmation. So a new switch doesn't fix a linkage fault. — [Gearstar](https://gearstar.com/blog/what-happens-when-a-transmission-range-sensor-goes-bad/); [YourMechanic TR sensor](https://www.yourmechanic.com/article/symptoms-of-a-bad-or-failing-transmission-position-sensor-switch)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.3): "Just replacing the seal may not fix the problem." Check the bushing and the yoke seal surface. — [FreeASEStudyGuides extension housing bushing](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/automatic-transmission-extension-housing-bushing.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.4): Fine metallic paste on the magnet and light friction material are normal. Only chunks, large flakes, brass pieces or glitter are red flags. — [Ricks](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/metallic-shavings-on-transmission-magnetic/); [FreeASEStudyGuides pan inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-oil-pan-inspection.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.4): The magnet won't show aluminum or brass. A clean magnet doesn't rule out non-ferrous wear. — [EngineNeeds](https://engineneeds.com/metal-shavings-in-transmission-oil-pan/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): Coolant contamination is not fixed by a flush. Friction material, seals and the converter are compromised. — [Gears ATF and Water](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/atf-and-water-the-never-ending-problem/); [Acustrip/Gears PDF](https://www.acustrip.com/res/pdf/CoolantContamination.pdf)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): Don't judge cooler flow by the flusher. Flow-test with the transmission's own pump at operating temperature. — [Transmission Digest, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/avoiding-after-overhaul-problems/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.5): A thermal (temperature) cooler control valve is not the same as a pressure bypass/relief valve. Don't confuse their failure logic. — [Sonnax cooler control valves](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1268-understanding-cooler-control-valves-what-thermal-vs-pressure-control-means-for-your-build)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): Checking a no-dipstick unit hot lets expanded fluid run out of the check hole, so the unit ends up low when cool. Temperature must be in the SI window. — [Ricks, no dipstick](https://ricksfreeautorepairadvice.com/check-transmission-fluid-level-without-a-dipstick); [Gears AF50](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/gms-af50-8-speed-transmission-fluid-level-and-condition-check/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): "Universal" or multi-vehicle substitution is risky. ATF+4 applications shudder with Dexron/Mercon. ATF in a CVT destroys the belt and pulleys. LV in a ULV unit changes shift feel. — [Mobil ATF+4](https://www.mobil.com/en/lubricants/for-personal-vehicles/our-products/products/mobil-atf4); [Valvoline CVT Fluid 101](https://shop.valvolineglobal.com/blog/cvt-fluid-101-why-a-cvt-transmission-uses-different-fluid); [F150Forum LV vs ULV](https://www.f150forum.com/f129/anyone-tried-lv-transmission-fluid-instead-ulv-10r80-554849/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): A pan drop replaces only part of the fluid, not "all" of it. — [K&K Richardson](https://www.kkrichardson.com/kwik-blog/transmission-flush-vs-drain-and-fill)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.12): Overfill can cause slipping and erratic shifts, so "slip = low fluid, add fluid" is not automatically right. Check level correctly first. — [FreeASEStudyGuides leak/vent](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transmission-leak-check.html); [Sun Transmissions overfill](https://suntransmissions.com/blogs/news/more-is-not-better-why-overfilling-your-transmission-can-be-fatal)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [GM-specific] (B.10): P1810 (TFP switch) is not the IMS code. The IMS codes are P1825/P1819. — [go-parts P1810](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p1810); [go-parts P1819](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p1819)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.11): New mounts installed "wedged" or not centered can create vibration. Loosen all of them and let the powertrain settle before torquing. — [ThirdGen](https://www.thirdgen.org/forums/transmissions-drivetrain/681396-driveline-vibration-after-replacing.html); [Audizine](https://www.audizine.com/threads/motor-mount-alignment-droning-in-cab-after-mount-install.533834/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) [Universal] (B.9): When ATF is found in a TCM connector, replacing only the TCM leaves the leaking pass-through seal and a harness still full of fluid. ### Inferences
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.1/B.11): A worn mount can pull on a shift cable and change the TR/manual-valve index under load. So an intermittent wrong-gear display or clunk that appears only on engagement may be a mount problem, not a switch problem. This comes from the Mechanic Base linkage-tension statement and the TR-index sources, but no single source states the chain.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.2/B.12): Fluid out the vent right after a service is overfill until proven otherwise. The same symptom with a correct level points to a clogged vent or overheating.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.6/B.7): For "slips/erratic after valve body service" questions, the likely right answers are assembly errors: sequence or torque, check-ball placement, gasket/plate ID, or an accumulator piston proud of the surface. They are more likely than a new hard-part failure.
- INFERRED [Universal] (B.8): Slide-bump and flare both look like "slip." Slide-bump points to accumulator stroke/seal problems. Flare between gears with a band-applied range points to servo seal or pin length. Harsh points to a stuck, bottomed or broken-spring accumulator, or high pressure.
- INFERRED (test strategy): Area B has the most questions per task of any area, so the reliable scoring pattern is "verify the cheap root cause before the expensive part." That means linkage before switch, fluid level/type/temperature before internal repair, bushing and yoke before seal, and pass-through before TCM.
