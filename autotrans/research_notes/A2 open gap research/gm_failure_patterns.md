Researched 2026-09-30. Access date for every URL below: 2026-09-30.

OPEN = not resolved. **GM** = GM-specific fact, which ASE will not test by name. **UNIVERSAL** = a principle that holds across stepped automatics. **needs-SI** = spec, procedure or applicability must be confirmed in the vehicle's Service Information or the current bulletin before use. **ANECDOTAL** = forum or owner report, used only as corroboration.

## Q1. 4L60-E family (4L60-E/4L65-E/4L70-E): sun shell, 3-4 clutch burn, stator-support bushing/TCC, low/reverse, internal mode switch

### Takeaway
The 4L60-E is the classic "shared element" teaching unit. A broken reaction sun shell takes out exactly the ranges that route through it: 2nd, 4th and Reverse. 1st and 3rd remain. Burnt 3-4 clutches usually come from **accumulated small leaks** (boost valve/sleeve, pump/stator bushings, sealing rings, a #7 check ball), not one bad part. Worn stator-support bushings leak TCC apply oil and set TCC slip codes, and the leak gets worse when the fluid is hot and thin. All three are applications of "which apply circuit or element is common to every failed range?" and "a leak that grows with temperature is a clearance leak."

### Cited Findings

**1a. Reaction sun shell (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Symptom. When the sun shell fails (cracked hub or stripped splines), the unit loses **2nd, 4th (OD) and Reverse**. — [gmt400 4L60E troubleshooting thread](https://www.gmt400.com/threads/look-at-what-i-found-4l60e-trouble-shooting-guide-sticky.10848/); [Blazer Forum](https://blazerforum.com/forum/engine-transmission-35/sun-shell-sun-gear-59841/); [YouTube short "700R4 4L60E No Reverse, 2nd or 4th - Sun Shell Failure"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/v8SbNW3Br_A) (ANECDOTAL / rebuilder videos; consistent with each other)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Failure form. "Most common, the spline area is cracked", and the splined section cracks off the shell and stays attached to the sun gear. Factory shells are described as fatiguing. The aftermarket fix is a heavy-duty shell. — [ck5 "4L60E Common Problems"](https://ck5.com/forums/threads/4l60e-common-problems-and-some-700r4s-too.328319/); [pirate4x4](https://www.pirate4x4.com/threads/4l60e-no-reverse.700664/) (ANECDOTAL; excerpt attribution between these two uncertain)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, differential diagnosis: No 2nd and no 4th **with Reverse OK** points instead to the 2-4 band, servo seals or band wear. No 3rd/4th with Reverse OK points to the 3-4 clutch pack. "The sunshell is probably fine because reverse is still functional." — [astrosafari diagnosis chart](https://www.astrosafari.com/threads/diagnosis-chart-for-common-4l60e-problems.5956/); [trailvoy](https://www.trailvoy.com/threads/confusing-transmission-symptoms-possible-sunshell-or-2-4-band-problem.97105/) (ANECDOTAL)

**1b. 3-4 clutch burn (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Root cause is cumulative leakage. "Even small leaks at a few junctions along the way can add up to a big enough pressure loss to allow slipping." Named leak points: the pump **boost valve sleeve moving and wearing its bore** (fix: O-ringed sleeve); PR-valve bore wear; **pump and stator bushings wearing so the shaft no longer runs true and the input-shaft sealing rings stop sealing**; and leakage at the hidden **#7 check ball** (3rd accumulator), which is "a direct cause of burnt 3-4 clutches." — [Sonnax, "All Over the Map: Attacking 4L60-E Burnt 3-4 Clutches with Confidence"](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/659-all-over-the-map-attacking-4l60-e-burnt-3-4-clutches-with-confidence); republished in [Transmission Digest](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/all-over-the-map/) (Brian Wing, 2018-06-01)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet): There may be one failed part or a combination of leaks, so "the repair that worked last time won't necessarily work this time." A rebuild can also create new failures, for example a pump cover cracked during stator shaft installation or a damaged input shaft seal. — [Transmission Digest, "All Over the Map"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/all-over-the-map/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Line pressure that is low or doesn't rise, plus a 2-3 flare, reported together on a 4L60E. — [silveradosierra thread](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/4l60e-2-3-flare-and-low-no-rise-pressure.760837/) (ANECDOTAL; corroborates the pressure→flare→burn chain)

**1c. Stator-support (pump) bushing and TCC slip (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: The pump/stator bushings "keep the shaft running true and seal TCC-apply oil." A little wear becomes "a much larger leak when the fluid is hot and thin." A TCC-apply circuit leak "usually at the rear pump-stator bushings", or at the turbine-shaft TCC O-ring, can set **P0741** (TCC performance/stuck off). The older GM slip code **P1870** is tied to the same leak. — [Sonnax, "Beyond 1870: GM Converter Codes P0741, P0742 and 1887"](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/120-beyond-1870-gm-converter-codes-p0741-p0742-and-1887); [Motor, "Deciphering GM Transmission DTC P1870"](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/deciphering-gm-transmission-dtc-p1870-june-2006/) (excerpt attribution between these two uncertain)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / ANECDOTAL: After a new rear stator bushing plus a new turbine shaft/input drum, P1870 was gone and TCC slip was back to acceptable at lockup. — [batauto "4l60e stator bushings and p1870 slip code"](https://batauto.com/threads/4l60e-stator-bushings-and-p1870-slip-code.13618/)

**1d. Low/Reverse (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Loss of Reverse **with 2nd and 4th still working** points to the low/reverse clutch or piston, or the reverse input clutch or piston (not the sun shell). The reverse input drum is fed through the sealing rings on the rear of the pump. The low roller clutch rarely fails; one rebuilder reported fewer than 10 failures in more than 2,000 teardowns. — [tahoeyukonforum](https://www.tahoeyukonforum.com/threads/4l60e-no-reverse-wear-gloves-to-handle-this-post.139957/); [hotrodders](https://www.hotrodders.com/threads/4l60e-low-reverse-clutch.224896/); [ls1tech](https://ls1tech.com/forums/automatic-transmission/1977125-4l60e-low-reverse-clutch.html) (ANECDOTAL; excerpts merged across threads)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, ANECDOTAL: Cross-leaks can partially apply the L/R piston while the vehicle is driving forward. Posters attribute to the ATSG manual a note that some early 60-Es had a manual valve that failed to exhaust the L/R piston. — same threads above. **Unverified second-hand ATSG citation; OPEN.**

**1e. Internal mode switch (IMS) (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: The IMS is a sliding-contact switch on the valve body. It sends 4 inputs to the TCM that report manual-shaft position. The code is **P1825** (IMS invalid/illegal range). The generic TR-sensor circuit code is P0705. — [autocodes P1825](https://www.autocodes.com/p1825.html); [engine-codes P1825](https://www.engine-codes.com/p1825.html); [camaroz28 4L60E tech thread](https://www.camaroz28.com/forums/drivetrain-44/4l60e-4l65e-4l70e-tech-thread-codes-diagrams-how-toos-etc-687398/page2/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Symptoms: no movement from a stop except in manual low, or a 2nd-gear start; normal upshifts once moving; PRNDL display blank, flashing or wrong; starting in gear if the switch falsely reports Park. — [trnw 4L60E page](https://www.trnw.net/z-search/4L60E.php); [camaroz28 thread](https://www.camaroz28.com/forums/drivetrain-44/4l60e-4l65e-4l70e-tech-thread-codes-diagrams-how-toos-etc-687398/page2/) (the "starts in gear" line comes from a merged excerpt with unclear attribution; treat as ANECDOTAL)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 1a/1d, tasks **A.8, A.14, C.13**): **Build a table of working and failed ranges, then find the one element or circuit common to every failed range and absent from every working one.** No 2/4/R with 1/3 OK means a shared reaction member. No R alone means a reverse-only element. No 2/4 with R OK means the holding device for 2/4 only. This is the same clutch-chart logic ASE uses on any unit.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 1b, tasks **A.11, A.17, C.8, C.11, C.19**): **A burnt clutch is a result, not a root cause.** Before you replace the frictions, check every leak point in that clutch's apply path: the pressure source, regulator and boost valve, sealing rings, bushings (shaft run-out), check balls and accumulator, and the piston seals. A pressure test that is low only in the affected range, or only when hot, points to a leak in that range's circuit rather than a pump problem.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 1c, tasks **A.13, A.18, C.11, C.12**): **A TCC slip code means the lockup is not holding. It does not mean the converter is bad.** A worn bushing, sealing ring or O-ring in the apply circuit bleeds apply oil, and hot, thin fluid leaks more through the same gap. If the code appears only hot, suspect clearance leaks. Replacing the converter without fixing the circuit is a likely comeback.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 1e, tasks **A.28, B.1, A.19**): **The TCM decides what gear to command from the range input.** A bad range switch can look like a mechanical failure (no movement, wrong start gear) even though the hard parts are fine. Check range-input PIDs against the selector position in every range before you condemn clutches.

## Q2. 6L80/6L90: TCC shudder, 3-5-R / P0776, TEHCM/valve body, thermal bypass valve bulletin, drainback/delayed engagement

### Takeaway
The 6L80 family teaches four A2 lessons. **(a)** A pressure-control-solenoid "performance/stuck off" code (P0776) often points to a **mechanical leak downstream of the solenoid**: a cracked clutch housing, worn boost-valve bores or leaking seal rings. It does not have to mean a bad solenoid. **(b)** An integrated in-pan TCM (TEHCM) is exposed to heat and vibration and is a known failure point. **(c)** Many "overheating/shudder/harsh" complaints are **cooler-circuit** problems (twisted line, stuck thermal bypass valve). **(d)** Delayed engagement after sitting overnight is **converter drainback**, confirmed by timing how long turbine speed takes to match engine speed.

### Cited Findings

**2a. 3-5-R / P0776 and cracked housing (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: P0776 (clutch/pressure-control solenoid "stuck off") on the 6L80 can come from anything in the 3rd/5th/Reverse apply chain, "including TEHCM, valve body (individual valve trains), and pump stator sealing." — [Sonnax 6L80 unit page](https://www.sonnax.com/units/301-6l80); [silveradosierra 6L80 information thread](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/gm-6l80-transmission-information-thread.753826/) (attribution between these uncertain)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Three clutch boost valves in the upper valve body wear their bores and cause flare shifts. The fix is either a new valve body or reaming the bores and fitting oversized valves in all three locations. — same sources
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, bulletin: **09-07-30-004** (later revisions): slips in Reverse or 3rd, delayed R/D engagement, harsh 2-3, and DTCs **P0776, P2715, P2723**, caused by a **cracked 1-2-3-4 / 3-5-R clutch housing**. The repair is to inspect the housing and the pump seal rings. The applicability range quoted was 2006–2009 Cadillac and 2006–2008 other vehicles. A related **09-07-30-004A** revision covered flare/harsh 2-3 shifts from leaking 1-2-3-4 and 3-5-R seal rings. — [silveradosierra "wont shift into 3rd"](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/tranny-problems-wont-shift-into-3rd.94513/) (bulletin text quoted in forum); also named in the first 6L80 search summary alongside [Transmission Digest, "GM 6L80 Transmission Early Failure Issues & Fixes"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/gm-6l80-transmission-early-failure-issues-fixes/). **needs-SI**: the revision letters and dates in the excerpts disagree (one gives -004J, another -004 dated 2012-05-10, and -004A dated 2009-04-21). Confirm the current revision in SI.

**2b. TEHCM (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: The TEHCM combines the TCM, pressure-control solenoids and pressure switches, and mounts on the valve body inside the pan. There it is "exposed to high heat and vibration, leading to a higher failure rate compared to external modules." Common TEHCM faults include cracked pressure-switch membranes, which cause erratic shift timing with codes such as P0877 or P2715. — [Monster Transmission, "6L80/6L90 TEHCM Guide"](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/6l80-6l90-tehcm-guide-common-failures-year-differences-tag-id-reference) (vendor blog); corroborated by [Sonnax pressure switch rebuild kit](https://www.sonnax.com/parts/5414-pressure-switch-rebuild-kit) and remanufactured-TEHCM instruction sheets ([Summit PDF](https://static.summitracing.com/global/images/instructions/sox-gm6ltehcmc6.pdf))
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: The 6L80 IMS is part of the TEHCM assembly. It reports **P182E** (invalid range) or P1915 when it fails. — [Monster Transmission TEHCM guide](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/6l80-6l90-tehcm-guide-common-failures-year-differences-tag-id-reference) (single vendor source; needs-SI)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: Separating a control fault from a hard-parts fault is hard on this unit because its circuits can't be back-probed. Rebuilders compare mainline pressure against the commanded value instead. — [Gears, "6L80/6L90: Issues That Could Drive You Crazy"](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/6l80-6l90-issues-that-could-drive-you-crazy/)

**2c. TCC shudder / converter and pump (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: 6L80s come into shops "mainly because of a failing torque converter", and the pump and stator usually need repair or replacement as well. Signs of pump or stator wear: low pressure, delayed engagement especially when hot, or a "shaky" gauge needle at idle. — [Gears, "6L80/6L90: Issues That Could Drive You Crazy"](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/6l80-6l90-issues-that-could-drive-you-crazy/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: For a shudder confirmed on the TCC slip-speed PID (or one that goes away when the TCC is commanded with the scan tool), GM bulletins direct checking fluid condition, and possibly a TCM reprogram or a DEXRON-VI drain and fill. — search summary citing [NHTSA-hosted GM bulletin MC-10198840](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2021/MC-10198840-9999.pdf) and [PIP5608H](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2021/MC-10202384-9999.pdf) (full text not readable; which transmission each covers is **OPEN**)

**2d. Thermal bypass valve / cooler bulletin (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, bulletin: **22-NA-182** (Aug 2023 revision per excerpt) covers 6L80, 6L90, 8L90, 10L80, 10L90 and 10L1000 vehicles with harsh shifts, shudder, surge, stall, neutral/flare, overheating or high transmission fluid temperature. The causes it names are a **twisted cooler line** (low-mileage vehicles) and, if the lines are OK, the **thermal bypass valve (TBV)**. On the 6-speeds the TBV is external and "opens at 158°F (70°C)." It also calls for a fluid-level check. — search summary citing [NHTSA-hosted GM bulletin MC-10242240](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10242240-0001.pdf); corroborated by [silveradosierra "New GM TSB for Thermal Bypass Valve"](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/new-gm-tsb-for-thermal-bypass-valve.742585/) and [tahoeyukonforum "Techlink for the 6L80 thermal bypass valve"](https://www.tahoeyukonforum.com/threads/techlink-for-the-6l80-thermal-bypass-valve.130931/). needs-SI for the opening temperature on a given vehicle.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / ANECDOTAL: A plastic cap on the TBV thermostat plunger can break and stick the valve closed, which causes overheating. — [silveradosierra "Thermal valve bypass update"](https://www.silveradosierra.com/threads/thermal-valve-bypass-update.744351/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / ANECDOTAL: A forum thread title references a bulletin **21-NA-199** for 6L80E thermostatic bypass valve replacement. — [gm-trucks](https://www.gm-trucks.com/forums/topic/256594-6l80e-thermostatic-bypass-valve-replacement-and-bulletin-21-na-199/). **Title only; content OPEN; verify in SI before citing.**

**2e. Drainback / delayed engagement (GM)**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, bulletin: **16-NA-014** (Nov 2016): delayed P→R or P→D engagement after the vehicle sits engine-off for several hours, typically overnight. Cause: fluid drains from the converter back to the sump, and the pump has to refill the converter before a clutch can apply. Confirmation: drain-down is present if more than **2 seconds** pass between engine speed ramping up and **transmission input shaft speed equaling engine speed**. "DO NOT replace the stator shaft support on vehicles taking 2 seconds or less." — [NHTSA-hosted GM bulletin 16-NA-014](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10112282-9999.pdf) (title and excerpt); mechanism corroborated by [Transmission Digest, "GM 6L80 Early Failure Issues & Fixes"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/gm-6l80-transmission-early-failure-issues-fixes/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: An older 6L80 bulletin addressed delayed P→D/P→R engagement with a **TCM reprogram**. — [corvetteactioncenter bulletin index title](https://www.corvetteactioncenter.com/tech/knowledgebase/article/2006-corvette-service-bulletin-6l80-automatic-transmission-delayed-engagement-from-park-to-drive-or-park-to-reverse-reprogram-transmission-control-module-tcm-275.html) (number not visible)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, rebuilder: One drainback case traced to pump **rotor thickness**, with a new rotor at .708 in. and the vehicle's at .699 in. The advice was to measure rotors even when they look good. — [Gears, "What a Drip! 6L80 Drain Back, That Is"](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/what-a-drip-6l80-drain-back-that-is/). A separate GM bulletin (number not visible) linked **loose stator-support-to-pump-cover bolts** and gasket leaks to delayed Drive engagement, launch shudder or slipping. — search summary of NHTSA-hosted GM bulletins, e.g. [MC-10137951](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10137951-9999.pdf) (number and applicability OPEN)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 2a, tasks **A.17, A.19, A.24, C.11**): **A solenoid "performance" or "stuck off" code only means the TCM saw the expected result fail to happen** (a pressure switch or ratio did not change). The cause can be anywhere from the solenoid to the clutch piston: the valve, bore wear, seal rings or a cracked drum. Check the solenoid electrically first, since that is cheap and fast. If it passes, the fault is hydraulic or mechanical in that circuit, and the other ranges that share the circuit narrow it down.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 2b, tasks **A.25, A.19, C.6**): **An in-pan or integrated control module is exposed to heat, vibration and conductive debris, and it can't be back-probed.** Diagnosis relies more on scan data (commanded versus actual, pressure-switch states) and on a pressure gauge. Replacing it usually needs programming and a relearn (see Q7).
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 2d, tasks **B.5, C.4, A.10, A.22**): **Before blaming the transmission, verify the cooler circuit: line routing (kinks and twists), bypass/thermostatic valve operation, and fluid level.** A stuck-closed bypass valve or kinked line causes high fluid temperature, and that in turn causes shudder, harsh shifts and slips. This maps directly to the ASE sample item on cooler and bypass valves noted in `research.md`.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, 2e, tasks **A.8, A.24, A.12**): **Delayed engagement after an overnight soak, with normal engagement later in the day, points to drainback** (converter or pump oil draining to the sump). Delayed engagement that gets worse as the fluid warms points to internal leakage (pump, stator or sealing rings). The scan-tool test is the time for input/turbine speed to reach engine speed (or, where the ISS reads a clutch drum, to drop to zero). A timed PID is objective evidence, better than "it feels slow."

## Q3. 6T40/45 and 6T70/75: 3-5-R wave plate, harsh shifts

### Takeaway
The 3-5-R **wave plate breaks at a tooth corner** (a stress point), and the pieces damage the 3-5-R housing splines. The TCM sees this as pressure-control-solenoid performance codes (P0776/P0777/P2714/P2715/P2723) and harsh shifts. That is the same "solenoid code, mechanical cause" lesson as the 6L80. Separately, many 6T70 harsh-shift complaints were fixed by **TCM calibration updates**, with no parts replaced.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: The 3-5-R waved plate breaks in 6T40/45/50 and 6T70/75 units, especially 2007–2009, "though it's not uncommon in later units." Broken pieces damage the 3-5-R housing splines, and **P0776, P0777, P2723, P2714 and P2715** may be stored. The OE plates were "insufficiently stress relieved," and all the broken plates studied "separated exactly at a tooth corner." GM's change was to **shot-peen** the plates, but updated (including Gen 2) plates are still reported failing. — [Sonnax, "Protect Against 6T40 & 6T70 3-5-R Failures"](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/764-protect-against-6t40-6t70-3-5-r-failures-salvage-damaged-drums-with-sonnax-drum-saver-kits); [Sonnax Drum Saver article](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/765-gm-6t40-6t70-drum-saver-kits-unbeatable-protection-against-3-5-r-failures-comebacks). Corroborated by [Transmission Digest, "Pattern Failure"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/pattern-failure/) and owner reports ([acadiaforum](https://www.acadiaforum.net/threads/warning-2007-2009-acadia-owners-re-3-5-r-wave-plate-failure-in-transmission.12424/page-4), [saturnfans](http://www.saturnfans.com/forums/showthread.php?t=211967), ANECDOTAL)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, bulletin: **PI1131** (Dec 2013 per excerpt) covers 2011 Cruze and Malibu with the 6T40/6T45 and lists DTCs **P0776, P0796, P0878, P0873, P2714 and P2723**. Hard shifts and/or P0776 can come from a broken 3-5-R wave plate. Low line pressure from a damaged pump is also named as a cause. — [NHTSA-hosted bulletin PI1131](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2013/SB-10073521-5448.pdf); [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/701687536/6T40-6T45-SB-PI1131-DTC-P0776-P0796-P0878-P0873-P2714-P2723) (excerpt attribution of the "damaged pump" line uncertain)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, bulletin: GM bulletin **12244** (Nov 2012): harsh 2-3 and 4-5 upshifts, harsh R→D, coast-down bumps and harsh tap shifts on some 2013 Cadillac XTS with the 6T70. The fix is a **TCM reprogram with no parts**. — [NHTSA-hosted GM bulletin SB-10063084](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2012/SB-10063084-7690.pdf); a campaign of the same kind is indexed at [charm.li XTS "TCM Software Update For Shift Improvements"](https://charm.li/Cadillac/2013/XTS%20FWD%20V6-3.6L/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Relays%20and%20Modules/Relays%20and%20Modules%20-%20Transmission%20and%20Drivetrain/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/By%20Symptom/Campaign%20-%20TCM%20Software%20Update%20For%20Shift%20Improvements/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, bulletin: **PI0724B** (Jan 2013), "Harsh 2-3 Shift": duplicated by accelerating at moderate throttle and then releasing just before or during the 2-3 upshift. — [NHTSA-hosted PI0724B](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2013/MC-10133121-9999.pdf) (which models and transmission it covers is **OPEN**; don't attribute it to the 6T70 without checking)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, rebuilder: A 2012 Acadia 6T70 had 2-3 and 4-5 harsh shifts from bad shift overlap ("brief bind up"). — [Transmission Digest, "GM 6T70, Ford 6F50 Shift Complaints"](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/gm-6t70-ford-6f50-shift-complaints/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, wave plate, tasks **C.19, C.7, A.17**): **A cushion (wave) plate is a stressed spring part.** When it cracks, the debris damages the drum splines and the clutch loses apply quality, and the controls report it as a pressure or ratio fault. During overhaul, inspect wave plates for cracks at tooth roots and inspect drum splines, not only the frictions.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, calibration, tasks **A.3, A.25, C.6**): **Look up bulletins and calibration updates before you tear the unit down.** A shift-quality complaint on a newer model can be a known software issue that a reflash cures. That is also why ASE task A.3 (research SI, TSBs and recalls) sits early in the diagnostic order.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, overlap, tasks **A.9, A.23**): **A harsh shift with a "bind-up" feel is too much overlap** (the off-going element releases late). **A flare is too little overlap** (the on-coming element applies late). Naming which one you have points to either release control or apply fill/pressure.

## Q7. GM diagnostic practices: Transmission Adaptive Functions bulletin, Service Fast Learn rules, "adapts are not a repair", TCC slip PIDs

### Takeaway
GM's position, as quoted from bulletins: adapts are a continuous, closed-loop correction based on ISS/OSS shift timing. **Service Fast Learn (SFL)** resets and relearns them in the stall. It is **required** after replacing the transmission, valve body or TCM (with a characterization-data refresh), and it only completes when its entry conditions are met: fluid temperature window, no DTCs, good voltage, a healthy engine and normal line pressure. A relearn **can't compensate for a hydraulic or mechanical fault**. It is a calibration step after a real repair, or a remedy only for low-mileage unconverged adapts.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM, bulletin: **16-NA-019**, "Information on Transmission Adaptive Functions and Correcting Low Mileage Harsh Shifts, Slips or Flares." The TCM adjusts pressure-control solenoid commands to hold the calibrated shift timing ("adaptive learning"), watching ISS and OSS during commanded shifts to judge a shift too fast (harsh) or too slow (soft). The adaptive learn procedure is for vehicles with about **5,000 miles (8,050 km) or less**, or after a valve body, clutch repair or transmission replacement. The excerpt quotes the 8-speed RWD unit. — [NHTSA-hosted "Information on Transmission Adaptive Functions…"](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10112288-9999.pdf) (title and excerpt); corroborated by the [GM TechLink "Transmission Adaptive Functions" repost](https://www.cadillacvnet.com/forums/threads/gm-techlink-transmission-adaptive-functions.1132/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM: "Transmission Service Fast Learn is the recommended method to reset and relearn adapts." It runs tests that let the TCM learn individual clutch apply pressures, in the service stall, with no driving. "If a transmission assembly, valve body or TCM replacement is required, reset the shift adapts by performing a refresh of the Characterization Data and completing the Transmission Service Fast Learn procedure." — [GM TechLink repost](https://www.cadillacvnet.com/forums/threads/gm-techlink-transmission-adaptive-functions.1132/); [GM Document ID 1688788 "Service Fast Learn Adapts" (forum attachment)](https://www.gm-trucks.com/forums/applications/core/interface/file/attachment.php?id=106422); NHTSA-hosted GM bulletins [MC-10169298 (2019)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10169298-9999.pdf) and [MC-10211684 (2022)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10211684-0001.pdf) (excerpt attribution across these uncertain)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, adapts, tasks **A.23, A.24, A.25, C.6**): **Adapts correct normal variation and wear within limits. They don't fix leaks, broken parts or wrong fluid.** Reset or relearn adapts (1) after a repair or replacement that changes clutch fill or clearance, or after a module or valve-body swap, or (2) when a low-mileage unit's adapts have not converged. If the adapt values are pinned at their limit, or the problem comes straight back after a relearn, the relearn has revealed a mechanical or hydraulic fault. It has not fixed anything.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, relearn preconditions, tasks **A.21, A.22, A.4**): **A relearn is only as good as its inputs.** Low system voltage, engine misfire, unrelated DTCs, wrong fluid level or temperature, and low line pressure can all block completion or teach bad values. That is why ASE groups charging-system checks (A.21) and "engine vs. transmission" checks (A.22) under transmission diagnosis.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (lesson, TCC PIDs, tasks **A.13, A.18, A.24**): **Compare commanded slip with actual slip.** Large slip under a full-lock command means the TCC isn't holding: check the apply-circuit leak (bushings, seals, valve) or the friction lining. Slip that oscillates around the target means shudder, where fluid or lining friction is the first suspect. If the concern disappears when you command the TCC off (or changes when you command it on) with the scan tool, the TCC is confirmed as the source. The same logic separates TCC shudder from engine misfire or driveline vibration (task A.22).
