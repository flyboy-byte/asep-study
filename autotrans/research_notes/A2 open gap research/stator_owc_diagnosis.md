## Q1. What symptoms does each stator OWC failure mode produce, and how is each confirmed?

### Takeaway
There are two opposite failure modes. (1) The OWC **freewheels in both directions**. The converter loses torque multiplication, so the car launches poorly and is sluggish up to moderate speed. Stall speed is low, and highway cruise is normal. (2) The OWC is **seized/locked**. Launch is normal, but the car loses power at highway speed, its top speed is limited, and fuel use rises. The converter and fluid also overheat. Mode 1 is confirmed by a low stall reading after ruling out the engine, then by a bench check. Mode 2 cannot be seen on a stall test and is confirmed from road-test symptoms plus a bench check.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL: Stator OWC function. At idle or stall, fluid leaving the turbine strikes the stator blades in a direction that would turn the stator backward. The OWC holds it stationary, which gives torque multiplication. — [aa1car, Torque Converter Basics](https://www.aa1car.com/library/torque_converters.htm) (excerpt; attribution probable)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL: At stall, oil leaves the turbine at a high angle and strikes the broad face of the stator blades. That locks the one-way roller clutch. At the coupling phase the roller clutch releases, and the stator freewheels and turns with the impeller, turbine and oil. — [Quizlet, AT 414 Auto Transmission 2.1](https://quizlet.com/481410090/at-414-auto-transmission-21-flash-cards/) / [Brainscape, Automatic Transmissions](https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/automatic-transmissions-1273406/packs/2531089) (student flashcards, apparently textbook-derived; low authority, but consistent with every other source)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL (freewheeling mode): "If the stator's one-way clutch spins freely at low speeds, there will be a loss of torque multiplication, resulting in poor low-speed performance," and "If the stator's one-way clutch is not holding, the stall speed will be low." — [aa1car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/torque_converters.htm) / [freeasestudyguides, Overrunning One Way Stator Clutch](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/diagnose-one-way-stator-clutch.html) (excerpt; combined summary)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL, with a rebuilder case example (freewheeling mode): When an OWC freewheels in both directions, it disrupts fluid flow and torque multiplication from a stop up to about **20–25 mph**. After the converter enters rotary flow at cruise, where the stator should freewheel anyway, the vehicle performs normally. Reported symptoms: a vibration-like feel in gear, the engine feeling as though RPM is being dragged down, and a stall speed that seems abnormally low. The case is written about Ford 6F35 units, particularly with performance engines. — [Sonnax, Troubleshooting Ford 6F35 Torque Converter One-Way Clutch Failure](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1082-troubleshooting-ford-6f35-torque-converter-one-way-clutch-failure); republished at [Transmission Digest](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/troubleshooting-ford-6f35-torque-converter-one-way-clutch-failure/) (excerpt). The 20–25 mph figure comes from a Ford 6F35 case article. Don't present it as a universal number.
- DOCUMENTED (freewheeling mode, rebuilder wording): If the OWC fails, the converter loses torque multiplication. Customers typically say the car went from working well to bogging after launch, and it has trouble reaching stall speed. — [Sonnax, High Performance Converters: Stall Speed, Core Selection](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/86-high-performance-converters-stall-speed-core-selection-and-more) (excerpt; attribution probable)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL (seized mode): "If the clutch jams and locks the stator, the converter cannot circulate the fluid properly between the impeller and turbine." The resulting turbulence can cause a lack of power at highway speeds and overheating during cruise, and the fluid can run dangerously hot. — [aa1car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/torque_converters.htm) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (seized mode): If the stator clutch freezes and stays locked, the vehicle has poor high-speed performance and the torque converter overheats. — [freeasestudyguides, Overrunning One Way Stator Clutch](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/diagnose-one-way-stator-clutch.html) / Quizlet flashcards above (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (seized mode, consumer-level): A seized stator makes the engine labor at highway speed and can overheat the transmission. — [RepairPal, Signs Your Torque Converter Is Going Bad](https://repairpal.com/torque-converter) (excerpt; consumer site, supporting only)
- DOCUMENTED (seized mode, forum anecdote only): In one owner's case the transmission ran hot at about 70 mph but cooler at lower speeds. Power fell off above 4000 RPM and fuel consumption was high. — [HotRodders, Seized Torque Converter Stator](https://www.hotrodders.com/threads/seized-torque-converter-stator.324194/) (anecdote; illustrative, not authoritative)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL (confirmation): A stall test can help diagnose the stator OWC. A low stall reading points to an OWC that is not holding. — [ClassAce, Tech A/Tech B answer page](https://www.classace.io/answers/technician-a-says-if-the-stators-one-way-clutch-is-not-holding-the-stall-speed-will-be-low-technician-b-says-in-order-to-diagnose-a-one-way-stator-clutch-inside-the-torque-converter-a-stall-test-can-b) and [Brainly](https://brainly.com/question/37584138) (study Q&A sites; weak authority but consistent with aa1car and the service-manual stall tables)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: A seized stator does **not** show up on a stall test. At stall the stator is supposed to be held anyway, so a locked OWC behaves like a good one at stall and the reading is normal. Mode 2 is confirmed from its symptom pattern (normal launch; power loss, overheating and poor MPG at speed) and then a bench check after removal. This follows from the stator function above. No excerpt stated it in these words, so re-check against Halderman or Today's Technician.
- INFERRED: A simple way to remember the two modes: **freewheeling means you lose the low end (multiplication); seized means you lose the top end (coupling efficiency).**

## Q2. How is a stall test used and interpreted for converter diagnosis?

### Takeaway
A stall test holds the vehicle stationary at wide-open throttle in D (and in R, where the manual calls for it), briefly, and compares peak engine RPM with the vehicle's specification. **High stall** means a slipping clutch, band or transmission OWC, not the converter. **Low stall** means the engine is making low power (or the exhaust is restricted), or the stator OWC is freewheeling. The engine has to be ruled out before the converter is blamed. Manufacturers limit each run to about **5 seconds** and require a cool-down in P or N between runs. Many modern vehicles no longer publish or recommend a stall test.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL: A stall test measures the maximum engine speed at full throttle in "D" and "R". It checks the torque converter stator overrunning clutch and how well the transaxle clutches and low-reverse brake hold. — Mitsubishi service information, [23C Torque Converter Stall Test](https://mitsubishitechinfo.com/data/RE/2019/23/HTML/M123100540066300USA.htm) and [newtonnet Converter Stall Test](https://www.newtonnet.co.uk/coupe/service/webtech/iindex.asp@id=12164) (excerpt; Mitsubishi = OEM, but a non-GM example)
- DOCUMENTED (OEM, non-GM): **Higher than specification** means a clutch or overrunning clutch in the transaxle is slipping. **Lower than specification** means low engine output or a faulty torque converter is suspected. Check for engine misfire, ignition timing and valve clearance, and if those are good, the converter is faulty. — [Mitsubishi SI](https://mitsubishitechinfo.com/data/RE/2019/23/HTML/M123100540066300USA.htm) / [newtonnet](https://www.newtonnet.co.uk/coupe/service/webtech/iindex.asp@id=12164) (excerpt; combined summary)
- DOCUMENTED (OEM, non-GM): Probable causes when stall is below the service limit are low engine output, the throttle valve not opening fully, and a slipping torque converter OWC. — search summary of Subaru/Acura manual excerpts, e.g. [Subaru 4AT Stall Test PDF](http://ken-gilbert.com/wrx/STi_Manual/04.%20TRANSMISSION/02.%20AUTOMATIC%20TRANSMISSION/05.%20Stall%20Test.pdf) (excerpt; attribution uncertain among the Subaru/Acura results)
- DOCUMENTED (textbook-style statement): A stall speed below specification indicates a **restricted exhaust or a slipping stator clutch**. — [Brainly](https://brainly.com/question/37584132) / [freeasestudyguides, Torque Converter Stall Test](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-stall-test.html) (excerpt; study-site level)
- DOCUMENTED (OEM duration caution, Subaru): Don't continue a stall test for more than **5 seconds** at a time, measured from closed throttle through full throttle to the stall reading. Otherwise engine oil and ATF deteriorate and the clutches and brakes are harmed. After each test, cool down for **at least 1 minute** in P or N with idle below 1,200 rpm. Run the test with coolant and ATF at operating temperature. — [Subaru 4AT Stall Test](http://ken-gilbert.com/wrx/STi_Manual/04.%20TRANSMISSION/02.%20AUTOMATIC%20TRANSMISSION/05.%20Stall%20Test.pdf); similar Subaru 5AT pages: [subarufanclub.cz](https://subarufanclub.cz/wiki/_media/navody/4eat/5at_stall_test.pdf), [bustedfingermotorsports (Tribeca)](https://www.bustedfingermotorsports.com/storage/datapile/06%20B9%20Tribeca%20Service%20Manual/TRANSMISSION%20SECTION/AUTOMATIC%20TRANSMISSION/Stall%20Test.pdf) (excerpt). These are **Subaru OEM figures**. Other makers' limits differ (SI).
- DOCUMENTED: A stall test "can be used on many older transmissions" to check holding ability and the converter OWC. — [aa1car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/torque_converters.htm) (excerpt). This supports the point that the test is less commonly specified on current vehicles.
- DOCUMENTED (conflicting, low-quality claim, flagged): One flashcard set states that stall "more than 200 rpm above specification indicates the torque converter stator one-way clutch is not holding." — [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com/481410090/at-414-auto-transmission-21-flash-cards/) / [Brainscape](https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/automatic-transmissions-1273406/packs/2531089) (excerpt). **This contradicts** the OEM tables (Mitsubishi, Subaru) and aa1car, which put a non-holding stator OWC at **low** stall and a slipping clutch at **high** stall. Treat it as an error or a garbled card. Don't use it.

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Why a slipping stator gives low stall: with the stator freewheeling, returning fluid is not redirected to help the impeller, so there is no torque multiplication. The converter acts like a plain fluid coupling that loads the engine differently, and the engine cannot reach the RPM where it normally balances against converter load. (This is mechanism reasoning. The excerpts state only the result, "low stall".)
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Why high stall is not the converter: at stall the converter is just the fluid link. If a clutch or band in the gear train slips, the turbine is no longer held, and engine RPM climbs past spec. Comparing D and R (and other ranges, where the manual lists them) shows which apply element is common to the high readings. That comparison method is standard in OEM tables but was not quoted in full here (SI).
- INFERRED: On the question "low by how much", no legitimate public source I could reach gives a universal RPM margin. Readings are judged against the vehicle-specific stall range in SI. The only numeric margin found (the "200 rpm above" card) is the contradictory one flagged above.

### Gaps
- OPEN: I found no reliable public source with a universal "X rpm below spec = stator" threshold. Some textbooks reportedly give rough bands (e.g. slightly low = engine, far low = stator), but I couldn't verify one. **Don't invent one.**
- OPEN / SI: GM-specific stall-test guidance. GM service information for many current units doesn't list a stall test; I couldn't verify this here. Check GM SI before telling Logan GM calls for one.
- OPEN: The Mitsubishi page's exact duration and cool-down wording was not visible in the excerpt (fetch blocked).

## Q3. What bench and inspection checks are taught for a removed converter?

### Takeaway
After removal, the standard checks are these. (1) **Stator OWC rotation**: turn the stator inner race splines with fingers or a tool through the hub. It should turn freely one way and lock the other. Free both ways, or locked both ways, means the converter has failed. (2) **End play**: measure with a special tool that grips the turbine hub, read on a dial indicator, and compare with spec. (3) Inspect the **hub** (the seal and pump-drive surface) and the **stator support/shaft splines** on the transmission side. Converters are sealed, welded assemblies, so a converter with a bad OWC is **replaced, or rebuilt by a specialist** (cut open, rebuilt, re-welded, balanced). It is not repaired in a general shop.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL: To check the stator clutch, touch the splines of the inner race. The stator should rotate easily in one direction and resist in the other if the OWC is good. Alternatively, insert a few fingers into the stator splines and turn: a good stator clutch locks one way and spins freely the other. — [freeasestudyguides, Torque Converter Removal and Inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html) / [aa1car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/torque_converters.htm) (excerpt; combined)
- DOCUMENTED: If the stator spins both ways or not at all, it has failed and the converter should be replaced. — search summary for the seized-stator query (appears to come from [HotRodders](https://www.hotrodders.com/threads/seized-torque-converter-stator.324194/) or [aa1car](https://www.aa1car.com/library/torque_converters.htm); attribution uncertain)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL: The stator support (stator shaft) splines on the front of the transmission anchor the OWC inner race. They hold the stator so the sprag or roller can work, and the stator freewheels on those splines when coupling. — [Grand Prix Forums, stator clutch thread](https://grandprixforums.com/threads/torque-converter-is-my-one-way-stator-clutch-gone.66830/) / [Taurus Club, converter splines](https://taurusclub.com/forum/82-maintenance-repair/298049-torque-converter-splines-diagnosis-repair.html) (forum excerpts; correct in principle, low authority)
- DOCUMENTED (GM example of hardware): The converter center hub rides on the OWC and is splined to the transmission's stator support shaft (4T65-E thread context). — [Grand Prix Forums](https://grandprixforums.com/threads/torque-converter-is-my-one-way-stator-clutch-gone.66830/) (forum; GM 4T65-E)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL: On an assembled converter, end play is the main thing you can check easily on the bench. It needs a special tool that hooks the turbine so it can be moved up and down. — [freeasestudyguides, Torque Converter Removal and Inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html) (excerpt; attribution probable)
- DOCUMENTED (rebuilder method): There are two dial-indicator methods. In one, the converter is inverted and a tapered mandrel pushes the turbine up while the indicator reads the travel. In the other, an expanding shaft locks into the turbine hub splines and the up-and-down movement is read. — [Transmission Digest, New Converters: Visually Inspect, Measure, Document](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/new-converters-visually-inspect-measure-document/) / [Sonnax, Clutch Release Clearance 101](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/81-clutch-release-clearance-101) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (rebuilder figures, NOT ASE/OEM specs): In the excerpts, Sonnax/Transmission Digest describe release clearance (end play) as design-dependent. About 0.005–0.010 in. applies where the turbine hub contacts the cover. Where it doesn't, it is seldom under 0.030–0.035 in., and 0.045–0.055 in. is common. The GM 298 mm converter needs *more* clearance than Chrysler designs. Another excerpt cites a 0.050 in. maximum stator/turbine end play, beyond which the converter must be cut open. — [Sonnax, Clutch Release Clearance 101](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/81-clutch-release-clearance-101); [freeasestudyguides inspection page](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html) (excerpts; attribution of each number uncertain). **Always SI for the real spec.**
- DOCUMENTED (GM tool): GM dealer tool J-29830-2 is a torque converter end-play checking tool. — [eBay listing, J-29830-2](https://www.ebay.com/itm/375114381060) / [Toolsource GM end play adapter](https://www.toolsource.com/misc-specialty-tools-c-1321_2577/gm-end-play-checking-tool-adapter-use-with-t1055-p-96664.html) (commercial listings; they only establish that the tool exists; GM)
- DOCUMENTED / UNIVERSAL: A converter is a sealed, welded unit. Rebuilding means cutting off the cover, replacing the clutch lining, bearings and stator OWC, then re-welding and balancing. A general shop can't service the TCC lining or the OWC, so it needs a new or rebuilt converter. — [Core Transmission Parts, Torque Converter Failure](https://coretransmissionparts.com/blogs/news/torque-converter-failure-diagnosis-symptoms-and-when-to-replace) / [Industrial Reman, How a Torque Converter Is Rebuilt](https://www.industrialreman.com/heavy-equipment-transmission-repair-news/how-a-torque-converter-is-rebuilt-a-comprehensive-look-inside-the-process) (excerpts; vendor blogs, consistent with each other)
- DOCUMENTED: "Replace the torque converter if it has a bad one-way stator clutch." A faulty converter must be rebuilt if it is to be reused. — [freeasestudyguides, Torque Converter Removal and Inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html) (excerpt)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: A spline-drive check on the transmission side belongs in the same diagnosis. A stator support with stripped or worn splines would let a healthy OWC turn, so it would look like a freewheeling stator (low stall, poor launch). Inspect those splines before condemning the converter. This is mechanism reasoning, and I found no cited case.
- INFERRED: Standard practice (general, not verified here) is to replace the converter and flush or replace the cooler after internal failure that produces debris. That is outside this question's scope, so verify it separately if a guide needs it.

## Q4. Common exam confusions, and TCC diagnosis as it relates to separating converter faults

### Takeaway
The traps: (a) **Low stall has two causes**, a freewheeling stator OWC or a weak engine (or restricted exhaust). Rule out the engine first. (b) **High stall is not a converter fault.** It means a slipping clutch, band or transmission OWC. (c) A **seized stator gives a normal stall** and only shows up at speed. (d) **TCC faults are different from stator faults.** A TCC stuck *on* stalls the engine when the vehicle comes to a stop, like a manual car with the clutch still engaged. A TCC stuck *off* causes a small loss of highway efficiency and a DTC, not poor launch. TCC shudder is felt at steady part-throttle cruise when the clutch applies.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (see Q2): Low stall means low engine output or a slipping converter OWC; high stall means a slipping transaxle clutch or overrunning clutch. — [Mitsubishi SI](https://mitsubishitechinfo.com/data/RE/2019/23/HTML/M123100540066300USA.htm) / [newtonnet](https://www.newtonnet.co.uk/coupe/service/webtech/iindex.asp@id=12164) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED: Low stall can also come from a restricted exhaust. — [Brainly](https://brainly.com/question/37584132) / [freeasestudyguides](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-stall-test.html) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (conflict flag): The "200 rpm above spec = stator not holding" flashcard claim contradicts the OEM tables. It is an example of how the confusion appears in circulated study material. — [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com/481410090/at-414-auto-transmission-21-flash-cards/)
- DOCUMENTED (TCC stuck on; GM and others): On certain GM vehicles, the engine dying when coming to a stop after highway driving is a known result of the TCC sticking on. A stuck-closed TCC solenoid can keep the converter from unlocking before idle (GM 6L80, Ford 6R80 and Toyota U760E are cited as examples). — search summary citing [LiveAbout, How to Diagnose Converter Lock-Up Problems in Older GM Cars](https://www.liveabout.com/diagnosing-gm-converter-lock-up-problems-262653) and [iCarsoft, P0742](https://www.icarsoft-us.com/blogs/news/p0742-code-torque-converter-clutch-stuck-on-fix) (excerpts; consumer and tool-vendor level)
- DOCUMENTED (DTC meaning, generic OBD II): P0742 = TCC circuit stuck on. P0741 = TCC circuit performance or stuck off. — [OBD-Codes, P0742](https://www.obd-codes.com/p0742); [Edmunds, P0741](https://www.edmunds.com/obd-dtc/p0741.html) (excerpts). Exact enable criteria and descriptions per manufacturer: SI.
- DOCUMENTED (scan data): Watch TCC slip speed in live data. If slip reads 0 rpm while coming to a stop, the clutch is stuck applied. — [iCarsoft, P0742](https://www.icarsoft-us.com/blogs/news/p0742-code-torque-converter-clutch-stuck-on-fix) (excerpt; vendor blog; the method is sound, and the PID name varies by make)
- DOCUMENTED (TCC shudder description): At steady cruise around 40–50 mph and part throttle, it feels like driving over rumble strips. The TCC cannot hold, so it slips and grabs in a rhythm. — [Gearstar, Bad Torque Converter Symptoms](https://gearstar.com/blog/bad-torque-converter-symptoms-diagnosis-and-fixes/) (excerpt; vendor blog; the speed range is illustrative, not a spec)
- DOCUMENTED (general): The Motor magazine archive has a lock-up article ("Torque Converter Lock-up", May 1999). Its content could not be read. — [Motor, Torque Converter Lock-up](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/torque-converter-lock-may-1999/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, TCC apply/release test concept: Command the TCC on and off (by scan-tool bidirectional control, or at steady cruise where the manual allows) and watch engine RPM and slip speed. On apply, RPM should drop and slip approach near zero. On release, RPM should rise. If commanding apply does nothing, suspect the TCC or its control (stuck off). If slip stays near zero at idle or stop, it is stuck on. This matches the slip-speed method above. The exact test steps and pass/fail slip values are **SI** (GM included).
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, telling the faults apart:
  - Poor launch, low stall, normal cruise: stator freewheeling (after ruling out the engine and exhaust).
  - Good launch, poor top end, hot fluid, poor MPG, normal stall: stator seized.
  - Engine stalls when stopping, and slip is 0 at stop: TCC stuck on (not a stator fault).
  - Shudder at light-throttle cruise when lockup applies: TCC apply or friction problem (often fluid-related; verify against SI or bulletins).
  - High stall: gear-train clutch, band or OWC slipping (not the converter).
- INFERRED: A TCC stuck on can make a stall test impossible or misleading (the engine may stall when shifted into gear at idle), so a stall test shouldn't be used to diagnose that complaint.
