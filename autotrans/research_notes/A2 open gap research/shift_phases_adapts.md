## What are the phases of a clutch-to-clutch (synchronous) upshift?

### Takeaway
Engineering sources break a power-on clutch-to-clutch upshift into **fill (and sometimes prefill/boost) → torque phase → inertia (ratio-change) phase → completion**. In the torque phase the load moves from the offgoing element to the oncoming one while input speed stays roughly constant. In the inertia phase the oncoming clutch pulls turbine/engine speed down to the new ratio. The shift ends when oncoming-clutch slip reaches zero.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: In a clutch-to-clutch shift the clutch being released is the **offgoing** clutch and the clutch being applied is the **oncoming** clutch. A traditional upshift has two phases, the **torque phase** and the **inertia phase**. — [USPTO 10343690 "Input torque control during transmission shift"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10343690); [Springer: Torque Phase Control of the Clutch-to-Clutch Shift Process](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-41572-2_3)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL, **fill phase:** Pressure in the oncoming piston chamber rises until it overcomes friction and return-spring force. The piston then moves until the clearance (gap) to the plates is taken up, and only then does apply force (torque capacity) build. Ideally the gap is closed, with torque capacity near zero, at the end of the fill phase. — [Wiley/Hindawi: Dynamic Analysis and Control of the Clutch Filling Process in Clutch-to-Clutch Transmissions (2014)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/293637); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275224987_Dynamic_Analysis_and_Control_of_the_Clutch_Filling_Process_in_Clutch-to-Clutch_Transmissions)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL, **prefill/boost:** Prefill fills the piston cavity to bring the clutch near its "kiss point" at a pressure below engagement pressure. It also reduces air and line instability and cuts valve reaction time. Some strategies command a short high-pressure "boost" so the piston strokes fully without yet producing capacity, while offgoing pressure is lowered to just enough to hold torque until the handoff. — [USPTO 10995852 "Systems and methods for controlling friction elements"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10995852); [USPTO 8308608 "Hydraulic clutch fill control systems"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8308608) (snippet summary spans this patent result set)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL, **torque phase:** After fill, oncoming pressure is ramped up and offgoing pressure ramped down, moving the load from offgoing to oncoming. The torque phase ends when offgoing torque capacity reaches zero and oncoming capacity can carry the input torque. Ideally those two things happen at the same moment. **Input shaft speed generally stays constant** during the torque phase. — [USPTO 10343690](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10343690); [ScienceDirect: Robust control and estimation of clutch-to-clutch shifts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096706611730117X)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL, **inertia phase:** Starts when offgoing capacity reaches zero. The oncoming clutch ramps somewhat above the torque needed and holds there, slowing the turbine and overcoming the inertia of the turbine, engine and connected parts, so speed ratios move to the new gear. The phase **ends when all slip across the oncoming clutch is eliminated**. — [USPTO 10343690](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10343690)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Transmission controls request an engine torque reduction just before and during the shift (spark retard or closing the electronic throttle) so the clutches do not have to absorb all the shift energy. — [Transmission Digest: Torque Reduction](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/torque-reduction/); [USPTO 5562567 "Shift torque management"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5562567)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **what a tech sees on scan data:** Fill phase shows no change: commanded gear changes, and ISS/turbine speed and ratio still read the old gear. This is the "delay" portion. Torque phase shows little or no rpm change, and output torque sags slightly (the "torque hole" in engineering papers). Inertia phase is where ISS drops toward the new-gear sync speed while OSS continues. That rpm drop is the **ratio change** the TCM times. Completion is when calculated ratio equals the new gear ratio and holds there.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: On a freewheel (OWC) shift, the OWC releases itself, so only fill timing of the oncoming element matters. That is why clutch-to-clutch designs are much more sensitive to control timing and fluid condition. This builds on the learner's existing OWC notes.

## What goes wrong in each phase: flare vs. tie-up (bind-up)?

### Takeaway
A **flare** (engine/turbine rpm rises during the shift) means torque capacity was lost: the oncoming clutch filled or applied too late, or the offgoing clutch let go too early. A **tie-up/bind-up** (harsh, momentary drop in output, a "double-engagement" feel, possible deceleration or tire chirp) means capacity overlapped: the oncoming came on too early, or the offgoing released too late. Both are timing faults. Worn friction is only one possible cause.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A flare is when engine rpm climbs during the shift instead of dropping cleanly into the next gear, and it "normally means the oncoming clutch is not applying quickly enough." — [Core Transmission Parts: 6R140 shift flare & delayed engagement](https://coretransmissionparts.com/blogs/news/6r140-shift-flare-and-delayed-engagement-causes-and-the-fix) (rebuilder parts blog)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Worn friction material can cause a flare, "but it is just as likely, maybe even more likely," that something is upsetting the timing of a double-transition shift. — [Transmission Digest: Ford 6R80 & 6R140 troubleshooting timing-related shift issues](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/ford-6r80-6r140-troubleshooting-timing-related-shift-issues/); [Sonnax copy of same article](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1137-troubleshooting-ford-6r80-6r140-timing-related-shift-issues)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Timing problems with clutch apply, or with combined apply and release, can cause "flared shifts to harsh shifts to a slight bind up or a tire-screeching bind up on a shift transition". — [Transmission Digest: 6R80/6R140 timing-related shift issues](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/ford-6r80-6r140-troubleshooting-timing-related-shift-issues/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / FORD-SPECIFIC example: Variable-bleed solenoids on the 6R80/6R140 tend to lose calibration over time. A slow solenoid output can cause a 2-3 upshift flare, and an overly aggressive one can cause a 2-3 upshift bind-up. — [Transmission Digest: Troubleshooting Ford 6R80, 6R140 2-3 shift quality issues](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/troubleshooting-ford-6r80-6r140-2-3-shift-quality-issues/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: If the offgoing clutch does not fully disengage, the result is **clutch tie-up**. Tie-up makes the oncoming clutch absorb more shift energy, can eventually cause component failure, and typically causes vehicle deceleration. — [US7559873B2 "Resolving tie-up in a clutch-to-clutch transmission"](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7559873B2/en); [US5046175 "Method of detecting clutch tie-up during transmission shifting"](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/5046175.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: When circuit pressure loss gets bad enough, two clutches can overlap or one can fail to fully apply, which causes bind-ups and slipping. — search summary of the flare/tie-up result set, including [Transmission Digest 6R80/6R140 timing article](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/ford-6r80-6r140-troubleshooting-timing-related-shift-issues/) (exact origin of this sentence within the set not confirmed)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet, uncorroborated) / FORD double-transition context: One snippet said the two halves of a double-transition shift "MUST complete within 30 milliseconds of each other". If the offgoing releases too soon the engine flares, and the flare grows with how much sooner it completes. — appeared in the result set for the query that returned [Transmission Digest 6R80/6R140](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/ford-6r80-6r140-troubleshooting-timing-related-shift-issues/) and [Core Transmission Parts 6R140](https://coretransmissionparts.com/blogs/news/6r140-shift-flare-and-delayed-engagement-causes-and-the-fix). **Do not quote the 30 ms figure.** A follow-up search could not tie it to a specific page. ### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **phase-to-symptom map (study framing):**
  - *Fill too slow / fill volume under-learned* (worn clutch = more clearance = more volume, leaking piston seal or sealing ring, low line pressure, slow solenoid): the offgoing ramps down on schedule but the oncoming has no capacity yet, so a **flare** at the start of the shift and/or a **long shift time**.
  - *Offgoing released too early* (offgoing solenoid or regulator exhausting early, return-spring adapt wrong): **flare**, same feel as a late oncoming.
  - *Oncoming too early/too much pressure* (over-learned fill, sticking-high solenoid, excessive line pressure, tight clutch clearance after a rebuild): **tie-up** with a harsh "clunk" and momentary loss of output, or a **short, harsh** shift.
  - *Offgoing released too late* (sticking offgoing regulator valve, restricted exhaust, solenoid stuck applied): **tie-up/bind-up**, possible deceleration.
  - *Inertia phase too much oncoming pressure*: harsh/short shift (bump at end). *Too little*: long, "soft/slipping" shift with slow rpm drop, and in time overheated friction.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: In scan data, a flare shows as **ISS rising above the old-gear expected speed** (ratio moving the wrong way) before the drop. A tie-up shows as a **dip in OSS/vehicle acceleration** with little extra ISS movement. Graphing ISS, OSS and commanded gear together is how you tell them apart. This matches the learner's strength in electrical/scan analysis.

## How does the TCM measure shift quality and "adapt"?

### Takeaway
The TCM times each shift, and watches the ratio during and after it, using ISS/turbine and OSS speed. It compares actual shift time to a calibrated target for that shift at that load ("cell") and adjusts the next shift's commanded solenoid pressure and/or fill time/volume. Long shift → more pressure/fill. Short/harsh shift → less. GM groups these corrections into TAP (Transmission Adaptive Pressure) cells by throttle/torque level. Newer GM units also learn clutch fill volume and offgoing return-spring pressure.

- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / STELLANTIS origin but UNIVERSAL principle: Using the input and output speed sensors, the TCM calculates the rate of change during a shift transition and the gear ratio held after the shift. — [MOTOR: Transmission as a Second Language](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/transmission-as-a-second-language/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM-SPECIFIC: TAP = Transmission Adaptive Pressure (Systems). ### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **two families of adapt:** (1) *fill/volume/time* adapts correct **when** the oncoming clutch starts to carry torque. They mainly fix flare/tie-up timing at the start of the shift. (2) *pressure* adapts (TAP-style, by torque/throttle cell) correct **how hard** it applies through the ramp/inertia phase. They mainly fix long/soft vs. short/harsh shift time. Newer GM units add (3) *offgoing return-spring* learning, which corrects release timing. Knowing which family is off tells you which phase has the problem.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Adapts are learned per shift **and** per load cell. A shift can feel fine at light throttle and flare at heavy throttle when the heavy-throttle cell is maxed. Duplicating the complaint at the right load is part of diagnosis.

## What do adapt values in scan data indicate, and what does "maxed-out" mean?

### Takeaway
Adapts near zero/baseline mean the unit shifts as calibrated with little correction. Adapts that climb steadily in the "add pressure / add volume" direction mean the TCM is covering for a growing loss: worn friction (more clearance, more volume), a leaking piston seal or sealing ring, worn valve-body bores, or low line pressure. When an adapt hits its limit the TCM can't correct any further. The customer then feels harsh/soft/flared shifts or delayed engagement, and ratio/slip DTCs and failsafe may follow. Maxed adapts point to a mechanical or hydraulic fault. Resetting them does not fix it.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM-SPECIFIC: On an ideal transmission all **steady-state adapts** would be at 0 psi, meaning the PCM is not raising line pressure to cover for slip. If the computer sees the slightest slip in any gear, it immediately raises steady-state TAP pressure to try to stop it. — [Sonnax: GM's Steady State Adapts](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/160-gm-s-steady-state-adapts); [Transmission Digest copy](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/gms-steady-state-adapts/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: When wear or leaks make shifts so long that the TCM can no longer compensate by changing solenoid PWM for more flow, a gear-ratio code sets and the TCM goes to failsafe. — [MOTOR: Transmission as a Second Language](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/transmission-as-a-second-language/) (snippet summary also drew on [MOTOR: Getting Up to Speed on Transmission Diagnosis](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/getting-up-to-speed-on-transmission-diagnosis/))
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: "Eventually wear overcomes the TCM's ability to compensate and becomes apparent to the driver in the form of harsh or soft shifting, delayed engagement and shift timing problems." — search summary of garage-shift/adaptive result set including [Transmission Digest: Adapt & Overcome](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/adapt-overcome/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **how to read adapts diagnostically:**
  - *One clutch's adapts high across all cells*: problem specific to that clutch circuit (friction wear/clearance, piston seal, sealing ring, that clutch's regulator valve/bore, that solenoid).
  - *All clutches' adapts high*: shared supply problem (low line pressure from the pump/regulator, low fluid level, clogged filter, line-pressure solenoid, or wrong fluid).
  - *Only heavy-throttle cells high*: capacity problem that shows up only at high torque (marginal clutch or leak that pressure can't overcome at high load).
  - *Steady-state adapts above zero*: the TCM is fighting **slip in gear**, not just shift timing.
  - *Adapts swinging or not changing at all*: suspect adaptation being blocked (DTC present, temperature out of range, input signal fault, wrong/missing calibration). See the Gears "why doesn't it adapt" finding.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Resetting maxed adapts on a worn unit gives a short "improvement" (base values) and then the same maxed values again. That pattern confirms a hard fault. Resetting is a verification step, not a repair.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Comparing adapts to a known-good or the same unit's history is more useful than an absolute number, because the "normal" range is calibration-specific.

## How do downshift and garage-shift (N→D/R) quality work and adapt?

### Takeaway
A power-on downshift reverses the upshift: the offgoing clutch is slipped to let turbine speed **rise** to the lower-gear sync speed, and the oncoming clutch applies when slip across it reaches zero. Offgoing too loose/early = flare, oncoming too early = bind. A garage shift (N→D or N→R) is an apply of one or more elements from neutral. TCMs adapt garage-shift pressure/fill separately, and GM garage-shift relearn is done by repeated N-D / N-R shifts. Slow or harsh engagement is the garage-shift equivalent of long/short shift time.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: During a power downshift, turbine speed rises along a controlled profile. Offgoing clutch pressure is varied during the inertia phase to make turbine speed follow that profile up to the lower-gear speed. The oncoming slip speed is the gap between turbine speed and target sync speed. — [USPTO 5029494 "Control method of clutch-to-clutch powered downshift"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5029494); [USPTO 5070747 "Adaptive powered downshift control"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5070747)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Synchronization is detected when slip across the oncoming clutch is zero. Closed-loop control holds sync, then offgoing pressure is ramped to zero (down to a calibrated return-spring pressure) and oncoming pressure is raised quickly to complete the shift. — [USPTO 5029494](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5029494); [USPTO 8744705 "Determining clutch gains … during a power downshift"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8744705)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Engineering literature separates four clutch-to-clutch shift types (power-on upshift, power-on downshift, power-off/coast upshift, power-off/coast downshift), each with its own control flow. Closed-throttle double-transition downshifts have their own patents. — [ResearchGate: Generic control flow for the four types of clutch-to-clutch shifts](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303413712_Generic_control_flow_for_the_four_types_of_clutch-to-clutch_shifts); [USPTO 4989477 "Double transition closed throttle downshift control"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4989477)

## Which non-hardware and electronic-input faults degrade shift quality? (low/wrong fluid, VB bores, solenoids, voltage, TPS/torque signal)

### Takeaway
Shift quality depends on the TCM getting correct inputs (TPS/pedal, calculated engine torque over the network, ISS/OSS, TFT, system voltage) and on the hydraulics carrying out its commands (fluid level/type, solenoid calibration, valve-bore sealing, line pressure). A bad input makes the TCM choose the wrong pressure. A hydraulic loss makes a correct command come out wrong. In both cases adapts may move to compensate, or adaptation may be suspended.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: The TCM asks the engine controller to briefly reduce torque (spark retard or throttle closure) just before and during the shift. An invalid engine-torque signal from the ECM to the TCM can set DTCs such as P2637, and harsh shifts are typically a byproduct of an engine-torque-reduction error code. — [Transmission Digest: Torque Reduction](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/torque-reduction/). **NEEDS SI** for the DTC definition and which vehicle it applies to.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (aftermarket calibration context): Cutting torque-reduction tables too aggressively gives "brutal shifts and drivetrain stress", and torque-reduction curves have a big effect on shift feel. — [EFILive forum: 6 Spd upshift torque reduction](https://forum.efilive.com/archive/index.php/t-22518.html) (enthusiast tuning forum, low authority; principle only)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / FORD-SPECIFIC example: Solenoid calibration drift (variable-bleed solenoids) causes flare (slow output) or bind-up (aggressive output). — [Transmission Digest: 6R80/6R140 2-3 shift quality](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/troubleshooting-ford-6r80-6r140-2-3-shift-quality-issues/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM-SPECIFIC: Worn clutch-regulator valve bores, solenoid signal accumulators, and pressure-regulator/shuttle-valve wear stop Fast Learn and cause poor shifts. — [Sonnax: Tips for Success with GM Fast Learn](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1322-tips-for-success-with-the-gm-transmission-fast-learn-process)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Low system voltage codes such as P0562 mean the supply voltage to the module is low. Low voltage to a TCM can cause shifting problems. Common causes are a low or disconnected battery or a charging-system fault. — [OBD-Codes: P0562](https://www.obd-codes.com/p0562); [Wrangler TJ Forum: P0562 low voltage / tranny shifting issues](https://wranglertjforum.com/threads/code-p0562-low-voltage-tranny-shifting-issues.6834/) (general code reference + forum; low authority)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet, low-authority sources) / UNIVERSAL: Low system voltage is reported to cause harsh/"slamming" shifts and false solenoid-circuit codes (e.g., P0973) on aging vehicles. Check battery/charging and TCM B+ supply before condemning a solenoid. — [iCarsoft: P0973](https://www.icarsoft-us.com/blogs/news/p0973-code-shift-solenoid-a-control-low); [JustAnswer: can low battery voltage cause TCM shift problems](https://www.justanswer.com/transmissions/jiaqw-alternator-battery-low-voltage-cause.html). These sources gave specific voltage thresholds (e.g., "12.3 V minimum", "within 0.2 V of battery"). They are **not OEM**, and the thresholds are **not quoted here as specs**. NEEDS SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Correct-spec ATF carries a balanced friction-modifier package for friction durability, anti-shudder protection, and smooth lock-ups. Historically, Type F's high static friction coefficient produced harsh shifting, which shows how fluid friction properties affect shift feel. — [Schaeffer Dexron VI/Mercon LV](https://www.schaefferoil.com/205-atf-fluid.html); [Wikipedia: MERCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MERCON)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Line pressure must be normal, with a pressure rise on throttle, before GM Fast Learn. The procedure lists line-pressure capability as a precondition. — search summary of GM bulletin #1392 / [gm-trucks attachment "Document ID 1688788 Service Fast Learn Adapts"](https://www.gm-trucks.com/forums/applications/core/interface/file/attachment.php?id=106422). **NEEDS SI** for the actual pressure values (the snippet quoted numbers; not repeated here as specs).

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **low fluid:** The pump can draw air (aeration), and aerated fluid compresses. Fill takes longer and pressure is soft, giving flare/slip, delayed engagement, and adapts driven up across all clutches, often worse on turns/stops or when hot. Fix level first, then re-evaluate adapts.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **wrong fluid:** Different friction characteristics change how quickly the clutch "bites" at a given pressure. Adapts learned on the right fluid no longer fit: harsh, shudder, or slip depending on the fluid. Also viscosity affects fill timing, especially cold. Correct fluid plus relearn is the fix. Exact effects depend on the fluid, so this is INFERRED, not documented.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **worn valve-body bores:** Leakage past a regulator valve lowers the pressure a solenoid command actually produces, and the leak rate changes with temperature (worse hot). The result is soft/long shifts or flares that appear hot, with adapts high for the clutch fed by that valve. A vacuum test of the bore is the rebuilder method (see sibling note `area_c_overhaul.md` / Sonnax vacuum-test guides).
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **solenoid faults:** An electrically open/shorted solenoid usually sets a circuit DTC and a default gear. A **mechanically** sluggish, contaminated, or calibration-drifted solenoid often sets **no** circuit code and shows up only as shift-quality complaints and adapts moving. That is why shift-quality faults can have no DTCs.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **low system voltage:** Pressure-control solenoids are current-controlled. If supply voltage sags, the driver may not reach commanded current, so hydraulic output is wrong. On many designs low current = high pressure (normally-high solenoid), so harsh. On normally-low designs it would be soft. The TCM may also set a voltage DTC and suspend adaptation. The direction depends on solenoid type, so check SI for normally-high vs. normally-low.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **bad TPS/pedal or engine-torque signal:** The TCM picks a pressure/TAP cell from the torque it believes the engine is making. If it believes torque is **lower** than actual, it commands too little pressure: flare/slip, soft, and adapts pushed up. If **higher** than actual: harsh shifts. A missing torque-reduction response from the ECM (network fault, engine DTC, aftermarket tune) makes the clutches absorb full torque, which feels harsh. Aftermarket engine power or tunes that change torque reporting fall into this category.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **bad ISS/OSS signal:** Since shift time and ratio are calculated from these sensors, a noisy or dropping-out speed signal gives false "slip/long shift" readings. The TCM may then add pressure (harsh) or set ratio DTCs on a healthy mechanical unit. Check signal quality on a scope before condemning clutches. This connects to the learner's `issoss.pdf` notes on drum-as-reluctor sensing.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: TFT (fluid temp) input matters too. Most adaptive strategies only learn within a temperature window, and cold/hot calibration tables differ. A skewed TFT sensor can cause wrong-feel shifts and block learning.

## Why does shift quality change after TCM replacement/reflash or repair, and why is relearn needed? (C.6)

### Takeaway
Adapt tables are the TCM's learned corrections for **one specific** transmission's clutch clearances, solenoids and valve body. A new/reflashed TCM starts from base values, and a rebuilt or replaced transmission no longer matches the old learned values. Either way the shifts will be wrong (harsh, flared or long) until adapts are reset/relearned, by a scan-tool "fast learn" in the stall (GM 6-speed and later), a drive-cycle relearn, or both. Firmer-than-normal shifts during early relearn are expected.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM-SPECIFIC: GM Service Fast Learn Adapts runs a series of tests so the TCM learns individual clutch characteristics, then translates that data into the adaptive data cells used for clutch control. It is done in the service stall with no driving, through GDS 2. If the transmission was replaced, reset adapts with Service Fast Learn, evaluate the shifts, and further learn pressures/volumes if needed. — search summary of GM bulletin result set: [e90post attachment (GM #1392, 6L45–6L90)](https://www.e90post.com/forums/attachment.php?attachmentid=3482186&d=1718730748); [gm-trucks attachment Doc ID 1688788](https://www.gm-trucks.com/forums/applications/core/interface/file/attachment.php?id=106422); [MC-10169298 (2019)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10169298-9999.pdf)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM-SPECIFIC: Perform a "Fast Learn" or "Reset Adapts" after reflash to ensure proper shift adaptation. — same GM result set as above
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM-SPECIFIC: Fast Learn preconditions include TFT in a specified warm range, drive wheels blocked, parking and service brake applied, zero throttle, and no external engine-rpm control. — same GM result set. **NEEDS SI** for the exact TFT range (the snippet gave one; not repeated as a spec here).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM-SPECIFIC: For GM 8/9/10-speed Fast Learn, follow the scan-tool steps exactly and use data logging to find why a learn fails. Most clutch-control-valve mistakes will not pass Fast Learn. — [Sonnax: Tips for Success with GM Fast Learn](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1322-tips-for-success-with-the-gm-transmission-fast-learn-process)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Resetting adaptations is mandatory for many electronically controlled transmissions after repairs. — [Sonnax: Adapt & Overcome](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/110-adapt-overcome-considering-control-module-adaptation-during-diagnosis-repair)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Most modern vehicles need a factory or equivalent scan tool to reset shift adapts to zero, then driving to relearn them. Shift adapts compensate for wear by raising pressure to keep the same shift feel as friction components and valve-body parts wear. — [Transmission Digest: Let's Talk about Adaptive Shift Strategy (Jeff Parlee, 2011-11-01)](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/lets-talk-about-adaptive-shift-strategy/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Relearn is needed in two directions. (1) **New/reflashed TCM on the old transmission** starts from base or blank adapts, so it is harsh or flared until it learns that unit's wear. (2) **Old TCM on a rebuilt/replacement transmission** carries adapts learned for worn clutches (high volume/pressure), so a fresh, tighter unit gets **harsh/tie-up** shifts until adapts are cleared. The second case is the classic "harsh after rebuild" comeback.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: If a Fast Learn fails or adapts return to limits soon after relearn, go back to hydraulic/mechanical diagnosis. The failed learn is itself diagnostic data (Sonnax says to data-log it).

## Manufacturer terminology: GM TAP / clutch fill cells, Ford adaptive shift strategy / KAM, Chrysler CVI

### Takeaway
All three OEMs do the same thing (time the shift, correct pressure/volume) under different names. **GM**: TAP (Transmission Adaptive Pressure) cells per shift and throttle level, plus steady-state TAP, and on newer units clutch volume/return-spring (fill) adapts and Service Fast Learn. **Ford**: "adaptive shift strategy", with adaptive pressure tables stored in the TCM/PCM's **KAM** (keep-alive memory), reset with IDS/FDRS "Reset KAM" (on at least some units a battery disconnect does not clear it). **Chrysler**: **CVI (Clutch Volume Index)**, a calculated number for the fluid volume needed to apply each clutch. CVI rises as friction wears (more clearance), and low CVI can mean a tight pack or hydraulic cross-leak/over-pressure. It is updated by **Quick Learn** after overhaul.

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **CVI as a clearance proxy:** Since CVI is the volume needed to stroke the piston and take up clearance, **high CVI ≈ excess clearance (worn frictions) or volume lost to a leak** (piston seal, sealing ring, bore). **Low CVI ≈ too little clearance (over-tight pack after rebuild) or something making the clutch apply "early"** (cross-leak pre-filling the circuit, stuck accumulator, high line pressure). This is the same logic as GM "volume adapts" and ATSG "fill-time adaptation". CVI is one OEM's name for fill-volume learning.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL, **exam-safe cross-OEM statement:** Adaptive systems measure shift time/ratio change with speed sensors and adjust pressure and/or fill for future shifts. Adapts drifting to limits indicate wear or leakage the TCM can no longer mask. Relearn after TCM replacement, reflash, or transmission repair. Names (TAP, KAM, CVI) are OEM vocabulary, and the principle is universal.
