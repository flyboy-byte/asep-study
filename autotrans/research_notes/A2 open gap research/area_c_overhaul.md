## Q1. Which Area C principles are repeated across multiple sources?

### Takeaway
Three groups of principles show up again and again. Each is backed by two or more independent excerpts.
1. **Engine-to-transmission interface (C.1–C.3).** Seat the converter fully: you feel staged engagements, often called "three clunks", as it picks up the input-shaft splines, the stator-support splines and the pump drive lugs. Confirm it sits deep and turns freely, with free play to the flexplate. Dowels must be present. The converter pilot must center in the crank bore. Check crank end play.
2. **Cleanliness and flow (C.4).** Flush the cooler and lines, then verify cooler flow, before a rebuilt or replacement unit goes in. This is tied to warranty.
3. **Wear and sealing inspection (C.7–C.23).** Friction plates: look for glazed, burnt or damaged frictions. Steels: look for heat-discolored or warped plates. Check piston check balls, bushings (worn bushings leak lube or pressure and cause cross-feed), and pump clearances (straightedge plus feeler gauge). Verify one-way clutch rotation direction and orientation. Air-test clutches and servos, **before and after disassembly**, with the valve body removed. Soak new frictions in ATF.

### Cited Findings

#### C.1 R&I; engine core plugs, rear crank seal, dowel pins/holes, mating surfaces
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: The A2 R&I task includes inspecting engine core plugs, the rear crankshaft seal, transmission dowel pins, dowel-pin holes and mating surfaces. — [freeasestudyguides, Torque Converter Removal and Inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html) (excerpt; matches the official task wording in `research.md`)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Missing bell-housing-to-engine dowel pins "can cause pump bushing failure, converter hub failure, damage to flex plate, pump seal leaks and possible pump damage on some transmissions." — [Sonnax, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/99-avoiding-after-overhaul-problems); republished as [Transmission Digest, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/avoiding-after-overhaul-problems/) (same article, so count it as one source)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Without dowel alignment, the converter pilot sits off-center. That can wear the crank pilot, damage the flexplate, and cause vibration and premature wear of the pump, the converter hub and internal bushings. — [Certified Transmission, The Importance of Dowel Pins](https://www.certifiedtransmission.com/transletter/april-2020/) (excerpt; independent of Sonnax, so this principle has two sources)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Using an impact gun on the crank-to-flexplate bolts can stretch and break the bolts. It can also distort the crank flange enough that the rear main seal leaks. — [Sonnax, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/99-avoiding-after-overhaul-problems) (excerpt). This links C.1 (rear crank seal) to C.2 (flexplate bolts).

#### C.2 Flexplate, indexing, converter bolts, converter pilot, crank pilot bore, crank end play, pump drive surfaces
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Some engine and converter combinations use a ring or sleeve between the converter pilot and the crank bore, and it can stay stuck to the old converter. A pilot that is loose in the crank points to a missing sleeve. The converter then does not spin on the crank centerline, which causes vibration at the least and possibly flexplate damage, pump damage or front-seal leaks. — [Sonnax, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/99-avoiding-after-overhaul-problems) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: The converter pilot must enter the crank bore fully and freely. — [Florida Torque Converters, installation instructions (PDF)](https://www.floridatorqueconverters.com/media/pdf/TORQUE-CONVERTER-INSTALLATION.pdf) (excerpt; attribution among several install PDFs in the same result is probable, not certain)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Missing dowels and cracked or bent flexplates normally cause transmission or converter failure in a very short time. — same result set; [Holley converter installation instructions (PDF)](https://documents.holley.com/6afa6bbdc813765a03b7cabc6b5609e9d0b52a8e.pdf) or the Florida Torque Converters PDF (attribution uncertain between the two)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Loose converter-to-flexplate bolts make a knock that "sounds very much like loose engine parts." Flexplates can crack in a ring around the crank bolt holes, which also knocks, and the only fix is replacement. The cracks are often hard to see in the car. — [EricTheCarGuy, Loose Torque Converter Bolts and Cracked Flex Plates](https://www.ericthecarguy.com/loose-torque-converter-bolts-and-cracked-flex-plates/) plus Jeep and TurboDodge forum threads in the same result, e.g. [NAXJA archive](https://naxja.org/forum/archive/index.php/t-72513.html) (consumer or forum level; consistent across several)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: The converter-to-flexplate bolts can be reached through the inspection cover. The flexplate-to-crank bolts can only be reached with the transmission out. — same result set (forum level)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (indexing): Repair manuals typically say to **mark the converter-to-flexplate position before removal and reinstall it in the same position.** Converters and flexplates are usually balanced separately. — [GMT Nation forum](https://gmtnation.com/forums/threads/torque-converter-to-flexplate-position-relationship.15539/) and [go4trans, Torque Converter Balancing](https://shop.go4trans.com/torque-converter/torque-converter-balancing-adjustment-necessity-or-waste-of-time/) (excerpts; the "repair manuals say" claim is second-hand, so SI for the actual unit)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (crank end play): Check crank end play with a dial indicator on the block, stem parallel to the crank centerline: pry forward, zero, then pry back. It should be checked before the transmission is installed. Worn thrust bearings allow too much end play. — [freeasestudyguides, Thrust Bearings and Crankshaft End Play](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/engine-thrust-bearings.html) (excerpt). That excerpt's spec (0.0015–0.0065 in.) and wear limit (0.016 in.) belong to one example engine: **needs SI; don't quote.**
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (the converter side of thrust failure): Converter ballooning, where charge pressure pushes the converter forward, can load the crank thrust bearing. The THM 400 is the classic case: it has no converter-regulating circuit and relies on cooler flow to keep converter pressure in check. — [MOTOR, Thrust Bearing Failure Prevention & Analysis](https://www.motor.com/magazine-summary/thrust-bearing-failure-prevention-analysis/) and [Gears, Under Pressure; Converter Pressure](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/under-pressure-converter-pressure-that-is-revisited/) (excerpts; which of the two says which is uncertain)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (pump drive surfaces): Check the converter hub and drive notches or lugs for burrs, nicks and scratches, and polish minor ones with crocus cloth. A deep groove where the pump seal rides wears the new seal and causes a leak. — [freeasestudyguides, Torque Converter Removal and Inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html) and [BTE Racing install instructions](https://www.bteracing.com/installation-guides/posts/torque-converter--transmission-installation-instructions) (excerpts; the two sources agree)

#### C.3 Install the converter; converter-to-pump engagement; converter movement for pilot engagement
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Turn the converter while pushing it in, and feel or listen for **three distinct engagements**: input-shaft splines, stator-support splines and pump lugs. One or two means it is not seated. — [freeasestudyguides/BTE result set](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html); independently in [Ford-Trucks forum, T/C not seated 4R70W](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/1573311-t-c-not-seated-properly-during-trans-install-4r70w.html) and the [AutoZone converter DIY](https://www.autozone.com/diy/transmission/how-to-replace-torque-converter) ("engage in stages") (three or more excerpts agree). The count of three is typical but depends on design: not every converter engages three separate splined or lug features, so check SI.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Measure the converter's depth in the bell housing, and use that measurement at reinstall to confirm the converter is aligned with the pump. — [freeasestudyguides, Torque Converter Removal and Inspection](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: When the converter is pulled forward to meet the flexplate, it must have free play. If it has none, the converter is pushing on the pump. An excerpt gives "typical" targets of 0.100–0.175 in. of free play, and another gives a 1/8–3/16 in. converter-pad-to-flexplate gap. **These are aftermarket figures (performance converter and installer instructions), not OEM: needs SI.** — [Monster Transmission, Prevent Stacked Pump Failure](https://monstertransmission.com/blogs/news/prevent-stacked-pump-transmission-failure-guide) plus forum excerpts (attribution approximate)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: The converter should spin freely from the moment the transmission is mated until every bolt is tight. Any resistance while hand-tightening the bell-housing bolts means the converter was not fully seated in the pump, so separate and start over. — [vintage-mustang / thirdgen forum result set](https://www.thirdgen.org/forums/transmissions-drivetrain/545371-incorrect-torque-converter-install.html) (forum level; consistent with the installer PDFs)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A converter that is not seated can destroy the front pump. The hub (snout) can deform against the pump, and a "pop" while bolting up can be the converter breaking the pump or stator support. The pump then has to come out and be inspected or rebuilt. — [Ford-Trucks forum](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/1573311-t-c-not-seated-properly-during-trans-install-4r70w.html) / [JustAnswer, Dodge](https://www.justanswer.com/dodge/mrq31-torque-converter-not-put-right-not-seated.html) (forum or Q&A level; mechanism consistent)

#### C.4 Cooler, warmer, thermostat, bypass valve, lines: inspect, test, flush, replace
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Contaminated coolers and lines (clutch material, metal) are a common cause of early failure in replacement transmissions. Remanufactured units ship with a cooler-flush kit, and skipping the flush can void the warranty as an "installation error." — [ACE Transmission Reman, cooler flusher](https://acetransmissionreman.com/tool-every-transmission-repair-shop-needs/) / [ACE, warranty claims](https://acetransmissionreman.com/how-transmission-warranty-claims-work/) / [Transmission Digest, To Flush or Not to Flush](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/to-flush-or-not-to-flush/) (several excerpts agree)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: **Check cooler flow after flushing, with the vehicle at operating temperature.** Don't use the flusher to judge flow: it pushes harder than the transmission does, and a cooler can start clogging again after a flush. Sonnax gives a minimum for units with 5/16 in. lines (one quart in 20 seconds; most units do one quart in about 10 seconds). **Rebuilder rule of thumb: needs SI.** — [Sonnax, Avoiding After-Overhaul Problems](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/99-avoiding-after-overhaul-problems) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A thermal bypass valve keeps fluid out of the cooler until it is warm. If it fails closed, from mechanical failure or debris, the cooler gets no flow and the transmission overheats, especially when towing. Bench test: heat the element in ATF with a thermometer and confirm full pin extension at the specified temperature (SI). — [Diesel World, Transmission Cooler Bypass Valve](https://www.dieselworldmag.com/diesel-technology/transmission-cooler-bypass-valve/); [Sonnax, Understanding Cooler Control Valves](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/1268-understanding-cooler-control-valves-what-thermal-vs-pressure-control-means-for-your-build); [Mishimoto TBV R&D](https://www.mishimoto.com/engineering/2020/01/dodge-ram-67l-cummins-thermal-bypass-valve-rd-pt1) (excerpts; attribution of the bench-test method is probably Sonnax). GM example: aftermarket listings for a 10L80 thermal bypass valve ([PPE](https://ppepower.com/products/transmission-fluid-thermal-bypass-valve-10l80-trans-2019-2024-gm-3-0l)) show that GM units use them. GM-specific test procedure: SI.

#### C.8 Fluid pump and drive components
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Pump clearance is measured by laying a **straightedge across the pump body face and using a feeler gauge** between the straightedge and the gears or rotors (gear-to-cover, also called side clearance). — [HowACarWorks, Checking the Oil Pump](https://www.howacarworks.com/engine/checking-the-oil-pump) and the [TH350 pump-gear-clearance thread (HotRodders)](https://hotrodders.com/forum/th350-pump-gear-clearance-44087.html) (excerpts; the first is an engine pump, but the method is the same). The TH350 figure in the excerpt (0.0015 in. wear limit) is **forum-quoted: needs SI.**
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: The other standard pump measurements are outer gear or rotor to body (pocket) clearance and tip clearance, both with a feeler gauge. This is textbook-standard, but **no excerpt this session stated it for a transmission pump**, so verify against Halderman or Today's Technician.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Converter-hub-to-pump-bushing clearance matters for lube-film thickness and alignment. One excerpt quotes 0.003–0.0045 in. **Unit and source uncertain: needs SI.** — [Sonnax, Bushwhacked: Avoiding Preventable Bushing Problems](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/809-bushwhacked-keys-to-avoiding-preventable-bushing-problems) (excerpt; attribution probable)

#### C.9 Transaxle differential bearing preload / C.17 transaxle final drive
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Differential side bearings are tapered bearings set by **shims or threaded adjusters behind the races.** Too loose lets the case move and damages parts. Too tight overheats the bearings and wears them early. True turning-torque readings can be taken **only with the geartrain removed from the case.** — [freeasestudyguides, Transaxle Differential Side Bearing Noise](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transaxle-side-bearings.html) (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / SI: One example method: measure end play, then choose a shim equal to the end play plus a constant preload, and confirm with turning torque. Figures in the excerpt: 0.18 mm (0.007 in.) preload and 6–12 in-lb. **These come from one unnamed transaxle: needs SI; don't quote.** — same source (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Final-drive and differential gears need correct backlash (dial indicator) and the tapered bearings need correct preload. Both are set with selective shims and checked against the manufacturer's spec. Some transaxles use a planetary final drive (sun, pinions, carrier, ring). — [freeasestudyguides, Transaxle Final Drive Gears and Chain Assembly](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/final-drive.html) and [freeasestudyguides, Ring and Pinion Backlash](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/ring-pinion-backlash-measurement.html) (excerpts; one site)

#### C.10 End play / C.11 shafts, splines, lube holes, bushings, journals / C.12 bushings, thrust washers, bearings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: **Bushings without lube grooves are sealing oil pressure.** On many units the front stator-support bushing seals converter-apply or TCC-release oil, so wear there can cause TCC slip codes or poor TCC operation. — [Sonnax, Bushwhacked](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/809-bushwhacked-keys-to-avoiding-preventable-bushing-problems) and [Transmission Digest, Tips for Quality Rebuilding](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/tips-for-quality-rebuilding/) (excerpts; possibly the same author's material)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL, with a named example: A worn rear stator bushing lets lube oil **cross-feed past the input-shaft sealing rings** into the 3-4 clutch circuit. That drags and burns the clutch, and can cause a third-gear start (4L60-E context). — same result set; see also [Sonnax, 4L60-E 3-4 Clutch Burnup](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/659-all-over-the-map-attacking-4l60-e-burnt-3-4-clutches-with-confidence) (GM example; the principle is universal)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Torrington bearings are caged needle thrust bearings that cut friction and help control end play. **Hold them in place during assembly with petroleum jelly or assembly lube, never white chassis or bearing grease**, because those don't mix with ATF. — [freeasestudyguides, Automatic Transmission Bearings](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/automatic-transmission-bearings.html) (excerpt)
- OPEN: No excerpt described the result of a Torrington bearing or selective thrust washer installed upside down or in the wrong position. See Gaps.

#### C.13 Planetary gear assembly
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Measure planet pinion end play with a feeler gauge between pinion and carrier. Inspect thrust washers, pinions, needle rollers and pinion shafts. A pitted or discolored race means replacement. One excerpt gives "typical" 0.010–0.025 in. — from a forum or heavy-equipment manual, **not an automotive A2 source: needs SI.** — [LS1Tech, Gear endplay in OD planetary](https://ls1tech.com/forums/automatic-transmission/1944750-gear-endplay-od-planetary.html), [TurboBuick, Rear carrier pinion end play](https://turbobuick.com/threads/rear-carrier-planetary-pinion-end-play.435936/), [Scribd pinion carrier inspection guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/403272792/4650-and-4850-Section-55-4) (weak sources; the method is consistent)
- OPEN: No textbook excerpt stated the pinion replacement rules (for example, whether pinions are serviced individually or the carrier is replaced as an assembly).

#### C.16 Drive chains, sprockets, CVT belts/pulleys, gears
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Checking transaxle drive-chain slack: deflect the chain one way with a screwdriver and mark the case, deflect it the other way and mark again. The total between the marks is the chain slack, compared against spec (SI). A good chain has slight deflection. — [freeasestudyguides, Transaxle Final Drive Gears and Chain Assembly](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/final-drive.html) (excerpt; one source)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (general chain practice, industrial): Hooked sprocket teeth mean replacement. Wear on the sides of the teeth means a misalignment check. — [Martin Sprocket, Chain Drive Maintenance Guide (PDF)](https://www.martinsprocket.com/docs//brochures/guides/maintenance-troubleshooting-guide.pdf) (industrial source; applies by analogy only)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (CVT): Rebuilders inspect pulley belt faces for scoring from belt slip and pressurize each pulley to check that it holds. Apparent scoring may be smeared bearing debris. Bearing failure is common, so pulleys get disassembled. Excessive wear on the sliding-sheave bushing means replacing the whole sheave assembly. Reassemble in the original positions. — [Gears, Jatco CVT overhaul](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/the-jatco-cvt-obstacles-to-the-overhaul-with-insight-from-robert-bateman/), [Gears, JF017E Pulleys and Chain](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/pulleys-and-chain-installation-jf017e/), [Transmission Digest, CVTs: Rebuild or Not](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/cvts-do-i-rebuild-them-or-not/) (excerpts; Jatco-specific examples)

#### C.19 Hydraulic clutch pack components
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Frictions: inspect for burning, glazing, uneven wear, material loss or flaking. Steels: inspect for warping, heat damage (discoloration or bluing), scoring and wear. Overheated steels lose hardness and wear resistance. Check flatness on a flat surface with a feeler gauge (one excerpt cites about 0.006 in. max: **generic, needs SI**). — [Automatic Kings, Parts You Should Never Reuse](https://blog.automatickings.com/the-transmission-rebuild-parts-you-should-never-reuse) plus powersports and forum excerpts in the same result (mixed quality; the "blued = heat-damaged" principle is consistent)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Many clutch pistons or drums have a **check ball (or reed valve) that exhausts or bleeds fluid** when the clutch releases. If it leaks, apply pressure is lost (slip, burnt clutch). If it is **clogged or stuck**, centrifugal force on trapped fluid can partly apply the released clutch and burn it. Rebuilders test it with vacuum; the excerpt gives a test-stand calibration: **vendor procedure, SI.** — [Sonnax, 4L60-E 3-4 Clutch Burnup](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/659-all-over-the-map-attacking-4l60-e-burnt-3-4-clutches-with-confidence) / [Transmission Digest, All Over the Map](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/all-over-the-map/) (the same article republished)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Return springs must all be present and serviceable. Replace Belleville springs and never leave out load-release springs (a rebuilder's opinion). A compressor tool is used to reach the snap ring. — [CarTech / OnAllCylinders / ChevyDIY rebuild articles](https://www.chevydiy.com/how-to-rebuild-gm-automatic-overdrive-transmissions/) (excerpts; mixed attribution)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (soak): **Soak new frictions in ATF before assembly.** Stated durations differ: Alto says "a minimum of 15 minutes" (and 20–30 minutes minimum for a partly submerged clutch). A rebuilder page says at least 30 minutes and adds that dry plates absorb fluid after assembly, which can shift the measured clearance. Raybestos install instructions say "pre-soak all friction plates in ATF … prior to final assembly" with no duration in the excerpt. — [Alto FAQs](https://www.altousa.com/page/faqs/) (fetch blocked; snippet only); [Sun Transmissions, clutch pack clearance specs](https://suntransmissions.com/blogs/news/transmission-clutch-pack-clearance-specs-by-family-4l60e-6r80-46re-48re); [Raybestos, 4L60E 3-4 ZPak installation](https://www.raybestospowertrain.com/blog/4l60e-3-4-clutch-zpak-installation-rzp-001). **The only friction-maker duration found is Alto's 15-minute minimum.** Follow the friction supplier's or the SI instruction.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Lip-seal lips face **toward the pressure (the apply chamber)** so fluid pressure pushes the lip against the bore. A backward lip leaks under pressure. Lubricate the seals and bore, and use an installer tool where one is needed. — [freeasestudyguides, Automatic Transmission Seals](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/automatic-transmission-seals.html) plus general seal-maker pages ([sealvendor](https://sealvendor.com/blog/oil-seal-installation-direction)) (excerpts)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (sealing rings): Sealing rings on shafts and drums come as **cast iron (hook-joint or butt-joint)**, **Teflon scarf-cut or step or butt joint**, and **solid Teflon, which needs an expander and sizing tool** after installation (one forum identifies white rings with red dots as solid rings that need sizing). Teflon rings seal because fluid pressure pushes them against the bore and groove side ("blown closed"), not through ring tension. Scarf-cut rings forgive tolerances better, and domestic makers mostly used them. — [Transmission Digest, Sealing-Ring Technology: Small Parts, Big Problems](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/sealing-ring-technology-small-parts-big-problems/) plus [YellowBullet](https://www.yellowbullet.com/threads/scarf-cutting-teflon-sealing-rings-on-4l80e-input-shaft.920554/) / [LS1Tech](https://ls1tech.com/forums/automatic-transmission/1554005-how-install-teflon-seals-without-tools.html) forum excerpts (TD is the stronger source)

#### C.21 Air test clutches and servos
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (**order**): "Perform air pressure tests **before and after** transmission disassembly." Once the valve body is off, the circuit passages are exposed for air testing. — [freeasestudyguides, Automatic Transmission Pressure Tests](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/automatic-transmission-pressure-tests.html) (excerpt). An installer describes air-testing servos "once the transmission is completely assembled but **before installing the valve body**" ([IH Parts America forum](https://forums.ihpartsamerica.com/threads/servo-air-test.5875/)). Mopar/Jeep service text says clutches are tested "after the valve body has been removed." — [Jeep JK service manual mirror](https://s3.amazonaws.com/jeepwranglerjk2010/7c033609.html) (three sources agree)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (result): A good clutch gives a **"dull thud"** as it applies. A servo gives a thunk you can hear, feel or see as the band applies. A **large amount of escaping air (hissing)** means seal leakage, and the unit must be disassembled and the seals inspected. — [Jeep JK service text](https://s3.amazonaws.com/jeepwranglerjk2010/7c033609.html); [TM 9-2320-266-34 (US Army), Clutch and Servo Air Pressure Tests](https://trucks125ton.tpub.com/TM-9-2320-266-34/TM-9-2320-266-340065.htm); [IH forum](https://forums.ihpartsamerica.com/threads/servo-air-test.5875/) (OEM text plus a military TM: strong agreement)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (air supply): The compressed air must be **free of dirt and moisture.** The Mopar text uses about 30 psi for clutch testing. **That figure is OEM-specific: needs SI.** — [Jeep JK service text](https://s3.amazonaws.com/jeepwranglerjk2010/7c033609.html) (excerpt)

#### C.22 One-way clutches
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Roller and sprag clutches have no friction material. **A sprag or roller clutch cannot be assembled in either direction.** It is serviced as a complete unit if any damage is found. — [freeasestudyguides result / A2 practice test 9 page](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/a2-automatic-transmission-test-9.html) (excerpt; the practice page is used as a source of principle, not as a question bank)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (bench check): Hold one race and turn the other. It should freewheel one way and lock the other, in the direction the SI specifies. The 62TE example: with the ID groove facing you, hold the inner race; the outer race should turn clockwise and lock counterclockwise. — [Transmission Digest, "Only One Way – the Right Way" (62TE overrun sprag)](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/only-one-way-the-right-way/) (fetch blocked; excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / NON-GM classic example: In the **Chrysler 62TE**, the overrun sprag's outer-race ID groove faces the low-clutch drum, *not* the high-clutch drum. **Installed backward, it causes a tie-up whenever the direct clutch applies.** If the sprag was disassembled and rebuilt wrong, the groove no longer tells you anything, so bench-check the rotation. — same TD article (excerpt)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / GM classic example (forum level): In the **4L60-E**, a backward **forward sprag** (input sprag) causes **no movement in D4 or D3** (it acts like neutral), while manual 2 and manual 1 still drive, because the overrun clutch carries the load. — [LS1Tech, Rebuilt 4L60E no drive, has reverse and manual 1st/2nd](https://ls1tech.com/forums/automatic-transmission/1978622-4l60e-rebuild-no-drive-but-have-reverse-manual-1st-2nd.html), [GMT400, no first gear in drive after rebuild](https://www.gmt400.com/threads/4l60e-no-first-gear-in-drive-after-rebuild.56146/), [YellowBullet 4L60/700R4 input sprag](https://www.yellowbullet.com/threads/4l60-700r4-input-sprag-question.417459/) (several forum threads agree; **not confirmed from GM SI or ATSG**). The same threads name the **low roller clutch** as the next suspect when first gear is missing in D.
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: To check roller-clutch cage wear, install the cage in the housing without the inner race and compare how far it rotates against a new cage. — [Quizlet, Chapter 16 flashcards](https://quizlet.com/775435086/chapter-16-251-quiz-flash-cards/) (textbook-derived flashcards; low authority)

#### C.23 Bands and drums
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Inspect bands for damage, wear and burned lining. — [Quizlet, trans 28 flashcards](https://quizlet.com/631046767/trans-28-flash-cards/) (low authority; the only transmission-specific excerpt found)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (adjustment method): Loosen the locknut, make sure the adjusting screw turns freely, **torque the screw to the specified value, back it off the specified number of turns, then hold the screw and torque the locknut.** The excerpts give Chrysler 46RE/48RE figures (e.g. 72 in-lb then 1¾ turns front / 3 turns rear on the 48RE). **Unit-specific: needs SI; do not quote.** — [Cummins Forum 48RE](https://www.cumminsforum.com/threads/48re-band-adjustment.244043/), [DodgeForum 46RE](https://dodgeforum.com/forum/2nd-gen-ram-tech/310787-band-adjustment-46re.html), [Jag-Lovers BW66](https://www.jag-lovers.org/xjlovers/xjfaq/transxadjust.htm) (forums quoting factory procedure; the method is consistent across three transmission families)
- OPEN: No transmission-specific excerpt was found for drum inspection (scored, glazed, out-of-round or heat-checked band surfaces). The heat-check and glazing criteria that came up were from **brake** drums ([Meritor bulletin](https://cbsparts.ca/admin/bulletins/trouble_shooting_brake_drum_wear_by_meritor.pdf)) and are not A2 sources.

#### Initial fill (spans C.3, C.4 and installation)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: After a rebuild, "initial fill" (dry unit and dry converter) is more than a drain-and-refill. The general method: add a partial fill, start the engine so the pump fills the converter, keep adding with the engine running, and **check level hot, engine running, in P or N** (or per SI; many modern units use a check-plug procedure). — [BITOG, What does Initial Fill mean?](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/what-does-initial-fill-mean.79944/) and forum excerpts (forum level). Quart counts in the excerpts are **one owner's numbers: needs SI.**

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: The C.1–C.3 findings follow one chain of cause and effect. Anything that pushes the converter hub off-center or forward (missing dowels, missing pilot sleeve, converter not seated, no free play, a ballooned converter, bolting up through bind) shows up as **pump bushing, front seal, pump gear or pump body damage** and **crank thrust wear or flexplate cracks.** At exam level, "front pump damage right after an R&I" should point first to installation causes.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (C.5): Misrouted or pinched cooler lines, harnesses and vent hoses, and mounts or cables not reinstalled correctly, are the typical post-install causes of overheating, leaks, codes, vibration or wrong-range complaints. No excerpt was found, so this is a gap to verify.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Air testing is a **diagnostic step at two points**. During teardown it locates the failed circuit before parts are disturbed. At reassembly, with the valve body still off, it confirms seals and sealing rings before the pan goes on. A dull thud is good. Hissing means find the leak (piston seal, sealing ring, bushing, case passage).
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: Presoaking is tied to clearance measurement: frictions absorb fluid, so the clutch-pack clearance (covered in earlier work) should be set with soaked plates. This is supported by the Sun Transmissions excerpt only.

### Gaps
- OPEN: No Halderman or Today's Technician excerpt could be reached for pump clearance categories, planetary pinion rules, or drum inspection. Verify against the course textbook when it is identified.
- OPEN: C.5 (routing) and C.14 (case bores, vents) have no dedicated public source.
- OPEN: No GM TechLink item on converter seating, flexplate or cooler flush was surfaced by search.

## Q2. What classic mistakes and traps appear in textbook and technician material?

### Takeaway
The traps that recur across sources:
- drawing the transmission to the engine with the bell-housing bolts, or with a converter that is not fully seated;
- missing dowels or a missing converter-pilot sleeve;
- impact-gunning the flexplate bolts;
- skipping the cooler flush or the flow check;
- installing a one-way clutch (sprag) backward;
- using white grease instead of petroleum jelly or assembly lube;
- reusing heat-damaged steels or skipping the friction soak;
- installing lip seals backward;
- trusting a flusher instead of a warm-engine flow test.

### Cited Findings
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: **Using the bell-housing bolts to pull the transmission to the engine can push the converter through the pump.** In one documented case the converter was jammed so badly that removal took hours. Any resistance while hand-tightening the bolts means the converter isn't seated: separate and start over. — [vintage-mustang forum](https://www.vintage-mustang.com/threads/can-transmission-be-separated-from-engine.1015898/) / [thirdgen forum](https://www.thirdgen.org/forums/transmissions-drivetrain/545371-incorrect-torque-converter-install.html) (forum level, several threads). The principle agrees with the installer PDFs above that call for free play. No textbook excerpt found (OPEN for a textbook citation).
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Only one or two "clunks" means the converter is not seated. Running it that way can destroy the pump. — [Ford-Trucks forum](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/1573311-t-c-not-seated-properly-during-trans-install-4r70w.html); [freeasestudyguides](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/torque-converter-inspection.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Missing dowels or a missing pilot centering ring lead to pump bushing, converter hub, flexplate and seal failures. — [Sonnax](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/99-avoiding-after-overhaul-problems); [Certified Transmission](https://www.certifiedtransmission.com/transletter/april-2020/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Impact-gunning the crank-to-flexplate bolts stretches them and can distort the crank flange, causing a rear main leak. — [Sonnax](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/99-avoiding-after-overhaul-problems)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Skipping the cooler flush is a classic cause of failure in replacement units and can void the warranty. Judging flow from the flusher, instead of a warm-engine flow test, is a trap. — [ACE Reman](https://acetransmissionreman.com/how-transmission-warranty-claims-work/); [Sonnax](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/99-avoiding-after-overhaul-problems)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A backward sprag or roller clutch gives a wrong-direction hold: a tie-up (62TE overrun sprag, when the direct clutch applies) or no drive in D (4L60-E forward sprag, forum level). The ID groove is useless if the sprag was rebuilt wrong, so bench-check the rotation. — [Transmission Digest, Only One Way](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/only-one-way-the-right-way/); [LS1Tech 4L60E thread](https://ls1tech.com/forums/automatic-transmission/1978622-4l60e-rebuild-no-drive-but-have-reverse-manual-1st-2nd.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL (the "Tech A/Tech B" misconception): "A one-way roller clutch will work fine if assembled in either direction" is **false**. — [freeasestudyguides result](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/a2-automatic-transmission-test-9.html) (excerpt; principle only)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: White chassis or bearing grease used to hold thrust washers or bearings doesn't mix with ATF. Use petroleum jelly or assembly lube. — [freeasestudyguides, Automatic Transmission Bearings](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/automatic-transmission-bearings.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A clogged piston check ball causes a partial apply from centrifugal pressure, which burns the clutch. A leaking check ball causes slip. — [Sonnax, 4L60-E 3-4 burnup](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/659-all-over-the-map-attacking-4l60-e-burnt-3-4-clutches-with-confidence)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A worn stator-support or pump bushing gives a misleading symptom. It shows up as TCC slip codes or a burnt 3-4 clutch (cross-feed), not as a bushing noise. — [Sonnax, Bushwhacked](https://www.sonnax.com/tech_resources/809-bushwhacked-keys-to-avoiding-preventable-bushing-problems)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A lip seal installed backward leaks under apply pressure. — [freeasestudyguides, Automatic Transmission Seals](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/automatic-transmission-seals.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Solid Teflon rings installed without the sizing tool, or unsized, won't seat, and scarf-cut rings behave differently from butt-cut rings. — [Transmission Digest, Sealing-Ring Technology](https://www.transmissiondigest.com/sealing-ring-technology-small-parts-big-problems/)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: A differential preload "measured" with the geartrain still in the case is not a true turning torque. — [freeasestudyguides, Transaxle Side Bearings](https://www.freeasestudyguides.com/transaxle-side-bearings.html)
- DOCUMENTED (snippet) / UNIVERSAL: Flexplate cracks around the crank bolt holes are often hard to see in the car, and the knock is easily mistaken for engine knock. — [EricTheCarGuy](https://www.ericthecarguy.com/loose-torque-converter-bolts-and-cracked-flex-plates/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL (exam framing): Area C questions are likely to test **cause and effect** ("which is the most likely cause of pump damage after installation?") and **which step comes first or last** (air-test before and after teardown; flush before install; seat the converter before mating; check free play before tightening the bolts), not numbers. Specs belong to SI.
- INFERRED / UNIVERSAL: One-way clutch rules to memorize: **orientation matters, bench-check before install, and a backward unit gives either no drive in the range it normally carries or a tie-up with another member.** Which symptom you get depends on where the OWC sits in the power flow. That links to the planetary power-flow material already in `note/notes.md`.
- INFERRED: Several traps come in matched pairs (dry frictions and clearance; unsized Teflon rings and air-test leaks; wrong grease and fluid contamination). The air test at reassembly is the catch-all check for sealing mistakes before the pan goes on.

### Gaps
- OPEN: The "never draw it up with the bell-housing bolts" rule is documented only at forum or installer level in this session. No textbook or OEM excerpt was found.
- OPEN: The 4L60-E backward forward-sprag symptom set is forum-sourced. Confirm it from GM SI, ATSG, or a GM-training power-flow chart (the learner's GM training material may already show which clutches and OWCs carry each range) before using it as a named example in a guide.
- OPEN: Torrington bearing or selective washer orientation errors: no source described their symptoms (likely end-play change or bearing failure, but that is not documented here).
- OPEN: Friction soak duration: the only manufacturer figure found is Alto's "minimum of 15 minutes" (snippet; fetch blocked). Raybestos says to presoak but no duration appeared in the excerpt. BorgWarner was not found. Don't state a single universal figure.
- OPEN: No public source gave a universal crank end-play limit or converter free-play spec, and there should not be one. All numbers are unit-specific (SI).
