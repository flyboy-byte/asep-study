driveslines class discussion
somewhat need this, especially for ase, although when i say we need this im not saying its more important than other topics by any means.
outer cv is almost always fixed and not plunge type
inside cv joint is a plunge joint.

all driveshafts are balanced.

ujoints allow the wheels and the rear axle to move up and down, remain flexibel and still trasfer torque
ujoints similar in design to flex type sockets for example.
most ujoints are called crossyoke joints or cardan joints.

if wer talking about a cardan joint wer talking about a typical ujoint classic.
ujoints:> most important thing, the output speed of the ujoint changes because of the angle of the joint. the speed changes twice per rev. the greater the angle, the greater the change in speed (velocity)

1st order vibration: vibration is the same speed as the source.
2nd order vibration: is a multiple of the rotation speed.
a ujoint axle vibration, bc of the varying speed bc of the design, is a inherent 2nd order vibration.
to solve ujoint axle vibration, we need to phase the ujoints 180 degrees apart. this gives a opposite angle the ujoint pivot is at, so they would cancel each other out.
not only do we need the ujoints to be phased apart, but we also need to have them at the same angle.
universal joints used in a typical driveshaft should have a working angleof 1/2 to 3 degrees. the working angle is the angle between the driving and driven end of the joint.
if the ujoints are not angles, the bearings in the ujoints will not turn and could damage them bc of the constant pressure.
so we need them in phase, at least 3 degrees apart, but no more than 3 or they will not cancel out. they must match degrees within .5 degrees apart.
remember these basic terms when dealing with ujoint axle vibration, and questions etc.

cv joints are different, they do not have the vibration problem the cardan has. constant velocity. it is fixed does not plunge.
but we can have cv joints that do plunge. idk.
tripod fixed joint is a plunging joint.

at the wheel on a drive axle its fixed, and at the transfer case or transmission etc its a plunging joint.
