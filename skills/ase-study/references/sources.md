# Source strategy

Reference routes checked: 2026-09-16. Recheck the exam's current documents when actual preparation begins. A linked resource is not a claim that its full contents were reviewed.

## Match the source to the question

| Need | Preferred evidence | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| What the instructor will assess | Notes, syllabus/review, supplied textbook pages and lab work | Governs class-final scope; record the instructor's emphasis. |
| What ASE can assess | Official study guide, task list and test specifications | Governs professional exam coverage and published weighting. |
| How a mechanism works | Manufacturer teaching documents, diagrams, public educational resources | Develop an explanation; identify the design shown. |
| Exact procedure or specification | Applicable OEM/component-maker service information | Match vehicle/assembly, conditions, units and revision. |
| An unfamiliar symptom | Verified service information and diagnostic reasoning | Develop competing causes and the next confirming check. |
| Exam-style practice | Official public examples/practice, inspected commercial prep, or manufacturer training the learner can access | Keep the bank separate from the guide; do not reproduce protected questions. Add original exercises only for a requested format or uncovered gap. |

Broad web research and individual-topic investigation complement each other. A primer can explain an entire layout; a service example can supply the measurement that makes the explanation testable. Search by the question to resolve, not by a desire to accumulate citations.

## Personal notes, public notes, or both

When web research is requested, personal notes are helpful but not a prerequisite. Start with the requested subject and output, establish the official exam coverage if relevant, and use a structured search:

1. Search broad teaching resources: publicly shared student notes, college lecture notes/slides, instructor handouts, open textbooks, lesson plans, and shop demonstrations. For example, combine the topic with `lecture notes`, `course pdf`, `lab worksheet`, or `training manual`; use school/manufacturer domains where useful, then broaden when needed.
2. Inspect the actual content. Record author or institution when identifiable, audience, date/edition, application and access limits. A search snippet or video title only establishes a lead. Do not claim exhaustive coverage of the web.
3. Build a compact source map in the course research file: resource → topics → useful explanation/visual → reliability or unresolved concern. Several sites copying the same notes are one evidentiary lineage, not independent confirmation.
4. Compare notes with the current official task list and appropriate technical primary sources. Separate a helpful teaching analogy from an accurate account of the mechanism. Confirm specifications and consequential diagnostic claims with applicable manufacturer/service information.
5. Write an original synthesis with source links, resolved differences and remaining gaps. Use publicly accessible or user-authorized material; acknowledge restricted sources without bypassing access or reproducing whole books. A forum or student's note can supply a question or reported experience without becoming universal service authority.

The resulting guide may be web-first or hybrid. For class-final prep, leave the instructor's actual scope unresolved until notes, a syllabus or a review sheet establishes it. For ASE prep, use the official coverage as the organizing reference. Expand the source search until the important gaps are addressed or the remaining access/evidence limits are clear, rather than accumulating a fixed number of links.

An external deep-research tool can take the same brief when actually available: subject/exam, requested output, known sources, learner context, open questions, application boundaries, and required citations. Save and verify its output like any other research input. If that tool is unavailable, use available search/browsing tools and state what was done.

## General starting points

| Source | Useful route | Access/evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [ASE A-series](https://ase.com/tests/a1-a9/) and [Automobile guide](https://ase.com/wp-content/uploads/ASE_Automobile_Study_Guide.pdf) | Current categories, published weights, tasks and public examples | Read the relevant exam section; professional and entry-level programs differ. |
| [ASE practice program](https://ase.com/official-ase-practice-test-program/) | Public description of official practice options | Check current terms; paid questions are not accessed just by reading the description. |
| [ASE Education Foundation](https://www.aseeducationfoundation.org/schools/entry-level/) | Entry-level resources | Do not substitute its blueprint or threshold for a professional certification. |
| [GM TechLink](https://gm-techlink.com/) | GM service explanations and named examples | Historical articles are application-limited; verify current procedures separately. |
| [TREMEC support](https://tremec.com/aftermarket/resources/product-support-faq/) | Component-maker explanations and manual-transmission examples | A source for its products, not a universal transmission specification. |
| [KHK gear primer](https://khkgears.us/media/fqrlut1m/abcs-of-gearing-b.pdf) | General illustrated gear mechanisms | General teaching; not an ASE coverage authority. |
| [Weber State / John Kelly](https://www.weber.edu/automotive/j_kelly.html) | Official route to automotive teaching videos | Inspect the actual lesson before citing a technical claim. |
| [Halderman resources](https://jameshalderman.com/) | Publisher/author teaching material | Some content needs access; do not claim an animation or chapter was reviewed without seeing it. |
| Owned textbooks and Motor Age or other prep books | A learner's supplied edition and relevant passages | Record edition/page and actual access; do not infer ownership of every subject volume. |

Use the manufacturer most relevant to the actual topic; this list is a starting map from a drivetrain project. It is not a ranking or a complete engine-performance bibliography. For L1, use the dedicated guide and composite reference described in `pathways.md`.

Owned question banks can inform what the learner practices without becoming repository content. Record the title/edition and only the explanation or weakness needed for study. Do not copy whole questions, answer sets, or publisher explanations into a public or local synthesis unless the user supplied the material and the requested use permits it.

## Record evidence without laundering it

**DOCUMENTED** means an inspected source supports the statement within its application. **INFERRED** identifies the reasoning added to that evidence. **OPEN** identifies what still needs verification. A statement about the learner's situation may be recorded as learner-reported without turning it into a general policy.

Record source title, original URL or local file, edition/date, page/section, access date, relevant application, and what was actually inspected. Preserve an original local PDF when useful and permitted; keep downloaded material out of the public framework. Link upstream sources rather than republishing manuals or commercial content.

Check diagrams visually when their layout carries the meaning. A PDF text extraction can scramble a chart. Never infer a complete procedure from a single contact-pattern photograph, a sensor name, or a keyed sample answer.

When sources conflict, first check their designs, dates, conditions, and terminology. A different assembly does not prove a class note is wrong. Put the qualification alongside the claim and retain unresolved differences until matched evidence settles them.
