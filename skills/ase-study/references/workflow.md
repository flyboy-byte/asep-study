# From course material to useful preparation

Read the local context before starting. The learner's current request selects the mode; a future-course discussion does not activate a study program.

## Take in material without making note-taking harder

Read relevant new and changed notes, handouts, guides, diagrams, and supplied book passages. A learner may use plain text, photos, PDFs, or Markdown and may drop files in the root. Preserve that working arrangement.

Capture what is available: unit/topic, instructor emphasis, named assembly, shop observation, measurements and conditions, and stated confusion. Unknown details stay unknown. If a file is unreadable or a source is inaccessible, identify that limitation and continue with material that can be inspected.

## Build the unit explanation

For a web-first request, first build a source map using `sources.md`: locate public notes and broader lessons, inspect their contents, identify duplication and conflicts, and verify the claims needed for the requested guide. This can produce an ASE-oriented explanation without personal notes. Keep instructor-specific coverage unknown until supplied, and do not label another course's material as the learner's class content.

When requested or appropriate to the established study workflow, update the unit's existing guide and cram sheet. Explain what the parts do, the sequence of operation, and how the shop example demonstrates it. Keep external qualifications visibly separate from what the instructor taught. Do not silently rewrite raw notes, even when an outside source suggests a correction.

For a useful explanation, connect:

```text
Operating condition → expected mechanism → observation
                    → possible causes → distinguishing check
                    → interpretation of the result
```

A list of causes is a starting point. Develop the evidence that would distinguish them. For measurements, state the reference points, conditions, units, specification source, and what each possible result would mean.

## Map coverage and research gaps

Compare the relevant course material with the current official exam tasks. Use a compact table in the research file:

| Topic/task anchor | Class/source evidence | Learner evidence | Open question or next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| A specific task, with guide edition/page | Actual file and location, or pending class | Observed explanation/result, or unassessed | The missing mechanism, measurement, or comparison |

Keep material coverage and demonstrated understanding separate. If the course has not reached a topic, record it as pending. Use weights to account for exam breadth, then combine them with actual uncertainty to select worthwhile research.

Research can mean finding a broad teaching guide or investigating one specific claim. Prefer the smallest addition that resolves the question. Record the explanation, source/date/location, application, evidence strength, teaching use, and remaining boundary in the existing research file. Use the source strategy that accompanies this skill.

## Study and review

Use short explanations, retrieval, worked measurements, and new diagnostic scenarios as appropriate. Ask the learner to explain why a competing answer is less suitable under the stated conditions. Distinguish mechanism errors, missed conditions, diagram-reading errors, and question-reading mistakes.

Constructed practice needs a defensible answer and rationale grounded in the provided evidence. Label it original practice. Resolve ambiguous wording before scoring it. Never imply it is an actual ASE question or that a score guarantees an exam result.

Record consequential misses in the current coverage row rather than creating a new document for every quiz. Use later unfamiliar examples to revisit a correction. Adjust the amount of fundamental review to the learner's experience; do not assume all experienced learners have identical strengths.

## Optional rhythm for a four-week course

This is a process pattern, not a topic syllabus or automated schedule. Use actual dates and instructor order when supplied.

| Phase | Alongside the course | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Opening week | Establish the context, source inventory, and first notes/guide loop. | A taught mechanism connected to a specific shop example. |
| Developing weeks | Add lessons, research uncertainties, and revisit earlier material. | Explanations that transfer to a new example; specific remaining questions. |
| Later weeks | Connect topics and review broader exam coverage. | Diagnostic reasoning across units rather than isolated definitions. |
| End of course / exam window | Produce the requested final and/or ASE package when its scope is known. | Accounted-for sources, checked examples, and explicit remaining gaps. |

The course final and ASE appointment may be on different dates. Do not assume the exam is the last Friday or that one course's date applies to another.

## Synthesize when requested

Inventory the entire then-current directory, including earlier research and corrected guides. Read the relevant contents, identify duplicates, compare conflicting statements, and retain source locations. State what could not be reviewed.

For a **class-final package**, let instructor coverage and emphasis drive selection. Include useful unit explanations, shop connections, appropriate practice, and a concise last review. Outside breadth should not overwhelm the actual course.

For an **ASE package**, account for the official task list and weights using the complete corpus. Develop coverage that the class did not provide, qualify manufacturer-specific rules, and emphasize reasoning where the learner needs it. Commercial prep material can complement this when supplied and inspected.

Before marking the requested output complete, check answer logic, calculations, source/application boundaries, diagram labels, and coverage claims. Keep unresolved contradictions explicit. Render and visually inspect print outputs when creating them. Do not prescribe a fixed number of questions or pages when it would work against the actual study need.

## Collaborate and leave state

Either assistant can draft; another can inspect coverage, sources, or reasoning if that review is requested. Findings from another model are inputs to check, not automatically verified facts. Work on separate files or sequentially to avoid conflicting edits.

Update the context with the date, files actually processed, outputs changed, unresolved issue, and next useful action. Keep old resolved research findings visible with a dated outcome when that history prevents rework. State what remains unreviewed or unfinished without treating documentation alone as progress through a learning milestone.
